## 1. What each evidentiary base can currently carry

**The pardon rolls. [Mixed tiers]** The open, first-hand anchor is
older than Harvey and fully readable: **Kriehn, *The English Rising in
1450* (1892; public domain, read this pass)** — the pardon lists give
"about 2203 names of the men of Kent and Sussex alone," and Kriehn's
own verdict: "Cade's rebels were not drawn from the lower classes
alone, but from the middle classes, the gentry and even from the
nobility" (p. 5). The gentry skeleton circulating in the memo ("1
knight, 2 MPs, 18 squires") is openly carried with attribution to
**Griffiths, *Reign of King Henry VI* (1981), pp. 619–620** (T2
attribution via T4 carrier); the "70+/74 gentlemen" element is **NOT
FOUND openly — [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Griffiths pp. 619–20;
Harvey]**. The occupational *percentages* the memo carried at flagged

carrier in this pass either: they remain **Harvey-derived,
tertiary-circulating, unpinned — [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Harvey 1991,
ch. 4 and appendix]**. Until Harvey is in the repo the draft may use

**The indictment rolls. [Gated]** The King's Bench indictments for Kent
1450–52 are edited in **Virgoe, "Some Ancient Indictments in the King's

**The chronicles. [T1-adjacent, already in the memo]** The rebels'

commons" speaking through the idiom of good governance; Gregory's

## 2. The muster machinery — Bohna's thesis, at its verified level

**[T2 abstract-level; article gated]** Bohna, "Armed Force and Civic
Legitimacy in Jack Cade's Revolt of 1450," *EHR* 118 (2003), pp.
563–582: the open abstract carries the thesis — the rebels mobilised
through "the traditional structure of the **posse comitatus or militia
under the command of locally-elected constables**." That is: the rising
assembled itself through the county's own hundred-and-constable muster
apparatus — the same administrative machinery the crown used to raise
Kent, operated from below. This is the probe's sharpest structural
point for ch07's propertied-anxious argument: the rising was not a
crowd but a *countyside mobilised by its office-holders* — which is
what the constables among the pardoned imply. **The phrase "the militia
of the county embodied," used at flagged level in the memo, was NOT
FOUND in any open text and must not be quoted until the article is
retrieved — [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Bohna, EHR 118, at OUP/JSTOR
3489286].** The hundred-level geography ("more than sixty parishes"
mobilised, in the memo at tertiary level) is likewise Harvey territory:
**[UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Harvey]**.

## 3. The Normandy-veterans question — a chronology correction

**[The probe's substantive finding; flag for ch07 memo Revisions at
next review.]** The memo (ch07 §1, reviewed) carries "returning
Normandy soldiers swelled the ranks" — open sources support the
presence of returning soldiers and sailors in the rising (T4-carried,
uncontroversial). But the *evacuation chronology*, now fixed at T1/T2
level by the ch06 evidence file, cuts the claim finer than the memo
states:

- The rising ran late May – 12 July 1450 (Sevenoaks 18 June; London
  Bridge 5–6/8–9 July [variant, memo §0.5]; Cade dead ~12 July).
- Caen's garrison embarked at Ouistreham only after **1 July 1450**;
  **Cherbourg — the last place — fell 12 August 1450**, a month after
  the rising collapsed; Falaise's and Domfront's garrisons left in
  late July–August.

**Therefore:** the veterans *in* the rising were the earlier returnees
— the truce-era discharged (Curry, in repo, pp. 186–87: the
establishment cut to ~2,500 with ~2,000 put out of employment from
1446–48, many "took to living off the land"), the 1449–50 refugee
flow from Rouen and upper Normandy through the Kentish ports, and the
Formigny survivors shipped home in the spring — **not the final
garrison evacuations**, which arrived after Cade was dead and fed the
*continued* Kentish unrest of late 1450–51 (the memo's aftermath
section) rather than the rising itself. The draft should state the
two-wave structure: discharged-and-refugee veterans in the rising;
evacuation veterans in its afterlife. Named individual veterans among
the rebels: **NOT FOUND openly — [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Harvey;
Grummitt on the Calais/garrison soldiery]**.

## 4. Assembled composition statement (what the draft may currently say)

**[CONSENSUS, at the tiers shown]** The rising was led and manned
disproportionately from the propertied and office-holding middle of
Kent — pardon evidence at ~2,200 names for Kent and Sussex (Kriehn,
open), including gentry to the level of knight and MP (Griffiths-
attributed skeleton, flagged), mobilised through the county's own
constable/muster machinery (Bohna, abstract level), with truce-
discharged soldiers and Normandy refugees among them (chronology as
§3); its demands were restorationist (the *Complaint*, T1, in the
reviewed memo). **[UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL]** remain: the occupational
percentages (Harvey), the indictment occupations (Virgoe), the
sixty-parish geography (Harvey), the "militia embodied" phrasing
(Bohna), and any named veteran. The probe adds no new claim beyond the
tiers stated; its one correction (the two-wave veterans chronology) is
flagged for the memo's next Revisions entry.
