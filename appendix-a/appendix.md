## I. The founding hypothesis

**Hedley Bull, *The Anarchical Society* (1977), ch. 10 and conclusion.**
*[T2 — GATED; quoted via named intermediaries]* The founding "new
mediaevalism" hypothesis: "a system of overlapping authority and
multiple loyalty" as a possible secular successor to the states system;
the five prima facie trends; and Bull's own refusal of the conclusion on
his decade's evidence — the caution this book takes as its honest hinge
(quoted via Baaz and Kelemen, one word varying by printing, flagged).
Engaged: Introduction (the debt and the precise disagreement, developed
in the Coda), Ch1 (the caution inverted). *Still on the acquisition
list; no formulation is cited as consulted.*

## II. Theoretical reconstruction

**Jörg Friedrichs, "The Meaning of New Medievalism," *EJIR* 7:4
(2001).** *[T2 — IN REPO, read first-hand]* The analytical engine: the
duality of competing universalistic claims (Empire/Church →
state-system/transnational market), definition at p. 475, redefinition
p. 482, restated p. 490; the disanalogy at p. 497. The book's Part I
correction — the second universalism institutionalised as churches, not
a market — is made against this text from its own pages. Engaged: Ch1
(central), Ch2 (the foil), Ch11–12.

**Philip G. Cerny, "Neomedievalism, Civil War and the New Security
Dilemma," *Civil Wars* 1:1 (1998).** *[T2 — IN REPO, read first-hand]*
The six-characteristic checklist (section headings, pp. 49–57; *zones
grises* after Minc, n. 16); "durable disorder"; the absent exogenous
pressure named as "the inter-dynastic struggles of the Hundred Years'
War" (p. 52); the prediction of "quasi-public, quasi-private dispute
settlement regimes" (p. 52); and the location of the first
consolidation in "the creative destruction of fifteenth to seventeenth
century warfare" (pp. 51–52, 58–59) — the sceptics-concede thread's
theoretical instance. Engaged: Ch1 (the checklist run), Ch8 (the sixth
characteristic), Ch10 (the mechanism attested), Ch12, Appendix C.

**Philip G. Cerny, "Terrorism and the New Security Dilemma," *NWCR*
58:1 (2005).** *[T2 — bundled]* The war-college-tested five-fold
restatement; working text pending the 1998 original's retrieval, now
superseded for enumeration by the original (in repo). Engaged: Ch1,
Ch3, Ch5, Ch8.

**Stephen J. Kobrin, "Back to the Future: Neomedievalism and the
Postmodern Digital World Economy," *JIA* 51:2 (1998).** *[T2 —
bundled]* The platforms line's origin: digital economies dissolve the
territorial basis of jurisdiction; overlapping, heteronomous authority
returns; and Kobrin's own anticipated *re-centralisation*, which the
book redirects from the supranational to the intra-bloc terminus.
Engaged: Ch2 (central), Ch4, Ch11–12.

## III. Security and private force

**Sean McFate, *Durable Disorder* (LSE thesis, 2011; *The Modern
Mercenary*, 2014).** *[T2 — thesis bundled; monograph on acquisition
list]* The eroded mercenary taboo as Bull's third trend returned; the
monopsony disanalogy (the US as consumer-in-chief; modern PMSCs without
independent fiscal bases) that Ch3 documents from SEC filings and Ch4
turns on. Engaged: Ch3 (central), Ch4, Ch11–12.

**Phil Williams, *From the New Middle Ages to a New Dark Age* (SSI,
2008).** *[T3 — bundled]* The pessimist counter-read: durable disorder
as a way-station that can tip; "alternatively governed" zones. Staged
against McFate by the book (chronology declared). Engaged: Ch3, Ch5,
Ch11–12.

## IV. European and regional application

**Ole Wæver, "European Security Identities," *JCMS* 34:1 (1996).**
*[T2 — bundled]* The securitisation lens and Wæver's own "new middle
ages" of concentric circles — the EU as post-sovereign system in a
security theorist's terms. Engaged: Ch11 (the EU church), Ch12.

**Jan Zielonka, *Europe as Empire* (2006).** *[T2 — GATED, acquisition
list]* The EU as neomedieval empire; needed for Ch2's EU-church section
at the upgrade pass.

## V. Popular and strategic synthesis

**Anne-Marie Slaughter, *A New World Order* (2004), chs. 5–6.** *[T2 —
bundled]* The optimist's read, engaged as important and wrong: networks
as the interregnum's connective tissue mistaken for architecture; the
capacity test applied in Ch1 §V. Engaged: Ch1, Ch2, Ch12.

**Robert D. Kaplan, "The Coming Anarchy" (1994).** *[T3 — bundled]* The
atmospheric predecessor and the declinism-cycle's finest instance;
quoted exactly, with the stretch-limo image credited to Homer-Dixon.
Engaged: Introduction (the first inoculation), Ch1.

**Parag Khanna, "The Next Big Thing: Neomedievalism" (*FP*, 2009).**
*[T3 — defective capture; acquisition list]* The mass-audience marker;
[ANALOGY-ONLY] per doctrine; annotation provisional pending a clean
copy.

## VI. The critics

**Bruce Holsinger, *Neomedievalism, Neoconservatism, and the War on
Terror* (2007).** *[T2 — punctum-carried page cites; re-verify on
purchase]* The medievalist's indictment: neomedievalism as "the global
idiom of the non-state actor" and a co-optable theoretical fashion.
Engaged: Introduction (the fairyland inoculation).

**Nicholas Rengger, the "fairyland" chapter.** *[T2 — GATED; only "its
calamitous predecessor" carried openly]* Not paraphrased beyond the
carried fragment until retrieved. Engaged: Introduction.

## VII. The historiographical anchor the literature lacked

**J. R. Green, *A Short History of the English People* (1874), ch. VI.**
*[T1 — IN REPO, public domain]* The "New Monarchy" coinage at source
("If we use the name of the New Monarchy to express the character of
the English sovereignty from the time of Edward the Fourth to the time
of Elizabeth…") — the contested frame Ch11 declares and extends.
