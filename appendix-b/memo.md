1. **The Venetian row (Lane; Lane & Mueller — pins verified against
   sidecars).** Budget of c. 1500 (Lane p. 237): receipts
   1,150,000 ducats, of which mainland cities 330,000 and direct
   taxes 160,000; ordinary expenses 530,000, of which interest on
   bonds 155,000; "Available for extraordinary expenses (wars)
   620,000." Lane & Mueller's 1469 breakdown (p. 187): revenue c.
   1 million ducats, "only a little more than half" from empire

   Fair contrast (p. 186) as the monarchy/republic fiscal
   distinction. Fiscal-arc cells: Monte Vecchio 4% at 66 (1423) →
   1% at 13 (1474); direct levies voted under the 1453 shock;
   cadaster 1463; Monte Nuovo 1482 (Lane p. 238). Disanalogies
   carried from ch10 memo Revisions 27: fisc driven by war not
   cannon; force limited by men not capital.

2. **The papal fisc row (Morris — pins verified).** Liber Censuum
   (1192): 1,214 gold ounces, "less than 5 per cent of Richard
   I's annual income from England"; Peter's Pence "just under
   £200" (p. 215); Hadrian IV's "no one can make war without pay"
   (p. 214); the Papal State "created on the battlefields of
   Sicily and Germany" (pp. 421–2); Honorius III's 1225 curial
   prebend scheme rejected at Bourges and London (p. 558). The
   universalism that could not pay for force.
