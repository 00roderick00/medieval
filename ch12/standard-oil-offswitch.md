## 1. What the Court actually ordered, 15 May 1911

**The standard [T1, verbatim].** The rule of reason: the Sherman Act
"prohibits all contracts and combination which amount to an
unreasonable or undue restraint of trade in interstate commerce"
(syllabus, 221 U.S. 1); the statute left the wrong "to be determined
by the light of reason, guided by the principles of law" (~221 U.S.
63–64).

**The decree affirmed [T1, the Court's recitation, ~221 U.S. 77–78].**
The circuit court "commanded the dissolution of the combination, and
therefore in effect, directed the transfer by the New Jersey
corporation back to the stockholders of the various subsidiary
corporations entitled to the same of the stock which had been turned
over to the New Jersey company in exchange for its stock." Decree § 5
"forbade the New Jersey corporation from in any form or manner
exercising any ownership or exerting any power directly or indirectly
in virtue of its apparent title to the stocks of the subsidiary
corporations"; § 6 enjoined the post-transfer owners "from in any way
conspiring or combining to violate the act… and prohibited all
agreements between the subsidiary corporations or other stockholders
in the future, tending to produce or bring about further violations
of the act." Thirty days was extended to "at least six months" (~221
U.S. 81); the court below retained jurisdiction "to compel compliance
in every respect."

**The structure severed [T1].** Decree § 4 (verbatim in the U.S.
Reports footnote, ~pp. 42–44) tabulates the holding company's ~37
subsidiaries — the state Standard Oils, Buckeye Pipe Line, National
Transit, Union Tank Line, Anglo-American, Waters-Pierce and the rest
— acquired for "more than $90,000,000" of SONJ stock; § 2 names "the
seven individual defendants" (John D. and William Rockefeller,
Rogers, Flagler, Archbold, Payne, Pratt) who "united with the
Standard Oil Company… to form and effectuate this combination."

## 2. What the decree did NOT do — the probe's first structural finding

- **No divestiture by the individuals.** Nothing ordered any
  shareholder to sell anything. The shares of the 37 successors went
  back "to the stockholders… entitled to the same" — i.e. pro rata
  to the holding company's owners, Rockefeller first among them.
  (The pro-rata mechanics appear in the opinion via the appellants'
  recitation, ~221 U.S. 10; the circuit court decree's own §§ 5–9
  verbatim text is in 173 F. 177 (C.C.E.D. Mo. 1909), NOT recited in
  full in the opinion — **[UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: 173 F. 177, if a
  verbatim decree paragraph is ever load-bearing]**.) The Court
  construed § 6 "not as depriving the stockholders or the
  corporations… of the power to make normal and lawful contracts or
  agreements, but as restraining them from, by any device whatever,
  recreating directly or indirectly the illegal combination."
- **No operational severance of the physical system.** The Court's
  own illustration, verbatim: "take the pipe lines. By the effect of
  the transfer of the stock the pipe lines would come under the
  control of various corporations instead of being subjected to a
  uniform control. If various corporations owning the lines
  determined in the public interests to so combine as to make a
  continuous line, such agreement or combination would not be
  repugnant to the act…" — and the same for Union Tank Line's
  tank-car pooling. The decree severed the CONTROL STRUCTURE, not
  the infrastructure; coordinated carriage was expressly
  contemplated as lawful.
- **The blanket injunction was struck.** Decree § 7's bar on the
  defendants engaging in interstate commerce pending dissolution
  "should not have been awarded."

**Reading [BOOK'S ARGUMENT, resting on the verbatim record].** The
1911 instrument is precisely a control-rights operation: it
extinguished a private power of unified command over the energy
system (the holding company's ownership powers, § 5) and forbade its
reconstitution (§ 6), while leaving wealth (the shares), assets (the
pipes), and even coordination-in-carriage intact. Spine §8(c)'s
sentence — "the state did not take the oil; it destroyed the
vertical integration that made oil sovereign" — is supported at the
level of control, with one precision: what the decree destroyed was
unified DISCRETION over the whole system, and it transferred that
discretion to no one.

## 3. Where the off-switch went — the migration, dated from open primaries

The answer to spine §8(c)'s question ("after the breakup, who owned
the power to turn off the energy?") is: in 1911, NOBODY — the decree
vacated the private off-switch without creating a public one. The
public one was then built over three decades, by instruments mostly
independent of the antitrust line, one of which PRECEDES the breakup:

| Date | Instrument [all T1 unless noted] | The control power, verbatim or near-verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 29 Jun 1906 | **Hepburn Act**, 34 Stat. 584 | pipelines "shall be considered and held to be common carriers" under the ICC — non-discriminatory carriage imposed on the trust's chokepoint FIVE YEARS BEFORE the breakup |
| 15 May 1911 | **221 U.S. 1** | dissolution; § 5 no exercise of ownership; § 6 no recombination; six months; jurisdiction retained |
| 1917 | **Texas Pipeline Petroleum Law** (SB 68, 35th Leg.) | pipelines declared common carriers under the Railroad Commission [carrier: the RRC's own .gov history page; the codified text (Tex. Nat. Res. Code ch. 111) NOT retrievable open this session — flag] |
| 6 Oct 1917 | **TWEA § 5(b)**, 40 Stat. 415 | the President "may investigate, regulate, or prohibit, under such rules and regulations as he may prescribe, **by means of licenses or otherwise**, any transactions in foreign exchange… transfers of credit… between the United States and any foreign country" |
| 14 Aug 1930 → 1 May 1931 → 17 Aug 1931 | **RRC statewide proration order** (750,000 bbl/day "based on the reasonable market demand formula") → first East Texas order → martial law in the East Texas field [NM OCD chronology, .gov exhibit] | the state commission sets "the rate at which every oil well in Texas might produce" (RRC history, .gov) |
| 12 Nov 1932 | **Texas Market Demand Act** (42nd Leg., 4th C.S., ch. 2) | proration to market demand — session confirmed via the Legislative Reference Library snapshot; **the session-law text itself NOT retrieved — [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL]** |
| 7 Jan 1935 | **Panama Refining v. Ryan**, 293 U.S. 388 | NIRA § 9(c) (presidential prohibition of "hot oil" shipments) struck as undelegated — the interdiction power had to be legislated properly |
| 22 Feb 1935 | **Connally Hot Oil Act**, 49 Stat. 30; 15 U.S.C. 715b | "The shipment or transportation in interstate commerce from any State of contraband oil produced in such State is prohibited" — federal enforcement of the states' allowables |
| 27 Aug 1935 | **Interstate Oil Compact**, 49 Stat. 939 | congressional consent to the Dallas compact (TX, OK, CA, NM) "to conserve oil and gas by the prevention of physical waste" |
| 26 Jul 1941 | **EO 8832** (6 FR 3715), under TWEA § 5(b), via EO 8389 | freezing order extended to Japan — "all financial and import and export trade transactions in which Japanese interests are involved under the control of the Government" (White House release, FRUS doc. 184); the oil cutoff then ran through licensing denials under this control [the licensing practice itself is not documented in the two FRUS pages — flag] |
| 22 Dec 1975 | **EPCA § 161**, 89 Stat. 888–89 (42 U.S.C. 6241) | the Strategic Petroleum Reserve; "no drawdown… unless the President has found that implementation… is required by a severe energy supply interruption or by obligations of the United States under the international energy program" |
| 28 Dec 1977 | **IEEPA**, 50 U.S.C. 1701–02 | upon declared emergency the President may "block… nullify, void, prevent or prohibit… exercising any right, power, or privilege with respect to… any property in which any foreign country or a national thereof has any interest" — administered by OFAC |

**Reading [BOOK'S ARGUMENT].** The off-switch migrated in three
distinct forms, none of them created by the breakup itself: (i)
CARRIAGE control (Hepburn 1906; Texas 1917) — the chokepoint
neutralised by common-carrier status, begun before dissolution; (ii)
PRODUCTION control (proration 1930–35, federally enforced by
Connally) — a state commission acquiring exactly the discretion the
trust had lost: how much flows; (iii) TRANSACTIONAL control (TWEA →
EO 8832 → IEEPA/OFAC; EPCA's reserve) — the presidential
license-and-block apparatus. By 1941 the state could do to Japan what
Rockefeller could once do to a refinery district — and no successor
company could do it to anyone.

  TimesMachine gated).
- **Power:** what is verifiable at T1 today is the control side:

  rebuilding it — while keeping every share. Wealth retained and

  decoupled by legal instrument") is supported on the power side at

## 6. Findings against spine §8(c) — recorded, not resolved

1. **Supported:** the breakup as control-rights story, the
   wealth/power decoupling (power side at T1; wealth side pending
   retrieval), and "the state did not take the oil."
2. **Refinement:** the off-switch did not pass to the state IN the
   breakup. The decree vacated private command and handed the
   discretion to no one; the public off-switch was assembled
   1906–1977 by carriage, production and transaction instruments —
   one of which (Hepburn) PRECEDES the dissolution. If ch12 states
   the proof case, it should state the lag: decoupling first,
   control-acquisition over the following generation. That sequence
   is, if anything, MORE useful to the book (it mirrors the
   1439→1445→1449–53 gradualism in ch10/fiscal-settlement.md) — but
   it is not the compressed version.
3. **Tension flagged for Roderick (draft is locked; nothing done):**
   ch12/draft.md §IV currently argues the platforms are "not the
   American state's Standard Oil but its monasteries," rejecting the
   antitrust register as nineteenth-century nostalgia, while spine
   §8(c) declares "Standard Oil is the proof case." The two are
   reconcilable — §8(c) reads 1911 as a control-rights consolidation
   rather than as trust-busting, and the draft's target is the
   NOSTALGIC reading — but the draft's sentence as written sits
   against the spine's wording, and TWEA's "by means of licenses"
   shows the licence working as a control instrument inside the very
   tradition the draft sets aside. Roderick's call at renovation.
