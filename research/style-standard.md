# Style Standard — The Artillery State
## Operational digest of the Cambridge History Faculty style guide, Section II

*Adopted 2026-08-04 on Roderick's instruction, from
`research/History_Faculty_style_guide_2020-21_0.pdf` (updated 2020–21),
under the governing reading: **Section I is coursework mechanics — ignored
except for typographic conventions of general application; Section II —
the CUP-derived footnote and bibliography style — is the operative
standard.** Where the guide is silent, the silence is noted; no rules are
invented. Items drawn from Section I under the general-application clause
are marked [§I].*

---

## 1. Note system and the two reference forms

- Full reference at first citation; **author-plus-short-title**
  thereafter (II.A). The short title contains "the key word or words
  from the main title, so as to make the reference easily recognisable."
- Notes are for reference, not digression; several references in one
  note are separated by semicolons; every note ends with a full stop
  [§I.D, general application to note-writing].
- **Ibid.** (no italics) may refer only to the immediately preceding
  reference, "so long as there is no danger of confusion" — never after
  a note containing more than one work (II.A.3).
- The author may be separated from the short title inside a discursive
  note sentence (II.A.3).

## 2. First-reference forms (II.A.1)

- **Book:** Author (name as in the work, or initials-plus-surname,
  consistently), *Title in italics* (Place: Publisher, Year), p./pp. —
  e.g. Rosamond McKitterick, *Carolingians and the written word*
  (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1989), p. 44. The 'p.' is
  optional but its use must be consistent; a convention of dropping
  p./pp. when a volume number is cited is permitted.
- **Edited volume:** Editor(s) (eds.), *Title* (Place: Publisher,
  Year), p.
- **Chapter in edited volume:** Author, 'Chapter title', in *Volume
  title*, ed. Editor (Place: Publisher, Year), complete page range.
- **Journal article:** Author, 'Article title', *Journal* Volume
  (Year), complete page range, at p. X for a specific page. Volume
  number is mandatory; give EITHER issue number OR month, never both;
  neither is required.
- **Printed primary source:** begin with the author's name; editor or
  translator after the title — e.g. Gregory I, *The Letters of Gregory
  the Great*, trans. John C. Martyn, 3 vols. (Toronto: Pontifical
  Institute of Mediaeval Studies, 2004); subsections as Book/Letter/
  chapter, then the edition, vol. and page. Calendars and rolls by
  title. (Section II's own note: medievalists and early modernists may
  omit the publisher where irrelevant or unknown — a permitted,
  documented alteration.)
- **Manuscript material:** item, collection, folio, repository, place —
  e.g. Richardson to Lady Bradshaigh, 15 December 1748, 'Richardson /
  Bradshaigh letters', Forster collection, XI, fol. 7, …
- **Thesis:** Author, 'Title', unpublished PhD thesis, University
  (Year), p.
- **Online sources:** cite in roman without angle brackets; include
  the access date ([§I.C.3.c], DNB-style form: …online edn, May 2006
  [URL, accessed 15 June 2007]); avoid citing websites where the
  source is readily available in print. Scans of printed matter are
  cited as the printed matter; unpaginated ebooks by chapter/paragraph
  or percentage.
- **Parliamentary papers, films/recordings:** the guide's forms
  (II.A.1) apply as needed; construct consistently on its principles.

## 3. Short-reference forms (II.A.2)

Books: Chakrabarty, *Provincializing Europe*, p. 74. Articles/chapters:
Salter, 'Pilgrimage to truth', pp. 34–5. Manuscripts: 'Richardson /
Bradshaigh letters', fol. 116.

## 4. Titles: capitalisation and italicisation (II.A.1 headnote)

Lower case in titles except proper nouns is the exemplified style;
**mandatory for French and Latin titles**; in English and German titles
either upper or lower case for non-trivial words is permitted —
consistently. Book and journal titles italic; article and chapter
titles in single inverted commas.

## 5. Quotations [§I.C.1, general application]

- **Single inverted commas**; double only for quotations within
  quotations.
- Punctuation follows the closing inverted comma — It was 'too close
  to call'. — EXCEPT grammatically complete sentences beginning with a
  capital, which keep terminal punctuation inside.
- Full sentences within brackets punctuate within; bracketed clauses
  inside a sentence punctuate outside.
- Quotations retain the punctuation conventions of their originals.
- **Block-quote threshold: the guide is SILENT** — no word- or
  line-count rule is given (its examples display longer quotations
  set off and indented; no threshold is stated). No rule invented;
  project practice to be fixed by Roderick at the conformance sweep.
- Foreign-language quotations: original with translation in the notes
  or vice versa; only the version consulted is cited if a translation
  was used [§I.C.5 — general application; the project's [TRANS.
  CLAUDE] marking is compatible pending Roderick's translations].

## 6. Numbers, dates, centuries [§I.C.2]

- Numbers **written out up to 100**, except where a discussion mixes
  numbers above and below (then all in figures), and except numbers
  with units (always figures, space before the unit: 4 cm).
- Dates: **20 December 1148**; 20 December; AD 245–50.
- **Centuries written out** (twenty-first century); decades without
  apostrophe (1920s).

## 7. Other typographic conventions of general application [§I.C]

- British punctuation and spelling throughout (quotations excepted).
- **Serial comma**: 'red, white, and blue'.
- Possessive 's after names in -s (Dickens's, Jones's); antiquity
  excepted (Socrates', Jesus').
- Abbreviations take a point (ch., vol., ed.); contractions do not
  (edn, Dr, St). The guide's standard abbreviation set (ed., edn, f./
  ff., fol., MS/MSS, qu, r., sig., trans., v., vol.) may be used
  unlisted; journal/series abbreviations (EHR, MGH SRG) require an
  abbreviations list or first-reference expansion.
- Widows and orphans avoided [§I.B.6 — typographic, general].

## 8. Bibliography (II.B, with [§I.E] structure)

- Sections, as applicable: (a) primary manuscript sources; (b) primary
  printed sources; (c) secondary sources [§I.E — structural convention
  of general application]. Alphabetical within sections by first
  author's surname (works without an author by title, same listing);
  full stop ends every entry [§I.E].
- Entry form = full reference with **surname inverted** (first author
  only in the guide's examples); no specific page references — only
  full page-ranges of articles/chapters; **hanging indent**.
- Manuscript sources: EITHER by item-and-collection name OR — the
  convention "widely used by medieval historians," which **this
  project follows as a medievalist work** — by place, library,
  shelfmark: Oxford, Bodleian Library, Rawlinson MS D.36…
- The bibliography contains everything cited plus other important
  works consulted.

## 9. Noted silences (no rule invented)

Block-quote threshold (above); em- and en-dash usage and spacing;
ellipsis style in quotations; capitalisation of titles of office
(king/King); treatment of n-dates ranges beyond the date examples;
URL line-breaking. These await Roderick's ruling at the conformance
sweep or default to CUP practice at press.
