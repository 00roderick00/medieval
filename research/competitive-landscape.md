# Competitive Landscape — The Trade Titles, Positioned

*Grouped as Roderick's triage list groups them. One line per title on
the book's differentiation. The scholarship from the same list
(Tilly, Brewer, Parrott, Andrade, Sharman, Bobbitt as evidence,
Parker/Rogers, Mangini & Petroff) is book work and lives in
retrieval-master.md; it appears here only where it bears on trade

---

## I. The dispersal camp (the neomedieval endpoint)

**Yanis Varoufakis, *Technofeudalism: What Killed Capitalism*
(2023).** The rival adjacent coinage: platform "cloudalists" as new
feudal lords, capitalism succeeded by something worse. The book
treats platform power as an institutional universalism inside a
repeatable arc rather than a successor mode of production — and takes
seriously the actor Varoufakis writes out (the Gane critique's point):
the state, which the book shows re-arming. Already flagged for a
ch01/ch02 note via the Gosztonyi & Gyetván engagement (in repo).

**Balaji Srinivasan, *The Network State* (2022).** The tech-right
exit thesis. The book prices exit — decisive force and a fisc — and
predicts the exit options are absorbed or licensed; the Próspera
episode already carries this in ch01 as documented fact rather than
polemic.

**James Dale Davidson & William Rees-Mogg, *The Sovereign
Individual* (1997).** The ur-text of the camp, revived among tech
elites. The book reads it as the dispersal phase's genuine prophecy —
right that legibility was in crisis, wrong about the pendulum: the
states rebuilt the legibility stack, and Part I documents the
rebuild.

**Aleksandra Spalińska, *Neomedievalism in International Relations:
Scattering the International* (Routledge, July 2026).** The academic
state of the art on the diagnosis side. The book concedes the
diagnosis and supplies what the diagnosis literature lacks: a
mechanism, an exit, and a falsifiable prediction — she legitimises
the term-space the book then argues *through*. (Also logged in the
crowding watch below by date.)

## II. The fragile-state camp

**Mustafa Suleyman with Michael Bhaskar, *The Coming Wave* (2023).**
The nation-state as "cracking foundation" that must nonetheless
contain AI. The book supplies the mechanism Suleyman's paradox
lacks: containment *is* consolidation — the wave capitalises the
state that contains it, which is the artillery lesson run forward.

## III. The re-consolidation camp (the book's nearest kin)

**Alexander Karp & Nicholas Zamiska, *The Technological Republic*
(2025).** Tech advantage matters only when converted into state
capacity — the book's conclusion as manifesto. The differentiation
is mode: Karp argues the state *should* take the stack back; the
book shows why it *must* (a priced mechanism with a documented first
run), what happens when it does not (ch05's control experiment), and
what would falsify the claim.

**Henry Farrell & Abraham Newman, *Underground Empire* (2023).** The
weaponised-interdependence account of one bloc's chokepoint reach.
Instrument-level reportage of the present; the book supplies the
five-century arc and the fiscal mechanism that predicts *where*
chokepoint power consolidates and why it stays consolidated.

**Edward Fishman, *Chokepoints* (2025).** The economic-warfare
narrative history, same family as Farrell & Newman. The book is
mechanism-level and comparative across four blocs where Chokepoints
is episodic and American.

**Chris Miller, *Chip War* (2022).** The upside-case comparable: a
supply-chain-of-decisive-capability story that broke out of the
policy shelf into trade. The book generalises the chokepoint into a
state-formation mechanism and adds the medieval control case Chip
War never claims. (On the retrieval ledger for citation purposes,
T3.)

## IV. History and scholarship, as trade positioning

**Patrick Wyman, *The Verge* (2021).** Proof the gunpowder and
state-formation story sells in trade at narrative register. The
Verge narrates the 1490–1530 arc; this book argues a mechanism
through the 1440–1500 arc and stakes a falsifiable prediction on it
— narrative proof-of-market, not a competitor for the claim.

**Philip Bobbitt, *The Shield of Achilles* (2002).** The titling and
category precedent: "the market state" as a durable coinage carried
by a big constitutional-history arc. "The artillery state" plays the
same category game with a shorter book and a falsification apparatus
attached. (Also on the retrieval ledger, LOW–MED, as an Appendix A
candidate.)
