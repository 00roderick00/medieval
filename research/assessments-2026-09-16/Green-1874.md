## 1. The work in two paragraphs

Green's is the founding one-volume Victorian narrative of English history
written as the history of a people rather than of its kings. Ch. **VI** is
titled "THE NEW MONARCHY, 1422–1540" (Contents, printed p. xi), in six
sections, of which **§3 is "The New Monarchy, 1471–1509" (p. 282)** (the others:
Joan of Arc, p. 265; the Wars of the Roses, p. 275; the New Learning, p. 297;
Wolsey, p. 314; Thomas Cromwell, p. 324). The coinage ch11 §I attributes to him
is a chapter title and a periodisation, not a stray phrase.

The account is constitutional decline in the Whig key: "But with the close of
the war of the Succession freedom suddenly disappears… Parliamentary life is
almost suspended… Arbitrary taxation re-appears in benevolences and forced
loans" (printed p. 283 / PDF 343). The cause is the destruction of the nobility
— "Freedom had been won by the sword of the Baronage… But with the battle of
Towton feudalism vanished away. The baronage lay a mere wreck after the storm of
the civil war" (printed pp. 283–4 / PDF 343–4) — leaving the Crown
"all-absorbing and unrestrained, the despotism of the New Monarchy" (printed
p. 284 / PDF 344).

## 2. THE VERIFICATION

**ch11/draft.md §I reads:** Green wrote of "the New Monarchy," the character of
"the English sovereignty from the time of Edward the Fourth to the time of
Elizabeth" (Green 1874, ch. VI).

**Green, printed p. 284 / PDF p. 344, verbatim:** "If we use the name of the New
Monarchy to express the character of the English sovereignty from the time of
Edward the Fourth to the time of Elizabeth, it is because the character of the
Monarchy during this period was something wholly new in our history."

**RESULT: ACCURATE, word for word, at printed p. 284 / PDF p. 344.** Both quoted
strings are exact and correctly framed — Green is *using* the name, and the
draft says so. The attribution **"ch. VI" is RIGHT**: the sentence stands in
ch. VI, §3, which begins at printed p. 282. The gap was bibliographic, not
factual, and it **CLOSES**. (A full-phrase grep fails because the sentence
breaks across a line in the sidecar at "from the time of Edward the Fourth to /
the time of Elizabeth" — recorded so the false negative is not repeated.)

At the same page, of interest and not for use: the old order "had been silently
sapped by the progress of industry, by the growth of spiritual and intellectual
enlightenment, and **by changes in the art of war**" (printed p. 284). A
gesture, not a mechanism — no artillery, no cost, no fisc, and in the next
clause the ruin is laid to persecution, disfranchisement and "the slaughter of
the Baronage."

## 3. TIER, and what its date makes it

Two uses, two tiers, never run together. **T1 for the coinage** — Green's text
is the primary document of what Green wrote, which is all ch11 §I's genealogy
needs. **T3 at best, and superseded, as an account of the fifteenth century** —
a Victorian Whig narrative of 1874, "serious trade and synthesis" under
CLAUDE.md §5. This **corrects ch11/memo.md's Addendum of 2026-07-27**, which
records the coinage as "quotable at T1 (public domain)": public-domain status is
not a tier, and a flat T1 would license Green's substance.

**Superseded on the New Monarchy by works already in repo** (Lander pins from
`research/assessments-2026-09-13/2026-09-13b-Lander.md`). (i) *Despotism* — "To
call such a monarchy strong is to mistake shadow for substance… inherently it
was a ramshackle structure … one of the shallow little backwaters of monarchy"
(pp. 72–3). (ii) *The baronage destroyed at Towton* — "Edward IV and Henry VII
would have regarded as little more than simple-minded the interpretation of
history which saw them putting down the nobility" (p. 67), with campaigning
across the whole wars "little more than 12 or 13 weeks in 32 years" (p. 62).
(iii) *Arbitrary taxation restored* — the fiscal record inverted: both kings
"were exceedingly circumspect in their demand for direct taxation" (pp. 39–40);
"The tax-paying classes had tied the king's hands behind his back" (p. 54). On
the administrative side, Wolffe's continuity corrective (*EHR* 79 (1964),
p. 237, at ch11/memo.md Revisions 1). At frame level the qualification runs
through Elton's revision and its critics and through Gunn, *HR* 82 (2009), and
Grummitt, *HR* 72 (1999) — **both held in this repo at abstract level only**,
named as critics and not cited for content.

## 4. What it ADDS

The pin, plus one fact about the historiography: Green's New Monarchy runs
**1422–1540** at chapter level and **1471–1509** at section level, and the
quoted sentence extends it to Elizabeth — useful to ch11 §I's honesty about the
frame, since the coinage was never Henry-VII-shaped.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

Nothing the book claims; §I already declares the frame contested. One drafting
caution: Green's fifteenth century is a story of liberty destroyed by the
crown's rise, so borrowing his vocabulary beyond the coinage would import a
constitutional-decline thesis the book does not hold and Lander refutes. Use the
sentence; leave the narrative.
