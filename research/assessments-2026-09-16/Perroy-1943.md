## 1. The article in two paragraphs

Perroy's subject is the administrative and financial anatomy of the
French royal artillery at the one moment it can be watched working: the
Picardy–Artois–Hainaut campaign of 1477, opened in the weeks after
Charles the Bold died at Nancy. His evidence is an archival accident.
Robert Fawtier found in a binding at the John Rylands Library "23
feuillets de papier et de 5 fragments de parchemin, épaves d'un dossier
plus important, qui avait dû comprendre une série de comptes tenus, sous
le règne de Louis XI, par divers officiers de l'artillerie royale" — 23
paper leaves and 5 parchment fragments, wreckage of a larger dossier
which must have comprised a series of accounts kept, under Louis XI's
reign, by various officers of the royal artillery [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p.
171; now Rylands French ms. 57). They matter because they are nearly all
there is: "toutes les archives et presque tous les comptes anciens de
l'artillerie ont dès longtemps disparu" (p. 171). Around them Perroy
sets a trawl of the Bibliothèque nationale (mss. fr. 20496–7, 20685,
23263–5, 26090–7 above all, with the Pièces originales), the Archives
nationales (PP 99), the communal archives of Amiens, Orléans, Beauvais,
Châlons, Lyon and Reims, and Côte-d'Or series B for the Burgundian side
(via Garnier) — work begun with Fawtier in 1925 and "longtemps enfoui
dans nos cartons" (p. 171).

He establishes four things: that the organised royal artillery belongs
to Louis XI's reign and not Charles VII's, its reform completed by
Guillaume Bournel, master from 15 August 1473 to his death in mid-1477;
that its unit was the *bande*, a small permanent regiment of gunners and
craftsmen with its own guns, repair workshop and wagon-train, first seen
acting together in 1477 and generalised at once; that the 1477 train can
be priced by the horse and by the day out of the financiers' own
forecast; and that the campaign was won without a battle: "Pas de bataille rangée avec un ennemi qui, d'ailleurs, n'était
pas en force. Mais une guerre de siège implacable, comme Louis XI
l'aimait. Le résultat justifiait l'effort : peu de vies humaines
sacrifiées, mais d'énormes dépenses de matériel … Les opérations de 1477
marquent donc l'apogée militaire du règne, et c'est à son artillerie
surtout que Louis XI dut ses succès" — No pitched battle with an enemy
who was in any case not in strength. But an implacable siege war, as
Louis XI liked it. The result justified the effort: few human lives
sacrificed, but enormous expenditure of matériel … The operations of
1477 therefore mark the military apogee of the reign, and it was above
all to his artillery that Louis XI owed his successes [TRANS. CLAUDE]
(pp. 299–300).

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**§IV's payroll-as-institution claim, at its own dates.** Contamine's
1491 *ordinaire* is the twenty-second of its series, which the draft
reads back to c. 1469; Perroy shows the object independently at that
date. On 2 October 1469 Gaspard Bureau paid nine gunners, Christophe
Constant the *garde de l'artillerie* and Jacques de Roubaix the *maistre
charetier* "pour leurs gaiges desdits trois moys 330 l. t., qui est pour
chacun d'eulx 10 l. t. par moys" (p. 172 n. 4); a quittance of 20
October covers 1,222 l. 5 s.t., the quarterly wages "de 40 canonniers, 1
maître charretier, 1 maître artilleur, 2 maîtres charrons et le garde de
l'artillerie" (p. 177 n. 28). A graded wage table for October 1469 is
printed entire (p. 191 n. 92): 11 gunners at 180 l., 2 at 144, 9 gunners
+ garde + carter at 120, 1 at 108, 1 at 96, 3 at 90, a *maître artilleur*
at 84, 2 at 72, 6 gunners + 2 master wheelwrights at 60, 4 at 48, 1 at
36. The standard rate is fixed by royal act: Roland le Tornier retained
on 23 May 1465 at 15 l.t. a month "ainsi que a noz autres canonniers"
(p. 191 n. 91) — 180 l. a year.

**§IV's audited-department claim, with teeth.** From 31 December 1464
Bureau ceased to account to the Chambre des comptes for the artillery's
money, which passed to finance officers and *gardes* (p. 177): command
separated from cash. The Chambre then long refused him his quittance
"parce qu'il restait redevable, pour les seuls deniers de l'artillerie,
de 28.704 l. 16 s. parisis, sans compter une somme de près de 12.000
livres de trop perçu sur ses gages et ceux de son contrôleur," forcing a
further peremptory royal mandate on 30 September 1467 (p. 177 n. 24).
Constant accounted directly to the Chambre from 11 August 1471, and its
register recorded his accounts "du 11 août 1471 au dernier septembre
1478, avec l'état final du 15 octobre 1478, jusqu'en 1486" (p. 172 n.
6).

**The two pins already in the draft**, both at p. 294: the Milanese
ambassadors' "la plus belle du monde," six leagues of convoy and 1,200
horses, "de quoi faire trembler le monde entier" (citing *Dépêches des
ambassadeurs milanais*, ed. Mandrot and Samaran, t. III, p. 130); and
the forecast's 800 *manœuvres ou pionniers* under a "capitaine du
charroi".

## 3. What it CORRECTS

**(i) The horse count. The draft (§IV) says the train's other stores
"required 1,009 more" horses; Perroy p. 294 says 1,609, and gives a
total the draft does not carry.** Printed: "Les canons et outils des
trois bandes, auxquels il fallait ajouter vingt-six coulevrines « de
l'ancienne ordonnance », demandaient déjà 521 chevaux. Pour les
provisions d'arcs, de flèches et d'arbalètes … il en fallait 1609
autres, ce qui portait le convoi au total de 2.130 chevaux" (p. 294,
verified on the page image; the sidecar OCR reads "1009" and "2.1 M").
The document itself closes at "Somme totale 2.130 chevaulx" (p. 302).
**The chapter has been carrying a figure six hundred horses short, and
no total.**

**(ii) The binding. The draft says the accounts were "recovered … from
the binding of a 1721 book."** Perroy says only "dans la reliure d'un
des volumes confiés à sa garde," and dates nothing (p. 171). The 1721 in
the vicinity is Père Daniel's *Histoire de la milice française*, named
two sentences later as the printed authority the find superseded. **The
date must be cut or re-sourced; it is not in Perroy.**

**(iii) An inconsistency internal to Perroy, to carry not resolve.** His
narrative says "vingt-six coulevrines de l'ancienne ordonnance" (p.
294); his document says "Pour les 36 couleuvrines de l'anciene
ordonnance … 60 chevaulx" (p. 301, verified on the image).

**(iv) Two further traps.** The forecast's arithmetic is defective as
printed, and Perroy prints it as found. The charroi's monthly sum is
"10.500 l. t." in the document (p. 302) and "10.600 l. t." in his note
119 (p. 294), while 2,130 horses at 3 s. 4 d. = 355 l.t. a day and the
grand total of 14,010 less 3,160 and 200 leaves 10,650; and "Somme par
jour 84 l. 6 s. 8 d." (p. 302) cannot be reconciled with its own five
line-items, which sum to 105 l. 6 s. 8 d. **Quote the totals as printed
and say the manuscript does not foot.**

## 4. What it ADDS

**(a) The establishment (pp. 187–194).** Three bands. The *grant bende*:
ten guns — one enormous bombard drawn by 41 horses, three very large,
two medium, four light *courteaulx* — 175 horses. Samain's: 29 permanent
men, thirty mouths of fire (the *Doyen* at eleven horses; two
serpentines, four heavy and twelve medium culverins, eleven *pers*), 121
horses. The Rousselet: 24 surviving names, seven serpentines, ten
culverins, eight *faucons*, 165 horses. Pay: Samain's captain 400 l.,
two at 180, two at 144, one at 120, one at 90, and "les vingt-deux
autres — soit l'énorme majorité — se contentent de 60 l. annuelles, ce
qui représente les gages d'un modeste ouvrier, maçon ou charron" (pp.
191–192); the Rousselet list seven at 90, one at 78, one at 72, fourteen
at 60, one at 48 (pp. 193–194). Day rates: *manœuvres* and pioneers 2 s.
6 d. (45 l. a year), masons, wheelwrights and unloaders 3 s. 4 d. (60 l.)
(p. 191 n. 89; p. 302). Officers: Bureau's *gages* 500 l. in 1467, 600
in 1469 (p. 177); Guillard 20 l.t. a month from 28 June 1468 (p. 174);
Pierre de Willeries, controller since 1461, 300 l. 1468–74 (p. 303 n.
13); the elder Rousselet's 200 l. pension assigned on the *recette* of
Vitry and the revenue of Château-Thierry and continued to his son on his
death, 17 March 1477 (p. 193).

**(b) The cost (pp. 300–302).** Charroi 2,130 horses at 3 s. 4 d. a day
= 355 l.t. a day. Extraordinary, per month: 800 pioneers 3,000 l.t.;
four men assisting the captain of the charroi 40 l.; six masons 30 l.;
six wheelwrights 30 l.; twelve unloaders 60 l.; greases and ironmongery
[? 50 l.]. **"Somme toute de la despence de ce present estat … par mois,
compris lesdites 200 l. pour achat de gresses cy dessus 14.010 l. t."**
(p. 302). Just over 14,000 l.t. a month for the train alone, before
powder — a forecast, not an account, and to be said so beside
Contamine's 1489 *extraordinaire* of 111,733 l.t.

**(c) The stores (pp. 294, 301–303).** Forecast: c. 60,000 lb of powder
and beaten matter, 20,000 saltpetre, 6,000 sulphur, 7 *muids* of
charcoal, together c. 92,000 lb at three horses the thousand = 276
horses; 43,000 lb of lead = 129 horses; 5,666 iron balls and a thousand
small, c. 200,000 lb = 600 horses; bombard- and cannon-stones = 200
horses. Actual, on the eve of the first fighting: "Guillard avait en ses
chariots 54.000 livres de poudre et salpêtre, 18.700 de plomb" (p. 294).
Sources named in the receipts: 30,039 lb of powder and 15,098 of lead
from Constant at Orléans; 7,263 + 7,893 lb of powder at Paris; 1,890 lb
of lead from Nesle; 6,845 lb from Reims and 1,229 from Rouen received at
Péronne, both delivered by the towns on royal order; 22,545 lb by
quittance of 23 July 1477 (pp. 302–303). **Powder was requisitioned from
towns; lead and shot moved on the crown's account.**

**(d) The sieges, dated (pp. 294–299).** Arras opened without a shot, 4
March; Thérouanne 3 April; Hesdin castle 8 April, where "un bombardement
de quelques heures, précédé d'une « approche » à la tranchée, suffit à
faire tomber le château" (p. 295); Boulogne invested 12 April, taken
before the 20th (Héliot: the 14th or 15th); la Montoire in Guines
battered 18 April, stormed and burned; the revolted town of Arras
invested 24 April and "capitulait le 4 mai" — ten days, the only serious
operation, requiring sap and mine against the outwork called the
Boulevard, for which the forecast 800 pioneers proved insufficient and a
further 3,000 were levied, 800 to 1,000 on Abbeville alone, paid daily
"pour éviter les désertions" (pp. 296–297); Le Quesnoy invested 23 May
and "en moins de 48 heures, la place se rendait"; Avesnes invested 10
June, a few hours' bombardment on the 11th sufficing; Saint-Omer failed
in August. **NOT FOUND AT THE PIN: Perroy nowhere writes *délai de
secours* or *composition*, and gives no terms of surrender** — only that
Commynes and the bastard of Bourbon negotiated the first submissions in
January and that Abbeville submitted without fighting on 17 January (p.
293 and n. 115). The capitulations are dated, not described.

**(e) Burgundy, for §VI — four findings.** (i) Bournel's reform may be a
Burgundian borrowing: "Il n'est pas impossible que Bournel ait emprunté
certains éléments de son organisation à ce qui existait déjà dans
l'armée du Téméraire" (p. 195 n. 106), with a ducal artillery band of
July 1474 for the Ferrette expedition costed from Garnier — one heavy
bombard, two *courtaulx*, nine serpentines, 267 horses, a master
bombardier, nine gunners, seventeen workmen, 120 pioneers, 2,350 l. a
month. (ii) The ducal apparatus did not pass only to Mary and
Maximilian: Louis XI raised a fourth unit, the *bande de Bourgogne*
under Jean Barrabin, styling himself "lieutenant général de l'artillerie
du Roi," for the conquest of the duchy and Franche-Comté — 17 gunners, 2
bombardiers, 17 craftsmen in spring 1477; guns and gunners from Troyes
and Langres; foreign gunners hired; seven iron serpentines of c. 500 lb
cast at Dijon for the siege of Vesoul in March 1477 — and the men are
paid through the *ducal* series, Archives de la Côte-d'Or B 1778 (p. 196
and nn. 108–112). Part of the office changed sovereign and kept
accounting. (iii) The French lost guns too: Constant's inventory of 151
pieces excludes "sept grosses coulloeuvrines de fonte amenées de Langres
et de Troyes que l'on dit estre perdues a Dole," lost in a sortie during
Craon's siege of Dole in summer 1477 (p. 173 n. 10, with Commynes and
Molinet). (iv) One of the twelve *pers* lost at Montlhéry in 1465 was
retaken from the Burgundians at Beauvais in 1472 and never rejoined its
band (p. 192; p. 301 n. 8).

**(f) The documents, citable at T1** (part 2, pp. 300–315). Four
*pièces justificatives*, printed entire; all T1 within a T2 edition, on
the Cosneau model. **1. [1476?] "Prévision de la dépense du
charroi et de l'extraordinaire de l'artillerie royale"**, BnF ms. fr.
20.497, f. 86–87 (pp. 300–302) — a financiers' forecast, and the
chapter's single best T1 exhibit: it prices a siege train by the horse
and by the day. **2. January–July 1477, "Fragment du compte des recettes
de P. Guillard, commis à la garde des munitions et outils de
l'artillerie"**, Rylands French ms. 57, f. 21 (pp. 302–304), closing
"Gontrollé de moy, Pierre de Willeris, controlleur de l'artillerie du
Roy nostre sire" (p. 304) — T1 for the supply chain and for
counter-signed control. **3. February–14 June 1477, "Compte des
distributions de Pierre Guillard"**, Rylands French ms. 57, f. 13–18
(pp. 304–313), signed "BOURNEL. DE WILLERIES." (p. 313) — the richest:
every delivery of powder, lead, crows and shovels by day, place,
recipient and weight, gunners and guns named (*la Levrette*,
*Michelette*, *Monjoye*, *le Chien*, *le Bouton*, the bombard
*Langres*). **4. July–September 1477, "Fragment d'un autre compte de
distribution"**, Rylands French ms. 57, f. 19–20 (pp. 314–315) — it
carries the authorising formulae Jean Cholet imposed on taking the
office ("par le commandement de bouche dû Roy nostre sire"; "par
l'ordonnance … dudit general de Normandie"; "par l'ordonnance de
Mons<sup>r</sup> de la Choletie, maistre de l'artillerie du Roy"), and
ends with the winter provision of Arras, 21 September 1477: 7,000 lb of
cannon powder, 3,000 of culverin powder, 3,000 of lead, 200 shovels, 100
picks and mattocks (p. 315).

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**The organisational claim for Charles VII's reign, at full strength.**
"D'organisation interne de l'artillerie, avec une hiérarchie dans le
commandement et des services administratifs distincts, il ne reste
aucune trace pour le règne de Charles VII. Les canonniers ne sont pas
enrégimentés, nous ignorons à quels chefs ils obéissaient, nous ne
savons qui contrôlait la fabrication et l'entretien d'un matériel encore
peu nombreux" — Of internal organisation of the artillery, with a
hierarchy of command and distinct administrative services, no trace
remains for the reign of Charles VII. The gunners are not formed into
units, we do not know what chiefs they obeyed, nor who controlled the
manufacture and upkeep of a matériel still few in number [TRANS. CLAUDE]
(p. 175). Perroy follows Borrelli de Serres, whose 1909 article he calls "incisif,
passablement désordonné et parfois outrancier" (p. 175), while rejecting
Borrelli's stronger negations: he accepts that the Bureaux exercised
"les fonctions de maître de l'artillerie" (p. 176) and that Gaspard kept
authority over matériel and personnel to his death (pp. 177–178). But
the deflation stands, and he dates the *bandes* after Bureau — barely
sketched at his death in 1469, little advanced by 1473, near-definitive
in late 1474, the merit "d'avoir accéléré et complété une réforme"
belonging "selon toutes probabilités" to Bournel (pp. 194–195).

**The book's best answer.** The chapter claims not a hierarchical corps
in the 1440s but a line of account and a department that can be audited,
and the masters' accounts run in series from 1418 — from Contamine's
annexes, not from Perroy, who is silent on them. Read forwards, Perroy's
evidence is the ratchet on schedule: the guns delivered the decisive
returns of 1449–53 under improvised command, and the institution that
made those returns permanent was built afterwards, by the king who
inherited them. **Honest grade: the answer is good, and it costs
something.** It forbids the chapter — and ch06 — from writing the
Bureaux as builders of an artillery *department*, and dates the
institutional claim to Louis XI wherever it rests on command structure
rather than on accounts.

**Two smaller contradictions, both anti-determinist.** The train lost
guns at Dole (p. 173 n. 10) and failed at Saint-Omer: "Moins heureux que
ses prédécesseurs, il échoua dans sa tâche et dut se retirer" (p. 299).
And the sequel: "Les campagnes ultérieures, contre les Flamands que
soutenait l'ardeur de Maximilien d'Autriche, ne devaient apporter que
des déboires" (p. 300). The best artillery in Europe stops working when
it meets Flemish towns. This reaches no spine ruling; it disciplines
§IV's register.

## 6. Placement

**ch10 §IV — primary.** T2 article carrying four T1 documents; cited
twice in the draft and badly under-used. It should carry the
establishment, the wage grid, the monthly cost and the audit of Bureau,
with Perroy named once in text as the editor of the only surviving
accounts of the office. **ch10 §VI** — footnote to paragraph-length: the
Burgundian model behind Bournel's reform, the *bande de Bourgogne*
raised on ducal accounts, the guns lost at Dole. **ch06** — the Charles
VII negative (p. 175) belongs to the chapter that owns the Bureaux;
ENTRY OWED there. **ch11** — the master's office as jurisdiction: the
memo cites Perroy p. 177 at second hand through Contamine, and the page
is now in the repo. **RETRIEVAL LIST** — the article carries Perroy's
own Montlhéry findings (four *faucons* and one *per* lost; Samain
commanding and captured) in his later citation of himself, which lowers
the 1925 row.

## 7. Verdict

It changes the chapter's weight and precision, not its direction: §IV
can now state one campaign's artillery establishment in horses, men,
wages, pounds of powder and livres a month, out of the financiers' own
forecast and the keeper's own account — the register the chapter claims
and has so far mostly asserted. The single most important thing
the author should know is that **the draft's "1,009 more horses" is an
OCR corruption of 1,609 and the printed total is 2,130**: the chapter is
six hundred horses short, and the sentence should carry the total and
the 14,010 l.t. a month. Second: Perroy dates the organised artillery
service to Bournel and Louis XI and finds "aucune trace" of one under
Charles VII, which the book should adopt as the ratchet's own chronology
rather than argue with.
