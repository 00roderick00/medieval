## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Leseur's *Histoire* is a domestic panegyric turned military journal. Its author was Gaston IV of Foix's *domestique* for nearly thirty years and wrote after his master's death at the commission of his heirs; Courteault is blunt about what that produces — "nul ne devait être plus capable de composer le panégyrique du comte que celui qui si longtemps avait vécu à ses côtés" — none could be more capable of composing the count's panegyric than the man who had so long lived at his side [TRANS. CLAUDE] (Intro., p. xix / PDF 33) — and equally blunt about the remedy: "aucun chroniqueur n'a peut-être plus de besoin d'être complété et rectifié à l'aide des documents diplomatiques contemporains" [TRANS. CLAUDE: no chronicler perhaps stands in greater need of being completed and corrected from contemporary diplomatic documents] (Intro., p. lxxv / PDF 89). Leseur was never in the count's councils — his was a "situation subalterne" — so he carries no policy or fiscal insight, only what he saw and what was said around him: "Tout ou presque tout ce que Leseur raconte, il le tient de la bouche de témoins oculaires" (Intro., p. xxvj / PDF 40). The chronicle survives in a single, non-original manuscript; chapter I, the opening of II, and chapters X–XIII are missing entirely.

Against that discount stands the editor's positive verdict, which is why this work is worth the chapter's time: "G. Leseur a dressé, pour trente années du xv^e siècle, un journal militaire des plus complets, le plus souvent exact et toujours très vivant" [TRANS. CLAUDE: Leseur has compiled, for thirty years of the fifteenth century, a military journal of the most complete kind, most often exact and always very vivid]; "peut-être même trouve-t-on dans Leseur plus de renseignements nouveaux et totalement ignorés jusqu'ici" (Intro., p. lxxv / PDF 89). For the Gascon war of 1451–53 that is exactly right, and Courteault says so at the page: of the Médoc manoeuvring, "Leseur est la source capitale pour toute cette partie de la campagne de Guyenne" (tome II, p. 11 n. 1 / PDF 25); of the quarrel among the French captains at Cadillac, "cet épisode du siège … ne nous est connu que par Leseur" (p. 24 n. 1 / PDF 38). He was **not** at Castillon — Gaston IV was in the Médoc — yet gives "un récit des plus détaillés, qu'il devait certainement tenir de quelque témoin oculaire" (p. 12 n. 1 / PDF 26). The standing caution is the editor's: "Leseur grossit volontiers les chiffres."

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

- **The *délai de secours*, at Fronsac, in the purest form the chapter has found.** Dunois and the English agreed that if by 15 June — "huyt jours après ensuyvans" — the English were not "les plus fors devant ladite place de Fronssac pour illec combatre les Françoys", then on that day they swore "de rendre la ville de Bourdeaulx et toutes les aultres villes et places de Guyenne, réservé la cité de Bayonne" [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome I, pp. 204–5 / PDF 302–3). Then the theatre of the empty field: "au jour xv dudit moys escheu, monseigneur de Dunoys mist tous ses gens d'armes en ung beau champ qu'il avoit fait clorre et préparer, et tindrent la bataille tout le jour, actendans les Angloys, qui toutesvoys n'y vindrent point; et lendemain ceulx de la place se rendirent. Aussy firent ceulx de Bourdeaulx, de Gadilhac, de Langon, Saint-Macquaire, Blanquefort, Gastillon et toutes les villes et places, réservé Bayonne" — on the fifteenth day expired, Dunois set all his men-at-arms in a fair field he had had enclosed and prepared, and they held battle-order all day, waiting for the English, who nonetheless did not come; and the next day those of the place surrendered. So did those of Bordeaux, of Cadillac, of Langon, Saint-Macaire, Blanquefort, Castillon and all the towns and places, Bayonne excepted [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome I, p. 205 / PDF 303). A duchy composing on the expiry of one unfought relief-day is §V's clock in one sentence of T1.
- **The composition market's demonstration effect, named by the buyer.** Bourg's captain composed early "doubtans d'aultre part que on leur feist le gieu que on avoit fait à leurs voisins de Blaye" — fearing besides that they would be played the game that had been played on their neighbours at Blaye [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome I, p. 203 / PDF 301). Fronsac's men composed "voyans que on les avoit fort batuz et approchez, et que à la longue il conviendroit que ilz disissent le mot" — seeing they had been heavily battered and approached, and that in the long run they would have to say the word [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome I, p. 204 / PDF 302).
- **The train's routine, at three sieges, and both Bureau brothers in the field in 1451.** Castillon: "à leur venue, maistre Jehan Bureau avecques ses pionniers fist clorre ung champ grant et spacieux … et oudit champ il fist mectre tout le bagaige et l'artillerie" (tome II, p. 13 / PDF 27). Bayonne: the heavy artillery sited where it would best batter the town, "et faisoit-on tyrer ladite artillerie jour et nuyt" (tome I, p. 210 / PDF 308). Cadillac: "maistre Jehan Bureau faisoit tyrer l'artillerie jour et nuyt, tant la grosse que la menue" (tome II, p. 24 / PDF 38). In 1451 "messire Jaspar Bureau, maistre de l'artillerie" serves with Foix's division while Jean Bureau serves with Dunois, who "conduysoit la bataille et l'artillerie" (tome I, pp. 207, 209–10 / PDF 305, 307–8).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

- **Castillon town: three days, not two.** Draft §VI: "The town of Castillon surrendered two days later under the continuing fire." Leseur: "Et, troys jours après, ceulx de la place de Castillon se rendirent et eurent pour composition" (tome II, p. 19 / PDF 33), with Courteault's note "La place capitula le 20 juillet" (p. 20 n. 1 / PDF 34) — three days after 17 July, and the treaty of 21 July in *Ordonnances* XIV, 280.
- **Cadillac's captain was executed by Gaston IV, not by the king.** Draft §VI: "it was reserved for a place under punitive terms, whose captain the king had executed (Vale 1969; 1974: 140; Hall 1997: 118)". Leseur: Gaillardet "se rendit à la volunté de mondit sieur de Foix, qui aultre composition ne luy voullut oncques accorder … mondit sieur le conte de Foix pour ses desmerites luy fist trencher la teste comme à ung traystre" (tome II, pp. 25–26 / PDF 39–40). Courteault, at the same siege: "Au moment de la capitulation de Cadillac, Charles VII était devant Bordeaux" (p. 22 n. 2 / PDF 36).
- **Escouchy's command attribution at Cadillac is wrong, and the editor says so.** "Mathieu d'Escouchy (t. II, p. 64) rapporte tout l'honneur du siège et de la prise de Cadillac au comte de Dunois: c'est une erreur" (tome II, p. 22 n. 2 / PDF 36). The siege was Gaston IV's, as the king's lieutenant; Charles VII sent Jean Bureau from Montferrand to reinforce him.
- **Blaye was stormed *and sacked*.** Draft §IV: "Blaye went in five days"; §III: "Blaye's walls were 'completely thrown down in many places' in five days". Leseur: "Et tost après ladite ville de Blaye fut prise d'assault, et les Angloys estans en icelle mis en sacqueman" (tome I, p. 202 / PDF 300). Memo Revisions 11 already carried Chartier's storm; Leseur is the second witness and adds the sack. This is outside the Normandy-scoped Somerset census and does not disturb its finding, but the chapter now has two independent witnesses to a Gascon storm-and-sack.

## 4. What it ADDS

- **The Castillon false report has an author and a different content.** Draft §VI carries "[GAP: the identity of the movers is carried in the tradition, not pinned in the parsed passage]". Leseur's version comes from inside the town: hearing the noise of the French fortifying, "ilz penserent que ilz chargeassent leur artillerye pour eulx enfuyr, et incontinent envoyerent au devant de Tallebot pour le fere avancer, disans que, se il ne se hastoit bien de venir, il perdroit les Françoys" — they thought the French were loading their artillery to flee, and at once sent to Talbot to hurry him, saying that if he did not make haste he would lose them [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome II, p. 16 / PDF 30). The garrison had baited him once already: the French "mectoyent grant paine d'eulx clorre et fortiffier, comme gens qui desjà avoient grant paour", and he would have "si beau marchié qu'il vouldroit" (p. 14 / PDF 28). **This does not confirm the camp-followers tradition; it supplies a rival, and a fourth witness's, answer**, which Courteault prefers: "Le récit de Leseur est plus vraisemblable que celui d'Escouchy" (p. 14 n. 2 / PDF 28).
- **Talbot's hurry, and what he saw before he charged.** He "se hasta tant qu'il n'eult point loisir de oyr la messe, et fist desfoncer deux ou troys pippes de vin pour fere boire ses Angloys, qui puysoient dedens avecques leurs salades comme en beau let" — he hurried so that he had no leisure to hear mass, and had two or three pipes of wine broached for his English, who scooped it up in their sallets as in fine milk [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 16 / PDF 30). His delay to let his infantry close up gave the French "bon loisir de assortir et asseoir toute leur artillerie sur chacune des venues"; then, at the park, "il se merveilla fort dont il vit les Françoys ainsi clos et fossoiez de si larges et profons fossez. Et adonc, sans marchander, vint et marcha ledit Tallebot … monté sur une petite hacquenée, jusques à la barriere du champ, et commença à cryer: A pié, à pié tout le monde!" — he marvelled greatly to see the French so enclosed and ditched; and then, without haggling, he came on, mounted on a little hackney, up to the barrier of the field, and began to cry: on foot, on foot, everyone! [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 17 / PDF 31). He saw the works and attacked anyway: deception and temerity in one paragraph, from one witness. Courteault's note adds Drouyn's count — three hundred pieces under the Genoese gunner Louis Giribault, the camp ditches laid so the enemy would be struck from several points at once (p. 18 n. 1 / PDF 32).
- **A private levy, mustered and paid by the crown — the mechanism at the baronial scale.** Gaston, made lieutenant, went to Béarn, "incontinent fist assembler ses estatz des nobles et gens de ses pays … et en toute diligence manda son ban et rieban", and in under a fortnight had 500 lances and (his figure) 6,000 crossbowmen and pavisers in the field (tome II, pp. 7–8 / PDF 21–22). Courteault supplies the documentary counterpart: on 15 April Charles VII sent Théaulde de Valpergue from Montbazon **to take the muster of the troops placed under the count of Foix**, the official figure being 500 lances without the *gens de trait* (Arch. des Basses-Pyrénées, E 68), and 10,000 francs were assigned to the payment of the counts of Foix and Armagnac and the sire d'Albret (BnF ms. fr. 26081, n° 6589) (p. 8 n. 1 / PDF 22). In 1451 the king went further: "pour ce qu'il estoit lieutenant du Roy en Guyenne, le Roy manda à aucuns des cappitaines qu'ilz et leurs compaignies se myssent soubz luy et en sa bande" (tome I, p. 206 / PDF 304). **And the count's own machine was coercive:** Courteault records that in 1451 "le trésorier de Foix reçut ordre de faire emprisonner tous les habitants qui refusaient de fournir leur contribution de guerre. Les Etats du comté de Foix étaient obligés de s'endetter pour satisfaire aux exigences du comte" (tome I, p. 49 n. 1 / PDF 147, citing Olhagaray and Faget de Baure) — flatly against Leseur's "voluntairement et agreablement obeirent".
- **The fisc in the field, and the two citadels.** Jean Le Boursier, seigneur d'Esternay, "gênerai de France" — *général des finances* — brought the victualling fleet up the Gironde to Blaye and took five Bordeaux ships (tome I, p. 202 / PDF 300); at the campaign's end he received 2,000 *livres* for his costs "tant sur la rivière de la Gironde comme au siège devant Bayonne" (Rôle of 7 May 1454, BnF ms. fr. 20683, fol. 47; Courteault's note, ibid.). At Fronsac the treaty commissioners were "Jacques de Chabannes, Théaulde de Valpergue, Jean Bureau et Jean Le Boursier, seigneur d'Esternay" (Courteault citing Chartier, tome I, p. 204 n. 2 / PDF 302) — the artillery master and the finance general sealing the instrument the guns compelled, as at Harfleur. And at the end: "le Roy commist à la garde et gouvernement dudit pays de Guyenne monsieur le conte de Clermont, et avecques luy y demourerent monsieur de Saintrailles, messire Theolde de Valpergue, maistre Jehan Bureau et messire Jehan le Bourssier, ausquelx le Roy donna charge de fere commencer le chasteau du Ha et de Trompecte" (tome II, p. 29 / PDF 43) — the same two men left behind to build the forts that would hold the reconquered city, while Gaston "s'en retourna en ses pays avecques ses gens d'armes, auxquelx il donna congié d'eulx en aller à leurs maisons". The prince's army dissolves; the king's stays and builds.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**Castillon was decided by a cavalry charge of Bretons, not by the guns.** Leseur's guns do work — "maistre Jehan Bureau faisoit fort bien son devoir à fere tyrer serpentines et grosses coulouvrines, dont les Angloiz furent fort batuz et endomaigés" — but they do not break the attack: "Toutesfoiz les Angloys … si croissoient et arriverent tousjours les gens de pié." What breaks it is fresh cavalry thrown into the press where Talbot's banners stood — la Hunaudaye and Montauban with 240 Breton lances and la Bessière with a hundred more — "et ceulx de ceste bande … commencerent tous d'un front à charger et donner dedens les Angloys si asprement … que en bien petit d'heure ilz porterent et ruerent par terre les enseignes de Tallebot. Et adonc les Angloys, se voyans charger de si près, virerent le dos et se mistrent en fuyte, et en ce tumulte fut abbatu Tallebot" — and those of this band, close-ordered, began all in one front to charge and drive into the English so fiercely that in very little time they bore down and threw to the ground Talbot's banners. And then the English, seeing themselves charged so close, turned their backs and took to flight, and in that tumult Talbot was brought down [TRANS. CLAUDE] (tome II, pp. 17–18 / PDF 31–32). The hand-to-hand fighting had lasted "par l'espace d'une heure" before that charge. Talbot dies in the rout, unhorsed in the tumult — not, as Pollard has him in the draft, killed at the rampart with his skull broken by an axe.

**The book's best answer.** It is the chapter's own two-speeds discipline, and Leseur strengthens rather than weakens it. The draft already stages Howard, DeVries and Hall to the effect that field guns earned their keep late, and already locates decisiveness at the walls; Leseur is a fourth witness to the same division of labour. What the guns did at Castillon, on his telling, was hold a position that could not be carried — Talbot "se merveilla fort" at the ditches and still had to come at them frontally, and the hour of mêlée a park bought was the hour in which fresh reserves could be brought to bear. The park existed because Bureau's pioneers built it as a matter of course to house the siege train; the battle was fought where the artillery train said it would be fought. That answer is good — but it must be made with Leseur's Bretons *in the text*, not smoothed away, because the alternative reading is available to any objector who opens the same page. The concession costs nothing the chapter needs and buys the register the chapter claims. Nothing here reaches a spine ruling.

**A second, smaller contradiction:** a witness with every motive to credit his master credits Bretons Gaston did not command at Castillon, and credits Gaston rather than the train at Cadillac — which is itself why the Breton limb must be carried and the Cadillac limb discounted.

## 6. Placement

ch06 §VI (Castillon) — **T1 primary, evidence and disciplined dissent**; engage Leseur **by name** in the text as the fourth independent witness: the park as Bureau's standing practice, Talbot's hurry and the wine in the sallets, the garrison's false report as a named rival to the camp-followers tradition, the Breton charge as the stated qualification. ch06 §VI (Cadillac) — T1 primary, decisive: the siege narrative, the execution by Gaston IV, the castle's next-day surrender. ch06 §V (1451) — T1 primary: the Fronsac *délai de secours* and the empty field of 15 June; Bourg composing for fear of "le gieu … fait à leurs voisins de Blaye"; the Blaye storm and sack. ch06 §VII / ch07 — footnote level: Le Boursier on the Gironde, Bureau and Le Boursier as treaty commissioners, the Hâ and Trompette commission, the muster of Foix's private levy by a royal commissioner. **Courteault's apparatus** is T2 riding beneath the T1 and is in places the more valuable half: it adjudicates Escouchy at Cadillac, dates Castillon's capitulation, supplies the Basses-Pyrénées muster and the ms. fr. 26081 payment, and rates Leseur's figures throughout ("Leseur grossit volontiers les chiffres"). **Role:** ally and witness — an ally whose panegyric frame must be flagged on every use, exactly as the chapter flags Escouchy's moral narrative and Basin's temerity topos.

## 7. Verdict

Leseur changes this chapter's **weight** decisively and its **register** at one point: he converts Cadillac from a three-way muddle into a narrated siege with a named commander and a stated sequence, closes the chapter's Vale problem by showing that the sentence Vale drew from him is not in him, and gives §V the cleanest T1 statement of the relief-clock the book has found — a duchy composing on the expiry of one unfought day. The register cost is real and must be paid: his Castillon is decided by a Breton charge after an hour of hand-to-hand fighting, and the chapter has to say so in the text rather than in a note. The single most important thing to know is that the fourth witness settles Cadillac against both modern secondaries and mostly for Escouchy — the town stormed, the castle composing the next day, the captain beheaded by Gaston IV for perjured homage and not by the king on 17 September — and that Vale's "on his arrival at the place, they surrendered" is the exact reverse of what stands at the pin he cites.
