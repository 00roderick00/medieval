# Decisions briefing — the second-reader review of 14 September

## How this enters the project

The review is a reflective document and is filed as one. It cannot be cited; its evidence enters the book only through the provenance gate — which is the first practical point, because two of its three new historical scenes (Alençon; the Lombard lenders) are pinned to Chastellain "actual tome III," and tome III is not in corpus (vols 1, 9, 11, 15 are). Those scenes are therefore [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL] until the volume is in and the pages read. The third pin (Blondel on Harfleur, Stevenson's *Narratives* p. 152) can be checked now: the *Narratives* volume is in corpus, and ch06 §IV already carries the sentence at Harfleur's seventeen-day battery — the review's "not Talbot at Castillon" caution is met by the draft as it stands, though the passage's re-use elsewhere would need the attribution repeated.

The review's central proposal is a book-level change and so is a CLAUDE.md §1 / spine matter: it does not drift the argument's three claims, but it splits the third. "Predicts parallel Tudor settlements inside three or four blocs" currently lets the reader assume that the subordination of the barons and the people's acquisition of control over the consolidated apparatus are one event. The review asks that they be separated, with the second left open and scored. That is compatible with ruling §8(b) (hybrid per bloc) and with §8(c) (America absorbs the stack); it adds a further axis — who controls the absorbing state — which no ruling yet addresses. Decision 0 below is that ruling. Everything else in the review is chapter-level and can be decided by yes/no, in most cases alongside an existing rulings-sheet row.

## Decision 0 — the split (spine, CLAUDE.md §1 claim 3; yours)

EXISTING CLAIM. Spine §3 (the pendulum) and §8(b); CLAUDE.md §1 claim 3: the mechanism "predicts parallel Tudor settlements inside three or four blocs." Ch12 §VII and the Coda close on the settlement as order. Nowhere does the manuscript state that the settlement's constitutional form is a separate question from its occurrence.

NEW EVIDENCE. Not a source but a distinction, supported by three things already in the file: Tilly pp. 207–8 (ch12 memo Revisions 10, PENDING) — consolidation financed without bargaining yields state edifices "in the absence of significant consent"; Elliott 1992 (assessed 13 Sept) — the composite endures as the settlement's own form; Ágoston/İnalcık — a consolidation that devolved from inside. Bagehot's dignified/efficient distinction (T2, public domain, one click) as the bridge.

CONSEQUENCE. The predictive claim becomes two claims: (i) the barons are subordinated (the mechanism, unchanged, DEFEND tier); (ii) the constitutional form of the resulting state is open, with "ornamental democracy" — legitimating authority separated from effective control — as one scoreable outcome and the review's five mechanisms as its indicators. The ending changes: the Coda can no longer let (i) imply popular control. Chapter 9 gains a second subject (constitutional appearance v. effective power, with the Henry VI protectorate as its historical instance); Appendix C gains a democratic-control sub-wager with its own falsifiers (elections that redirect strategic commitments; legislatures enforcing conditions; courts whose remedies change operations; public capacity to replace suppliers).

DECISION REQUIRED. Whether to adopt the split as spine §8(g). If yes: (1) the wording of claim 3 in CLAUDE.md §1 — your edit; (2) whether "ornamental democracy" is the book's second coinage (the voice rule allows one per chapter; the book-level coinage is spent on the artillery state / the Tudor turn — this would need to be ch09's or ch12's chapter-level coinage, and the ledger check against Crouch's "post-democracy" and Wolin's "inverted totalitarianism" must show it is not theirs renamed); (3) whether Bagehot's dignified/efficient is the bridge, given that the review's own caution — the dignified parts were not decoration — is exactly the disanalogy the bridge must carry. I would note one risk for your judgement: the split is the right move analytically and it is also the move a reviewer will read as the book hedging its prediction. The answer is the scoring in decision 8 — the democratic-control claim is made falsifiable in the same terms as the consolidation claim, not left as atmosphere.

## Decision 1 — what the barons lose and retain (ch11 §VII, ch12 §§IV–VII; chapter-level, yours)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch12: "the over-mighty citizen"; the twelve American cases preserving wealth by design (spine §8(c)); the licensing move ("regulation of the switch, not seizure," your 2 September ruling). The drafts already say the barons keep wealth and lose the switch; they do not itemise the switch.

NEW EVIDENCE. The review's list — refusal of strategic demands, control of essential infrastructure, succession, operational knowledge, independent rule-setting — is the itemisation. Chastellain t. III pp. 484–87 (Alençon: crown retains artillery and named fortifications, preserves other family interests, postpones execution) — UNVERIFIED, tome III not in corpus. Already in corpus and confirming: Lander on Bergavenny (bond, not destruction); Fortescue's instruments; Lane on Colleoni (sequestration after death, statue).

CONSEQUENCE. "Consolidating the barons" becomes five named transfers of discretion; the American licensing chapter can be scored transfer by transfer. Strengthens §8(c) without changing it.

DECISION REQUIRED. Yes/no on the itemised form; retrieval of Chastellain t. III before the Alençon scene is drafted (row added).

## Decision 2 — composite monarchy as the American form (ch11 §V, ch12 §IV; overlaps rulings-sheet (n))

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch11 §V treats Spain as a fourth instance of the sequence; ch12 §IV's American reading is "USA absorbs the stack" (§8(b)/(c)). Rulings-sheet (n) already asks whether the DEFEND-tier claim narrows to the core polity ruling a composite by contract.

NEW EVIDENCE. Elliott 1992 (in corpus, assessed): *aeque principaliter*; "the very looseness of the association was in a sense its greatest strength"; only three secessions 1523–1707. Elliott 1963 on Castile as the fiscal core.

CONSEQUENCE. American consolidation is predicted as selective — federal command in named strategic domains, private ownership, state-level resistance, judicial boundaries, sectoral variation — and that plurality is the settlement, not a delay before uniformity. This is (n) with a positive statement attached rather than a narrowing. The disanalogy (America is not Spain; the comparison is institutional) must be stated in the bridge.

DECISION REQUIRED. Rule (n) and this together: one decision.

## Decision 3 — indispensable intermediaries: command, dependence, capture (ch10 §VIII, ch12 §IV)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch10 §VIII concedes the modern base cannot be bought by revenue alone; ch12's licensing move leads with revocation (your ruling). Neither distinguishes a state that commands from one that depends.

NEW EVIDENCE. Chastellain t. III pp. 315–16 (the Lombard lenders punished and restored because publicly necessary) — UNVERIFIED, tome III not in corpus. In corpus and on the same point: Johns on the Stationers (the crown won *quo warranto* and could not run the medium); Lane on Venice unable to man its galleys; Miller's Pentagon-cannot-afford-a-fab; Farrell & Newman (arrived 14 Sept, unassessed) on chokepoints held by private firms.

CONSEQUENCE. The mechanism gains a three-way distinction — command (legal coercion), dependence (operational indispensability), capture (the dependent state's policy made by the supplier) — that can coexist; the platform settlement is predicted as command-with-dependence, and the durability of that bargain becomes the thing Appendix C watches. This is the strongest of the eight for the book, because it is where Hoffman's private-budget inversion and Varoufakis's co-rentier objection both get answered.

DECISION REQUIRED. Yes/no; retrieval of t. III; whether the three terms are the chapter-level coinage for ch10 (check against Farrell & Newman's own vocabulary before claiming it).

## Decision 4 — Talbot's competence as part of the tragedy (ch06 §VI; overlaps spine §8(e), §8(f) item 3)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch06 §VI: the Talbot conjecture, qualified; §8(f) "Talbot and the Belloc syndrome — Talbot's death illustrative of attacking the weapon, not the process." The Caen plot (Vale, assessed 13 Sept) entered as removing the objection that nobody thought that way.

NEW EVIDENCE. Barker (in corpus, assessed): Talbot's two hundred guns at Dieppe 1442–43. The review's distinction — the plot shows attacking the train was intelligible; it does not prove Talbot's intention — is exactly the 13 September synthesis's wording.

CONSEQUENCE. Talbot is recast from the man who missed the invention to the expert whose system stopped supplying the means; the Belloc pairing survives but the "syndrome" is no longer naivety. No ruling changes; §VI's framing sentence does.

DECISION REQUIRED. Yes/no on the recast. Low cost, high gain.

## Decision 5 — "the walls and the minds of the besieged" as a recurring problem of knowledge (ch06 §IV; ch09; ch12)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch06 §IV carries the Blondel sentence once, at Harfleur, with [TRANS. CLAUDE]. It is not used elsewhere.

NEW EVIDENCE. None new; Stevenson's *Narratives* is in corpus and the pin (p. 152) can be verified now. The review's reading — walls embody expectations that outlive their material basis; so do constitutional expectations — is the book's, not Blondel's, and must be presented as such.

CONSEQUENCE. A recurring image across ch06, ch09 and ch12 — but the voice rule permits ONE vivid metaphor per chapter, and ch06's is already spent (the ledger will say which). A book-level recurring image is a different thing from a chapter metaphor and is allowed, provided it is one image. The review's own discipline — dissent is not siege-mentality; some defenders negotiated intelligently (Cherbourg sold; the Stationers kept their court) — must be written in.

DECISION REQUIRED. Whether this becomes the book's recurring image (it competes with nothing at book level; the longbow/cannon metaphor is the governing one, spine §4, and this would sit beside it). Your call on voice.

## Decision 6 — untidy cases; establishment v. unit cost; planned v. collected (ch06 §§I, VII; ch10; overlaps rulings-sheet (a))

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch06 §I: "a pair of controlled comparisons"; §VII: "as near a controlled experiment as the fifteenth century offers." Rulings-sheet (a): establishment cost, not unit price.

NEW EVIDENCE. Barker's deflationary readings (Cherbourg sold; the Grey Tower starved); Hall on logistics; Depreter on planned états revised downward and the *charroi* levy contested; Cosneau on a levy assessed with the sum left blank; Grummitt on formal records masking informal reality.

CONSEQUENCE. "Controlled" goes; the proof is restated as an organised system that makes continued resistance unaffordable, and the mechanism is shown explaining failures (Burgundy, Venice's manning, the Ottoman devolution) as well as successes. Two vocabulary disciplines: establishment v. weapon; planned v. collected.

DECISION REQUIRED. Rule (a) and this together; the "controlled" wording is a one-line yes.

## Decision 7 — constitutional bargaining and popular agency (ch07, ch09; new for ch09)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch09 treats the 1454 protectorate as "improvised, resented, dissolved"; ch07 treats Cade's programme as "shaped, not prevented" and the commons as resistance the mechanism overrides. The minority of 1422–37 is not distinguished from the incapacity of 1453–55.

NEW EVIDENCE. Griffiths (in corpus, assessed) on the minority council's success and on the three Cade manifestos; Lander p. 76 (integrated 13 Sept) with his palace-revolution dissent on the protectorate; Scott & Scribner on the Speyer recess; Payling on the Chaworth petition (the community asking the state for enforcement); Lane on the Great Council reversing a war tax and the doge pleading it back.

CONSEQUENCE. Ch09 becomes the chapter on constitutional appearance v. effective power — the minority that worked because it was designed, the incapacity that failed because the office could not be filled — and so the historical anchor for decision 0. Ch07 must give the commons consequences for the settlement, not only a defeat. This is also where the book answers the review's demand to say how citizens prevent the ornamental outcome: bargaining over revenue, withholding cooperation, organising, enforceable conditions — each with a fifteenth-century instance.

DECISION REQUIRED. Whether ch09 takes the larger subject (it is the review's first-named chapter and the shortest Part II draft at 3,316 words; the outline's ch09 brief should be checked for conflict and flagged, not silently resolved).

## Decision 8 — scoring the forecast (Introduction; ch12 §VII; Appendix C; overlaps rulings-sheet Appendix C rows)

EXISTING CLAIM. Ch12 draft line 546: "critics can only refine." The Introduction publishes failure conditions with dates; Appendix C's indicators are scored bloc-wide.

NEW EVIDENCE. Chastellain's failed royal-death predictions (tome to identify — not necessarily III; UNVERIFIED) as the passage on forecasts surviving by changing their meaning.

CONSEQUENCE. "Can only refine" is cut. Appendix C scores four blocs separately (the review adds India — the spine's "three or four" already allows it); each indicator distinguishes enacted authority / demonstrated compliance / operating capacity / democratic accountability; a failed American prediction counts as a failure regardless of elsewhere. Decision 0's democratic-control sub-wager gets its falsifiers here.

DECISION REQUIRED. Yes/no on the four-level scoring and the per-bloc rule (CLAUDE.md §2: "never soften the introduction's failure conditions or Appendix C's indicators into vagueness" — this sharpens them, so it is inside doctrine). Cutting "can only refine" needs no ruling and should be recorded as a correction now.
