PROJECT OVERVIEW
The Artillery State
What the project is, and what it hopes to show
A concise essay and document map
Roderick B Jones
August 2026

The Artillery State: Cannon, Code, and the Return of the Consolidator is a book about where power goes when the price of decisive force changes. It begins from a question that runs through the project's private judgement layer, spine.md: who has power, when, and why? The immediate historical problem is the collapse of the distributed political order of the later Middle Ages. Kings shared authority with great lords, cities, mercenary companies, the universal Church, legal corporations, and local jurisdictions; sovereignty was layered, negotiated, and often visibly incomplete. The modern problem is that a comparable dispersal has returned in altered form. Platforms govern speech, identity, commerce, and access across borders; private firms hold military, intelligence, logistical, and computational capacities once associated with states; law is divided among public courts, arbitration, platforms, and transnational regimes. The project accepts the neomedieval diagnosis of that condition. Its disagreement concerns what comes next.

The detailed argument in outline.md, the working doctrine in CLAUDE.md, and the assembled text in manuscript.md all turn on one causal chain: the capital intensity of decisive violence creates a requirement for permanent finance; permanent finance creates administrative capacity and legibility; those capacities allow rulers to subordinate intermediate authorities. This is the project's meaning of an artillery state. Cannon mattered not because gunpowder was simply more modern than the longbow, but because an effective royal train demanded foundries, powder, transport, trained specialists, magazines, accounts, taxes, and continuity between campaigns. A lord might own guns, just as a city might hire infantry, but few political actors could reproduce the full system at scale. The state that could pay for the train acquired the means to decide what happened to everybody else's walls.

The book therefore treats the late medieval world not as a picturesque analogy for the present but as a completed historical mechanism. Part I diagnoses dispersal. Chapter 1, The Return of the Middle Ages, reconstructs the neomedieval literature and the overlapping sovereignties it describes. Chapter 2, The New Church, treats digital platforms as institutional universalisms rather than ordinary market actors. Chapters 3 and 4, Bastard Feudalism, Inc. and The Condottieri, examine contracted power, private force, the market for allegiance, and the conditions under which a contractor might become a sovereign. Part II turns from condition to crisis. Chapter 5, The Undermighty Kings, studies hollow monarchy and the prior rise of over-mighty subjects. Chapter 6, 1450: The Year the Walls Fell, makes the mechanism falsifiable through the Bureau artillery service, the temporal compression of the Norman and Gascon reconquests, Formigny, and Castillon. Chapters 7 and 8, Cade and the Communes and Private Justice, Private War, show resistance and legal plurality operating inside consolidation rather than preventing it. Chapter 9, The King's Two Bodies, Broken, asks how personal rule becomes institutional rule when the natural body of the sovereign fails.

Part III supplies the settlement. Chapter 10, The Artillery State, joins the royal artillery service to the compagnies d'ordonnance, the permanent taille, and the administrative machinery of Charles VII and Louis XI; Burgundy and the Swiss serve as counter-cases against technological determinism. Chapter 11, The New Monarchies, compares the French settlement with Henry VII's England, Ferdinand and Isabella's Spain, Ottoman centralisation, and the state's eventual absorption of the Church's functions. Chapter 12, Not Modernity, But Tudor, translates the five consolidating moves into a conditional modern prediction. The coda states the method and its limits: mechanism under conditions, not destiny; comparison governed by explicit disanalogy; and a wager that can fail.

The modern claim is deliberately sharper than the observation that drones resemble longbows or that data resembles print. Cheap, distributed systems can win battles and disrupt powerful institutions. They do not necessarily supply decisive power. The modern cannon is the full stack: advanced compute, leading-edge foundries, orbital sensing, secure communications, industrial munitions capacity, energy, identity systems, payment rails, and administrative tools capable of making continental populations legible. Only a handful of polities may be able to assemble and finance that stack. The project's wager is that the requirement will press the United States, China, the European Union, and perhaps India towards distinct Tudor-style settlements inside their own blocs, while disorder persists between and beneath them. China appears furthest along; the United States possesses much of the stack but has not subordinated its universal platforms; Europe has regulatory and fiscal tools but an unresolved political form; India is the conditional fourth. Russia is treated as a demonstration of what weapons without the full fiscal-administrative stack cannot achieve. Nuclear weapons remain outside the mechanism because destruction without territorial control is a different strategic category.

That prediction is not presented as an accomplished fact. The spine divides the book's claims into what it will defend, what it holds on balance, and what it knowingly wagers. It also insists on the future tense: the reassertion of state power is uneven and incomplete, but the ability to field decisive force may compel consolidation. spine-audit.md tests the drafts against that conviction map, identifying places where the prose is too certain, too cautious, or substantively at odds with the spine. Appendix C turns the wager into measurable leading indicators with baselines and directional predictions. The project hopes to show not merely that consolidation is possible, but that its necessity can be observed and its progress scored.

The archive is built to prevent the thesis from outrunning its evidence. Every chapter folder, from ch00-intro/ through ch12/, contains a fixed brief.md, a graded sources.md, a research memo.md, a steelmanned critiques.md, and a draft.md. The brief states purpose, comparators, research focus, and named objection. The source file distinguishes primary material, canonical scholarship, serious synthesis, unverified leads, and material still awaiting retrieval. The memo separates consensus, dispute, and the book's own inference. The critique file asks the strongest available objection to do real damage before the prose is accepted. The draft begins only after review. Chapter 6 adds artillery-reconquest.md, a fortress-by-fortress evidentiary table; Chapter 7 adds cade-composition.md, a focused probe into the rising's social and military composition. The same sequence makes the chapter folders laboratories rather than mere containers for prose.

The apparatus extends that discipline. appendix-a/ annotates the neomedievalism literature; appendix-b/ records the comparative polities; appendix-c/ maintains the book's scorecard and its longer running memo; and appendix-d/ provides a reader's guide. The coda/ contains the methodological conclusion, presently awaiting final human review. At the root, voice.md defines the historian-practitioner register; reading-list.md and retrieval-master.md maintain the source agenda; artillery_state_outline.pdf preserves a portable outline; and research/ holds the authoritative acquisition dossier, the rewrite status report, the Cambridge-derived style standard, its delta against the current drafts, and the underlying faculty guide. research/master-status.md currently records the remaining bridges, evidence gaps, translations, and press-date rechecks, making incompleteness visible rather than smoothing it away.

The corpus/ is the evidence store. Its bundled/ section contains the foundational neomedieval texts and an index mapping them into the book; retrieved/ holds medieval chronicles and historiography, military and state-formation scholarship, contemporary official and legal documents, and a structured source-library/ of OCR, Cambridge material, MPhil work, official primary sources, open-access scholarship, primary editions, and supplements. The workshop/ is a deliberately firewalled exploratory space: its Bureau, cannon, and Talbot dossiers develop lives, careers, supply chains, scenes, and hypotheses, but nothing crosses into the book without explicit authorisation and a fresh provenance check. tools/assemble.py builds the root manuscript and status report; archive/ is reserved for deprecated material rather than deletion; .git/, .gitignore, and old-commit-shas.txt preserve version history and provenance; and the minimal README.md simply marks the repository.

Taken together, these documents reveal the project's deeper ambition. It is not trying to prove that the twenty-first century is medieval, or that technology mechanically produces stronger states, or that the predicted settlement is desirable. It is trying to explain a return swing in the distribution of power. The neomedievalists described how sovereignty dispersed. The Artillery State asks what happens when decisive capability once again becomes too expensive, too integrated, and too administratively demanding for dispersed authorities to sustain. Its answer is conditional but plain: power will follow the institutions able to pay for the system, and the next political order may look less like the completion of modernity than another Tudor turn.

Project document and folder map

The root documents divide responsibility. outline.md is the public architecture and controlling argument; spine.md is the private judgement layer; spine-audit.md checks conviction and tense; CLAUDE.md governs provenance, comparison, workflow, and boundaries; voice.md governs prose; manuscript.md is the assembled working book; artillery_state_outline.pdf is its portable outline; reading-list.md, retrieval-master.md, and research/acquisition-dossier.md govern acquisition; research/master-status.md, research/style-standard.md, and research/style-delta.md govern completion and presentation. README.md, .gitignore, old-commit-shas.txt, and .git/ support repository identity and history.

The production folders are ch00-intro/ and ch01/ to ch12/, each using the brief.md–memo.md–sources.md–critiques.md–draft.md sequence, with the two special evidentiary probes in Chapters 6 and 7. coda/ retains the same five-file structure, although its memo, sources, and critiques remain open placeholders around the present draft. The apparatus is carried by appendix-a/appendix.md and its memo, appendix-b/appendix.md, appendix-c/appendix.md and its running memo, and appendix-d/appendix.md.

The evidence and development folders are corpus/bundled/, whose index.md maps the foundational PDFs to chapters; corpus/retrieved/ and its structured source-library/ branches; research/, including History_Faculty_style_guide_2020-21_0.pdf; and the firewalled workshop. The latter contains bureau/careers.md, bureau/dossier.md, and bureau/lives.md; cannon/dossier.md and cannon/supply-chain.md; and talbot/dossier.md, talbot/hypothesis.md, and talbot/life.md. The operational folders are tools/, containing assemble.py, and archive/. Together they move the project from thesis, through evidence and objection, to reviewed prose, assembly, audit, and a prediction that remains open to disproof.
