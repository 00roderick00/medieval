# "War: Watt Is It Good For?" — which stack is decisive, and what the article does to spine §4

## 1. The argument in one paragraph

Krejsa and Benich say the battlefield tools remaking war in Ukraine —
drones, unmanned ground vehicles, on-board AI, offensive cyber — all
strip down to the same components, which they call "electrotech":
batteries, electric motors, power electronics and embedded compute,
"the integrated set of hardware and software that are transforming
electricity from a physical flow into something that also can be
digitally generated, stored, and directed." The visible effect is a
"dramatic democratization of military capability" (Ukraine, "one of
Europe's poorest countries," holding off an invader many times its
size); the hidden cause is "an equally dramatic consolidation" of that
common substrate in China, because electrotech is consumer-electronics
manufacturing, not heavy industry, and the firms that make phones and
batteries (Xiaomi, BYD) became the drone, vehicle and robot firms. AI
compounds it: "compute is merely electricity by other means," so "the
ability to generate, store, and direct electricity is becoming the
master metric of national strength." Their prescription is domestic:
stop sabotaging the civilian electrotech market (the IRA repeal gutted
the demand the battery plants were built against), prioritise risk by
"smart-to-dumb" (the control-software layers, not the graphite), and
let the climate-tech and defence-tech communities recognise that they
are one industrial base — the Detroit "arsenal of democracy" point,
that a reserve "can only serve military needs if built at civilian
scale."

## 2. What it does to spine §4 (longbow and cannon)

The spine's governing metaphor already says what the article's first
half says: drones are "our longbows — the people's weapon:
democratised, cheap, effective en masse… not decisive"; the cannon is
"the full-stack, AI-automated state," assembled only by those who can
tax at scale. Krejsa and Benich add two things the metaphor does not
yet contain, and both are useful.

**(a) The longbow and the cannon now share one substrate.** In the
fifteenth century the longbow (yew, a village craft) and the cannon
(bronze, saltpetre, a fisc) sat on different industrial bases; the
democratised weapon owed nothing to the concentrated one. Here the
democratised weapon is built from the same batteries, motors and
compute as the data centre and the grid: "A very small tech stack has
suddenly become central to the home front and battlefield alike." So
the longbow of the present is downstream of a cannon-like base. This
strengthens the book's claim rather than weakening it: democratisation
at the point of use rests on concentration at the point of supply, and
the polity that holds the supply layer holds the switch over the
"people's weapon" too. It is also a cleaner statement of the layer
argument the Bratton assessment reached (research/bratton-assessment.md
§4.2–4.3): control is not over the packets or the drones but over the
reservoir. The article names the reservoir: watts.

**(b) The candidate for "decisive layer" is wider than compute.** The
spine and ch12's mirror table (ch12/american-pattern.md TABLE 2) run
the mechanism through compute, fabs, constellations, munitions, models,
identity. Krejsa and Benich argue that electricity generation, storage
and direction is the master metric beneath all of them — chips are
"electricity into tokens"; China's answer to a chip deficit is "ten
times more electricity"; data centres depend on batteries and power
electronics to sit on an aging grid. If they are right, the decisive
stack the book must name has an Earth/energy layer at its base (which
is where Bratton's 2026 piece also put the contest: minerals and
"secure as much electricity as possible"). The consequence for the
book is a decision, not a correction: does ch12 state the decisive
stack as compute-led with energy as an input, or as energy-led with
compute as the most valuable conversion of it? The article is the
best short T3 statement of the second position and should be engaged
as such.

## 3. Where it hands the book evidence for its own mechanism

- **Civilian scale as the modern fisc.** "Detroit became the Arsenal
  of Democracy only after selling generations of cars to families
  first." The fifteenth-century state bought its cannon from a fisc;
  the article says the present state cannot buy its base at Pentagon
  scale at all — it must cultivate a civilian market large enough to
  carry the industrial reserve. That is a genuine disanalogy with 1450
  and the book should state it: the taille paid for the Bureau
  brothers' train directly; the modern equivalent of the taille is
  partly a tax credit that shapes private demand. It belongs beside
  the capital-intensity passage (ch10 §VIII) and in Appendix C as an
  indicator class (see §5).
- **Estates politics, again.** The IRA is the article's Orléans 1439
  told as failure: a grant of permanence to an industrial base,
  repealed because the banner it flew ("climate") lost the Estates,
  while the CHIPS Act survived because "the coalition that passed it
  was so broad" and was framed as security against Beijing. The
  fiscal-settlement probe (ch10/fiscal-settlement.md) makes the
  Estates' consent the hinge; this is the contemporary case of consent
  withheld and consent given for the same kind of expenditure under
  different names. Ch11/coda material.
- **The switch at component level.** "Smart-to-dumb": risk lives in
  "the digitally active control layers… the software that governs
  battery systems or powers navigation computers," where an adversary
  can act "in a conflict's opening hours." This is the power-switch
  test (spine §8(c); the playbook's test; Bratton's tax-flows
  sentence) applied to a battery management system. Whoever writes the
  control layer holds the off-switch for the drone fleet, and the
  policy instruments the authors describe — component bans, licences
  to use foreign battery technology — are the licensing/revocation
  instruments the book predicts, already in use one layer down.
- **China as the polity that keeps the riverbed.** "Using
  authoritarian resolve to marshal watts, while the United States is
  stuck in a never-ending cycle of permitting hearings" — spine §5's
  ranking (China closest to Tudor norms) stated by two American
  officials in the energy register. Evidence, not authority: the
  claim about China's annual grid additions needs an IEA or NBS
  primary.

## 4. Where it cuts against the book, to be answered rather than smoothed

1. **Ukraine as the walls that held.** The article opens with the
   democratised weapon "allowing a country to hold off an invader many
   times its size." The book's fifteenth century is the moment walls
   stopped holding; its present is a moment when cheap defence is
   holding. The answer the book already has (spine §4, the Economist
   essay of 28 May 2026 — "easier to start, harder to win") is that
   this is the pre-artillery condition, the longbow's hour, and that
   decisiveness has not yet been purchased by anyone. The article
   supplies the industrial half of that answer: the hour is being
   consolidated from below even as it is democratised from above.
   Ch12 should say both halves together.
2. **The decisive layer may not be the one the book has been
   describing.** If watts are the master metric, then the
   consolidator is whoever can add generation fastest, and that is a
   permitting-and-construction question more than a platform-control
   question. The over-mighty-citizen pattern (spine §8(c)) is about
   severing private control of a stack layer; the article's problem is
   a state that cannot build. The two are compatible — Alcoa's remedy
   was public capacity (american-pattern.md §9) — but the book must
   not let "consolidation" mean only "discipline the platforms." Part
   of the Tudor turn is the state building.
3. **The article's own prescription is a hybrid, not a Tudor turn.**
   Licensing foreign (Chinese) battery technology, accepting Chinese
   "dumb" components, tax credits for consumer batteries — this is
   coexistence-with-dependency managed by risk tiering, not
   re-subordination. Spine §8(b) already predicts a hybrid stated per
   bloc; this is what the American hybrid looks like at the energy
   layer as two insiders describe it. It should be recorded as an
   observed baseline, not argued away.

  energy/electrotech row — batteries, power electronics, grid

  present is built from the cannon's materials — democratised in use,
  concentrated in supply. (Roderick's document; proposal only.)

  disanalogy, stated as such.

  the same kind of grant, consent turning on the banner.

  (appendix-c/memo.md CA-series, DRAFT): (i) whether an all-purpose
  battery/electrotech demand instrument is enacted under a security
  rather than climate banner; (ii) whether component controls move
  from "Made in China" blanket bans to control-layer ("smart")
  licensing — the article predicts both and both are scoreable.

  "democratisation driven by consolidation" formulation as the
