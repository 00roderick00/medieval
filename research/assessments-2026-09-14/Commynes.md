## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Tome III is Commynes's account, as participant, of Charles VIII's Italian expedition of 1494–95 and its aftermath to the king's death in 1498. He opens by declaring his standing as witness: "Pour continuer les memoires encommancés, vous vueil dire comme advint que le roy Charles VIII, de present regnant, entreprint son voyage d'Ytalie, auquel je fuz" — to continue the memoirs begun, I wish to tell you how it came about that King Charles VIII, presently reigning, undertook his voyage to Italy, on which I was present [TRANS. CLAUDE] (printed p. 1 / PDF p. 7). His governing judgement of the enterprise is that it succeeded against all institutional reason: "l'emprise sembloit à tous gens saiges et experimentéz très deraisonnable" — the enterprise seemed to all wise and experienced people most unreasonable [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 2 / PDF 8) — and, of its success, "c'estoit chose impossible aux gens qui le guidoient, s'il ne fust venu de Dieu seul" — it was a thing impossible to the men who guided it, had it not come from God alone [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 81 / PDF 87). His explanatory frame is providential and moral, not institutional.

Yet inside that frame he supplies the century's most quotable primary testimony to what the French state's instrument actually was. On the army's superiority: "ilz n'entendoient point le faict de l'artillerie, et en France n'avoit jamais esté si bien entendue" — they [the Italians] understood nothing of the business of artillery, and in France it had never been so well understood [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 50 / PDF 56); and, in 1497, "nostre artillerie, qui, à la verité, passe toutes les artilleries du monde" — our artillery, which, in truth, surpasses all the artilleries of the world [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 285 / PDF 291). The volume is T1 primary in a scholarly edition; Calmette's apparatus (variants against the Dupont, Sauvage, Lenglet and Mandrot editions; notes anchored to Delaborde, Pélicier's *Lettres de Charles VIII*, Sanudo, Burchard) is T2 riding beneath it.

## 3. What it CORRECTS

- **The Estates of Tours figure (ch10 draft §III).** The draft says the 1484 Estates granted the taille "for two years only and cut to some 1.5 million" (ch10/draft.md:201–202, carried at tertiary level and flagged). Commynes, a participant, states the Tours grant as **1.2 million francs of taille**: Charles VIII in 1498 resolved "qu'il ne levast sur son peuple que douze cens mil frans et par forme de taille, oultre son domayne, qui estoit la somme que les trois Estatz luy avoyent accordée à Tours lorsqu'il fut roy" — that he would levy on his people no more than twelve hundred thousand francs, and that by way of taille, beyond his domain — which was the sum the three Estates had granted him at Tours when he became king [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 304 / PDF 310). Not a flat contradiction — the draft's 1.5M presumably includes the additional two-year grant above the taille proper — but the T1 witness says 1.2M *par forme de taille*, and the renovation should reconcile the two and pin the primary.
- **Nothing else.** No date, name or sequence in the manuscript's Commynes-adjacent passages is contradicted by tome III. For discipline: the draft's "more than thirty-six pieces" entering Rome (Giovio via Contamine) and Commynes's "quatorze piéces de grosse artillerie" at the Apennine recrossing (p. 161 / PDF 167) are different moments and different counting categories, not a conflict. The Additions' tome II items are editorial minutiae (e.g. "P. 33, n. 2 … lire : vingt écus, et non : vingt mille écus", p. 319 / PDF 325 — a salutary caution about figures in the older apparatus), none touching project passages.

## 4. What it ADDS

The manuscript's 1494 demonstration (ch10 §IV close, draft lines 321–338) currently rests entirely on Contamine carrying Giovio. Tome III supplies the T1 eyewitness layer it lacks:

- **Italian defencelessness, in the participant's words.** "Ilz n'entendoient point le faict de l'artillerie, et en France n'avoit jamais esté si bien entendue" (p. 50 / PDF 56). Mordano fell though "ne fut batue que demy jour" — battered only half a day [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 50). Monte San Giovanni, "une très forte place" (p. 91 / PDF 97), "fut batue sept ou huyt heures et puis prinse d'assault et tout tué ce qui estoit dedans, ou la pluspart" — was battered seven or eight hours and then taken by assault, and all within killed, or most [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 92 / PDF 98). The summary verdict: "hors le chasteau de Napples, n'y eut nul qui empeschast le roy ung jour naturel" — apart from the castle of Naples, there was none that delayed the king one natural day [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 81 / PDF 87). These are ch06's siege-clock (the *délai de secours* collapsed to hours) restated in Italy by a T1 witness — they belong at ch10 §IV and as a back-reference from ch06.
- **The chalk line, at source.** "Comme a dit ce pape Alexandre qui régne, les Françoys y sont alléz avecques des esperons de boys et de la craye en la main des fourriers pour marcher leurs logis, sans aultre peyne" — as the reigning Pope Alexander has said, the French went there with wooden spurs, and chalk in the hands of their quartermasters to mark their lodgings, without other trouble [TRANS. CLAUDE] (pp. 81–82 / PDF 87–88). The most famous contemporary verdict on 1494, and its actual T1 carrier is this passage.
- **Campaign speed, quantified by the witness.** "Ne mist le roy, depuis Ast à entrer dedans Napples, que quatre moys, dix et neuf jours. Ung ambassadeur en y eust mis une partie" — the king took, from Asti to his entry into Naples, only four months and nineteen days. An ambassador would have used a good part of that [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 82 / PDF 88).
- **The train as apparatus.** The Apennine recrossing (Livre VIII ch. VII): fourteen pieces of heavy artillery hauled over trackless mountains by the German infantry roped in teams of one and two hundred (pp. 160–161 / PDF 166–167), "et ne se perdit une seulle pierre ne une livre de pouldre. Et croy que jamais homme ne vit passer artillerie de telle grosseur ne à telle diligence par les lieux où passa ceste-cy" — and not a single stone-shot nor a pound of powder was lost; and I believe no man ever saw artillery of such size pass with such diligence through such places [TRANS. CLAUDE] (pp. 210–211 / PDF 216–217). Logistics as the machine's signature — pairs exactly with the draft's 1477 Artois accounts paragraph.
- **The financing of the expedition — for ch10's honesty and ch04's Italy chapter.** "Nul argent content : car, avant que partir, empruntérent cent mil francs du banc de Soly à Gennes, à quatorze pour cent d'interest de foyre en foyre" — no ready money: for before leaving they borrowed one hundred thousand francs from the Sauli bank at Genoa, at fourteen per cent interest from fair to fair [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 3 / PDF 9); 50,000 ducats from a Milan merchant on pledges, Commynes himself standing surety for 6,000 (p. 35 / PDF 41); the jewels of Madame de Savoie and of the marquise de Montferrat pawned for 12,000 ducats each — "Et pouvez veoir quel commencement de guerre, si Dieu n'eust guidé l'euvre!" — and you may see what a beginning of a war, had God not guided the work! [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 37 / PDF 43); the fleet "cousta trois cens mil francs et ne servit de riens" (p. 32 / PDF 38); 500,000 francs to settle the mutinous Swiss at Vercelli (p. 245 / PDF 251).
- **The revenue figure (task 3): the 1.8M→4.7M arc is NOT in tome III.** Commynes's statement of crown revenues under Charles VII against Louis XI's death — the ledger entry currently carried via Vale — belongs to the fiscal discourse of Livres V–VI = **Calmette tome II; it awaits tome II's retrieval**, and the Additions do not touch it. But tome III supplies the arc's *later endpoints*, T1: the Tours 1484 taille grant of 1.2M (above), the domain "passe ung milion de francs", and the 1498 actual — "le peuple, qui paie au jour d'uy plus de deux milions et demy de francs de taille" — the people, who today pay more than two and a half million francs of taille [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 304 / PDF 310). This is the draft's "control experiment" (the machine absorbed the 1484 cut and rebounded) proven from the primary: 1.2M granted in 1484; over 2.5M levied by 1498.
- **Calmette's apparatus (task 4), T2.** Full variant collation against Dupont, Sauvage, Lenglet and B. de Mandrot on every page (the drafts' older-edition readings can now be checked, e.g. the Dupont mislections recorded at pp. 50, 99–100); notes anchored to Delaborde's *L'expédition de Charles VIII en Italie*, Pélicier's *Lettres de Charles VIII*, Sanudo and Burchard — a ready-made T2 retrieval ladder for 1494; the consolidated index to all three tomes (pp. 320–437), which locates tome I–II passages (Montlhéry, Bedford, the Burgundian artillery) in advance of their retrieval; and the Perroy 1925 Montlhéry reference (§2, item 1).

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

At full strength, two things:

1. **Fornovo: the guns did almost nothing.** "Je ne croy point que l'artillerie des deux costéz tuast dix hommes et ne dura point le combat ung quart d'heure" — I do not believe the artillery of the two sides killed ten men, and the fight did not last a quarter of an hour [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 192 / PDF 198; Calmette's n. 3 reports Mandrot's explanation: the storm had wetted the powder). The first French field trial of the invincible train, and its eyewitness says it killed fewer than ten men. Anyone who reads the 1494 demonstration as artillery winning battles has Commynes against him.
2. **The expedition as improvisation, not machine-output.** Commynes's sustained theme is that the enterprise lacked money, sense and order — "toutes choses necessaires à une si grand emprise failloient" — all things necessary to so great an enterprise were lacking [TRANS. CLAUDE] (p. 3 / PDF 9) — financed by pawned jewels and 14 per cent Genoese credit, and explicable only as God's punishment of the Aragonese. His own frame for 1494 is providential; he nowhere credits the fiscal-administrative apparatus the manuscript's mechanism runs on.

**The book's best answer.** To (1): the manuscript's claim was never that cannon win field battles — ch06's whole architecture (siege speed versus campaign speed; the *délai de secours*) locates decisiveness at the walls, and tome III itself supplies the confirming series: Mordano in half a day, Monte San Giovanni in eight hours, nothing but Naples's castle costing "ung jour naturel". Fornovo the field battle was decided by lance and mêlée; Italy the campaign was decided by what walls no longer did. Stated with the wet-powder note and the <10-dead line *quoted*, this becomes a disanalogy the chapter carries proudly (CLAUDE.md §6), and the answer is good. To (2): the standing train — fourteen great pieces moved without losing "une seulle pierre ne une livre de pouldre" — and the compagnies were the machine's product; the expeditionary treasury's chaos shows the difference between standing capability and campaign cash-flow, and the crown's very ability to borrow rested on a 2.5M-franc taille. That answer is adequate but must be made honestly: the witness's own explanation is God, not the généralités, and the chapter should say so — the mechanism is the historian's frame imposed on, not found in, Commynes. Neither point reaches a spine §8 ruling or a DEFEND-tier claim; both discipline the register of ch10 §IV.

## 7. Verdict

Tome III changes the weight, not the direction: it converts the manuscript's 1494 demonstration from a Contamine-carried summary into eyewitness T1 — chalk, eight-hour sieges, an artillery "surpassing all the artilleries of the world" — while verifying none of the Commynes passages the drafts actually cite, all of which sit in tomes I–II and rightly remain on their Scoble pins. The single most important thing to know: Commynes hands the book both its best Italian evidence and its best discipline in the same volume — at Fornovo the guns killed fewer than ten men, so the 1494 chapter must locate decisiveness at the walls and the exchequer, never on the field, and must say plainly that the mechanism is the book's frame, not the witness's. Retrieve Calmette tomes I–II next; until then the p. 304 fiscal passage (1.2M granted, 2.5M paid) is the one revenue figure the project can carry at T1.
