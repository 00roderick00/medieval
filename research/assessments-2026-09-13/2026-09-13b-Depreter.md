# Assessment — Michael Depreter, *De Gavre à Nancy (1453–1477): l'artillerie bourguignonne sur la voie de la « modernité »* (Turnhout: Brepols, Burgundica XVIII, 2011)

Read: the whole book (Introduction, Parts I–III, Conclusion générale, Glossary, Annexes 1–6, Tableaux 1–3); not the bibliography (pp. 187–208) or index. Printed page = PDF page − 12 throughout the body. Pins give printed page first. The OCR is clean apart from soft-hyphen breaks inside words; no quotation used here is affected. All translations are [TRANS. CLAUDE].

Corpus note: this is the monograph (a revised *mémoire de maîtrise*, dir. de Waha and Cauchies). It cites the manuscript's existing "Depreter 2011" — the BCRH article "L'artillerie de Charles le Hardi (1467–1477)" — throughout as *à paraître*, by edited piece number (pièces n°1–17). The monograph is the argument; the article is the document edition.

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Depreter's subject is not the guns but the men and paperwork behind them: administration (Part I: master, controller, receiver), personnel (Part II: gunners, engineers, logistics), and where the pieces came from (Part III: ducal arsenals against borrowing from towns and nobles), from the Ghent war to Charles the Bold's death. Two turning-points organise it: "la guerre contre Gand, qui vit une première ébauche de professionnalisation" and "Le règne de Charles de Bourgogne [qui] marqua le deuxième tournant. Encore comte de Charolais, ce prince mit en place les bases d'une véritable professionnalisation et spécialisation du personnel de l'artillerie" (p. 154 / PDF 166) [the Ghent war, which saw a first sketch of professionalisation … The reign of Charles of Burgundy marked the second turning-point. While still count of Charolais, this prince laid the foundations of a genuine professionalisation and specialisation of the artillery personnel]. The institutional core is the separate artillery receivership: "en 1458, précédant le roi de France et le duc de Bretagne, Philippe le Bon institue un receveur particulier chargé du matériel et des dépenses de l'artillerie. C'était là une étape importante dans la conquête de l'autonomie financière indispensable au bon fonctionnement de l'arme" (p. 43 / PDF 55) [in 1458, ahead of the king of France and the duke of Brittany, Philip the Good instituted a special receiver charged with the matériel and expenditure of the artillery. This was an important step in the conquest of the financial autonomy indispensable to the proper functioning of the arm].

His verdict is deliberately double. On organisation: "l'artillerie bourguignonne pouvait donc bien, sans doute, être considérée comme « la meilleure d'Europe », ou du moins l'égale de celle des rois de France" (p. 183 / PDF 195) [the Burgundian artillery could indeed be considered "the best in Europe", or at least the equal of the French kings']. On administration: "Il semble que les malversations aient été légion dans les années 1470 … à l'époque du second siège de Nancy, il semble que le charroi fut en partie retardé pour des questions d'ordre administratif et financier, alors même que la demande de renforts se fit pressante. C'était là sans doute un élément qui a pesé lourd lors de la bataille finale" (p. 182 / PDF 194) [Embezzlement seems to have been legion in the 1470s … at the second siege of Nancy the wagon-train seems to have been partly delayed for administrative and financial reasons, just as the demand for reinforcements became pressing. That no doubt weighed heavily in the final battle]. He ends on a question he declines to answer: "Ne serait-ce pas à ce moment que la France prit une avance considérable? … C'est là une autre histoire, qui reste encore largement à écrire" (p. 184 / PDF 196).

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**(a) The recette de l'artillerie of 1458, audited at Lille.** Manuscript (ch10 §VI): "Burgundy instituted a dedicated *recette de l'artillerie* in 1458, its accounts auditable at the Chambre des comptes of Lille". Depreter: "Le 9 septembre 1458 vit le jour l'office de receveur de l'artillerie" (p. 35 / PDF 47); the receiver "tenu de rendre annuellement (son) compte à la Chambre de comptes de Lille, qui vérifiait … l'existence, l'exactitude et l'exigibilité des dépenses" (pp. 38–39 / PDF 50–51). Nuance: audit passed to Malines "entre 1473 et 1477" (p. 3 / PDF 15).

**(b) Estimates through correcting hands; the duke as ordonnateur.** Manuscript: "its budget estimates passing through multiple correcting hands". Depreter: états "soumis au prince, puis à son conseil des finances" (p. 38 / PDF 50); "Comme en atteste sa signature sur un nombre de pièces soumises à son approbation, Charles le Hardi prit particulièrement à cœur son rôle d'ordonnateur principal" (p. 10 / PDF 22). See §3 on the Neuss-état attribution.

**(c) Burgundy as rival consolidator, not subject (ch06 §VIII).** Depreter's Part III: "Sous Charles le Hardi, l'emprunt se raréfie encore. C'était là certainement un atout politique dans la mesure où les ducs n'avaient plus à composer, en cette matière, avec d'autres forces politiques, surtout urbaines" (p. 179 / PDF 191); "une indépendance presque totale vis-à-vis des villes et de la noblesse" (p. 182 / PDF 194); Bruges lent the duke no cannon at all 1450–77, and Namur in 1465 borrowed from the ducal park to defend itself (p. 171 / PDF 183). Suppression of intermediaries documented at the level of gun-loans.

**(d) The 1460s as the moment of the mobile bronze system (ch06 §II).** Manuscript: "fixed in their mature form by about 1465." Depreter: "aucun affût sur roues n'est signalé dans les comptes … entre 1450 et 1458, alors que les mentions en sont légion dès la seconde moitié de la décennie 1460" (p. 90 n. 229 / PDF 102). The 9 January 1467 Lambillon contract is the T1 text: refit five great iron serpentines "et a y asseoir deux tourillons pour les mectre en ung affust a mener sur deux roes" (p. 11 / PDF 23) [and set two trunnions on them to mount them on a carriage drawn on two wheels].

**(e) Scale of the train (ch10 §VI, La Marche's "more than 300 carts").** From the accounts rather than the chronicle: an état of c. 1467–70 totals "319 chariots 1 quart" (Annexe 3, p. 213 / PDF 225); "vers 1467, 1 188 chevaux … en 1473, l'on comptait 2 894 chevaux en Gueldre … En 1475 … jusqu'à 4 788 chevaux" (p. 146 / PDF 158).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

1. **Manuscript (ch10 §VI, n. 10-30) cites "Depreter 2011: 83" for the Revolution-cartridges anecdote.** In the monograph it is p. 2 (PDF 14): "Un providentiel transfert à Bruxelles en 1769 le préserva de la furie révolutionnaire qui récupéra les anciens comptes pour la confection de ses gargousses de poudre." If "Depreter 2011" is the monograph the pin is wrong; if the article, sources.md must distinguish the two. "All but one … destroyed" overstates: one account survives "dans sa totalité" (1473) and partial accounts survive (e.g. p. 51 n. 17); and "for the revolutionary army" is not in Depreter — only "gargousses de poudre".

2. **Manuscript (n. 10-27): "the état for the Neuss campaign of November 1474 bears Charles's own signature".** The monograph cites that état (ADN B 3519 n°124220, pièce n°15) a dozen times without mentioning a signature; the remark at p. 10 is general ("sur un nombre de pièces"). Pin to the article's edition or soften.

3. **Manuscript (n. 10-29): the post-Morat inventory "repeatedly corrected for lack of means".** Depreter: the July 1476 état "commence par un récapitulatif inédit de l'artillerie à disposition du duc"; serpentines "se prendront d'enprunt car il en y a trop peu" (p. 171 / PDF 183). The downward revisions (four bombards to two, 400 pioneers to 30) are at pp. 99, 116, 135; the financial reason is posed as a question (p. 117 / PDF 129), not asserted. The phrase should not stand in quotation marks against Depreter.

4. **Manuscript (ch10 §VI): "By every measurable input — organisation, guns, accounts, pay — Burgundy was running the artillery state's programme, and running parts of it better than France."** On pay Depreter is two-sided: wartime bombardiers at 20 francs/month outpaced the French, but in peace ducal ordinary gunners drew 2–3 sous a day against the king's "moyenne annuelle de 112 livres tournois, soit environ 6 sous par jour en 1469" (p. 72 / PDF 84); on numbers, "En 1469, [Louis XI] pouvait déjà compter sur quarante canonniers ordinaires … Le duc de Bourgogne réussit-il finalement à combler ce retard sur son cousin français? Les sources ne permettent pas de l'affirmer avec certitude" (p. 73 / PDF 85). Confine "better than France" to the recette (1458 v. 1464) and wartime gunner pay.

5. **Manuscript (ch10 §VI): Burgundy's fall "transferred the whole apparatus intact — lands, revenues, recette and guns — into the French and Habsburg fiscs."** Depreter cannot carry "intact" or "guns": "Les pertes en matériel à Grandson comme à Morat furent sans doute catastrophiques" (p. 183); "Les arsenaux ducaux étant quasi vidés" (p. 184 / PDF 196). Menostey was gaoled 15 January 1477 and dismissed with a 2,400 lb. fine (pp. 40–41); what passed to Mary and Maximilian was the office structure (Jehan Charles "resta en fonction après la mort du Hardi", p. 33; Dommarien master again in 1478, p. 22 n. 94), not a park. Nothing in Depreter bears on the French share.

6. **Minor.** For the manuscript's "ordinance labelling … to pin against Vaughan": Depreter gives Abbeville 31 July 1471, Bohain 13 November 1472, Saint-Maximin autumn 1473, with editions (p. 4 n. 11 / PDF 16), and notes the ordinances say almost nothing about artillery: "Sur le terrain, l'expérimentation a sans doute précédé la législation" (p. 4).

## 4. What it ADDS

**(i) The archive's own voice on the technical threshold (ch06 §VI, ch10 §III–IV).** The c. 1470 état: "ladite artillerie est tres petitement pourvueue de bons canonniers, qui ne sont pas a recouvrer de legier … car combien que ladite artillerie soit bien et largement pourvueue de bons bastons, touteffois sans avoir gens qui s'en sacent aidier, c'est tres pou de chose" (p. 69 / PDF 81) [the artillery is very poorly provided with good gunners, who are not easy to come by … for although it is well and amply provided with good guns, without people who know how to use them it is very little thing]. The memorandum that followed proposed a minimum of "trente « bons canonniers, qui sera ung bon commenchement »" (p. 70). The book's argument that the decisive capability is a salaried corps, not the metal — stated by a Burgundian clerk in 1470.

**(ii) Cost curve for Appendix B and ch06.** Artillery expenditure "passant de 9 000 livres pour l'année 1465 à 55 000 livres lors de l'année 1473, atteignant même un sommet de 80 000 livres à l'occasion du siège de Neuss" (p. 40 / PDF 52). Forecast monthly charroi: Liège 1467/8, 8,910 lb.; Gueldre 1473, 17,364; Lorraine 1475, 28,728; Lorraine 1476/7, 7,176 (Tableau 2, p. 219 / PDF 231; personnel in Tableau 3). Gunners 40 (1453) → 86 (1473) → 102 (1476) (Tableau 1). Pioneers 134 in 1453, 1,000 forecast for 1473, c. 1,300 at Neuss, 30 for the last Nancy campaign (pp. 113, 116). The only serial data comparable to the French 1489 figures in ch10 §IV.

**(iii) The Nancy dossier (ch10 §VI).** The chancellor's letter of 2 November 1476 (Annexe 5): the Estates of Brabant and Flanders appealed against the charroi assessment ("non obstant leur contredit et appel"); Flanders would deliver two-thirds "ou de paier en argent 10 livres de gros pour chacun chariot"; the receiver pleaded "faulte d'argent" (pp. 215–16 / PDF 227–28). Dommarien from Metz, 15 December 1476: "tout le monde prent du notre a toutes moins, a cause que voient que les gens de monsseigneur l'aubandonnent par povretés … le grant besoing qu'il a principallemant de poudre et les bonbardes sejournent par faulte" (p. 217 / PDF 229) [everyone takes ours at any price, because they see my lord's people abandoning him out of poverty … the great need he has chiefly of powder, and the bombards lie idle for want]. "À la mi-décembre, Charles le Hardi n'était donc toujours pas en force devant Nancy. Or, le duc était sur place depuis le 22 octobre!" (p. 151 / PDF 163). No Liège miners were brought to Nancy (pp. 110–11).

**(iv) The efficiency-versus-control trade-off (ch10 §III–IV).** "À deux reprises au moins, le duc intervint pour réduire les modalités de contrôle … Le financier ne risquait-il pas de prendre le pas sur le militaire?" (p. 43 / PDF 55). Distribution rolls were abolished after 1472; Menostey's fraud, exposed by the controller in 1472, went unpunished until 1477 (pp. 30, 40–41, 44). The 1466 ordinance abolishing the master's twentieth penny on contracts, "sans promesse, paction ou fraulde quelxconques" (pp. 11–12), is a clean T1 case of the centre disciplining its own officers.

**(v) Ducal independence from towns and nobles as a technical as well as political fact (ch08/ch10).** Town artillery was refused on loyalty (Calais 1436), on political hostage-taking ("celles-ci puissent demander des contreparties", p. 172) and on obsolescence and calibre (p. 173); the nobility likewise: "la noblesse était-elle réellement en mesure d'entretenir sur le long terme un nombre conséquent de canonniers …?" (p. 177 / PDF 189). Suppression of intermediaries with the reason stated as capital cost.

**(vi) Primary colour.** Haynin on Montlhéry: the French fled "a cause de la grant hideur et fureur que les serpentines du conte de Charolois avoite fet sur eus" (pp. 58–59 / PDF 70–71) — a Burgundian counter-witness to DeVries's "ten salvos" at ch10 §V. Mons paid gunners' wives 30 sous a week during the Nancy siege, stopped when "sondit mary estoit lors et auparavant trespasset en laditte armee" (p. 79 / PDF 91).

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**The attribution itself.** The manuscript calls its finding "the Depreter refinement": "What Burgundy lacked … was not administrative capacity — Depreter's work shows the capacity in detail — but a *survivable dynastic centre*". Depreter says nothing about a dynastic centre; the phrase and the inference are the book's. The inference may stand, but the label must change in ch10, ch11, ch12 and Appendix C (CLAUDE.md §4: (c) must not masquerade as (a)).

**The substance, at full strength.** Depreter's diagnosis of 1476–77 is the opposite of "apparatus complete, centre missing". He locates the failure in the fiscal-administrative leg: "le charroi fut en partie retardé pour des questions d'ordre administratif et financier … un élément qui a pesé lourd lors de la bataille finale" (p. 182). The Estates of Flanders and Brabant appealed against the carriage levy and Flanders bought out a third of it (Annexe 5); the receiver was an embezzler tolerated because "l'efficacité primant et le prévaricateur bénéficiant en quelque sorte de la « complicité du 'système' »" (p. 44); the duke's men "l'aubandonnent par povretés" (Annexe 6). The corps was still being built — "encore timide, certes, mais bien réelle" (p. 154) — and had no stable bandes. Morat destroyed what could not be replaced: "on ne remplace pas du jour au lendemain des techniciens de l'artillerie rodés … et il était également extrêmement difficile de reconstituer un parc d'artillerie d'envergure à court terme" (p. 184). On this reading Burgundy did not fail *despite* having fisc and administration; it failed where the fisc met the Estates and the administration met its own corruption — the mechanism, not an exception to it.

This reaches the DEFEND-tier mechanism (spine §6) only in that the present handling of the counter-case weakens it; it reaches no §8 ruling. It does touch spine §5 and §7, where the "Depreter test" scores the EU as "apparatus without the centre": if the Burgundian apparatus was itself fiscally contested and administratively porous, the EU analogy carries a second variable the manuscript has not named.

**The book's best answer.** Concede the label and re-state the refinement in the form Depreter's evidence supports: Burgundy had the *offices* of the artillery state — recette, contrôle, maîtrise, arsenals, ordinary gunners — six years ahead of France, but not the *fiscal permanence* behind them: the charroi still rested on assessed corvée the Estates could appeal, the receiver's fraud went unpunished for five years, one season's losses could not be re-funded. What killed Burgundy at Nancy was a compound of the missing centre (one mortal duke) *and* a fisc not yet irreversible — the ratchet of spine §8(a) had not locked. This is the better answer: it makes Burgundy the mechanism running incomplete rather than a case the mechanism cannot explain, and it keeps the survivable-centre point (the offices outlived the duke: Jehan Charles, Dommarien under Maximilian) without claiming Depreter said it. Honestly: the answer is good, but it costs the manuscript its cleanest sentence ("apparatus without a centre") and obliges ch11/ch12 and Appendix C to carry two variables for the EU test instead of one. Whether that sharpens or blurs the EU score is Roderick's call.

**A smaller contradiction.** Depreter refuses any teleological verdict for the French system: "le temps était encore à l'expérimentation, du côté français comme du côté bourguignon" (p. 182); "En aucun cas n'oserions-nous donc affirmer … que le duc de Bourgogne devait « nécessairement » être vaincu" (p. 88). He lists fifteen sieges won, Dinant 1466 to Grandson 1476 (p. 183), against the four famous failures. The manuscript's "It lost anyway" must not read as "its artillery did not work".

## 6. Placement

- **ch10 §VI (Burgundy counter-case): T2, engaged BY NAME in the text.** Replace "Depreter's work shows the capacity in detail" with the evidence (recette, p. 35; the 1470 memorandum, pp. 69–71; Part III, pp. 169–79) and add his contrary diagnosis (pp. 182–84; Annexes 5–6) as the objection the section answers. Re-label "the Depreter refinement" as the book's own.
- **ch10 §III–IV (the audited department): T2 evidence, in-text.** Controller/contrerôle (pp. 27–31), the 1466 anti-fraud ordinance (pp. 11–12), efficiency-versus-control (pp. 43–44). Rival, not ally: the same anatomy as Contamine's 1489 French department, a generation earlier and leakier.
- **ch06 §VI–VIII: footnote.** Wheeled-carriage chronology (p. 90 n. 229; Lambillon, p. 11); cost curve 9,000 → 55,000 → 80,000 lb. (p. 40); the "rival consolidator" line.
- **ch08: footnote.** Town and noble artillery refused on cost and loyalty grounds (pp. 172–73, 177).
- **Appendix B: T2 evidence row.** Tableaux 1–3 (p. 219), the only serial personnel/charroi cost data for a non-French polity; enter as forecasts, "avec correction des erreurs de calcul (ou essais de prévarication?)".
- **ch11/ch12 and Appendix C ("Depreter test"): rename; footnote to ch10.** Depreter is the evidentiary base, not the author.

## 7. Verdict

It changes the register of the Burgundy counter-case, not the book's direction: Depreter confirms the apparatus (recette, controller, ordinary gunners, ducal monopoly of the guns against towns and nobles) but denies the manuscript's premise that the apparatus was complete and fiscally sound, locating the Nancy failure in a contested charroi levy, an unpunished embezzling receiver and unreplaceable losses — that is, in the fisc and the administration, not only in the dead duke. The single most important thing the author should know is that "the Depreter refinement" is not Depreter's: he never mentions a dynastic centre, and his own evidence supports a two-variable reading (offices without fiscal permanence, plus one mortal node) that fits the ratchet of spine §8(a) better than the present one-variable sentence. Weight: heavy for ch10 §VI and Appendix B; the cost series and the 1470 gunners memorandum are the best new material.
