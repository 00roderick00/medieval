# Assessment — Yanis Varoufakis, *Technofeudalism: What Killed Capitalism* (2023)

*Read whole (~80k words, all seven chapters, both appendices, the notes; index skimmed only). Text file note: the pdftotext capture carries no running heads, so printed page numbers are not recoverable; every pin below is a PDF page (1 + form-feeds). The book's own internal cross-references (Peel at "pp. 60–61", the cloud-fiefs section at "pp. 85–88", Figure 3 "on p. 234") do not map to the PDF pagination by a constant offset, so the file is evidently an e-book conversion rather than the print edition. No OCR garbling found; the text is clean throughout. Not on the project's ledger; added by Roderick.*

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Varoufakis argues that capitalism has ended — not been reformed or intensified but replaced — and that what has replaced it is a rent-based order run by the owners of a new species of capital. The thesis sentence is in the Preface: "capitalism is now dead, in the sense that its dynamics no longer govern our economies. In that role it has been replaced by something fundamentally different, which I call technofeudalism" (PDF p. 8). Two named causes — the privatisation of the internet by American and Chinese Big Tech, and the post-2008 central-bank response (PDF p. 9) — and one structural claim: "Markets, the medium of capitalism, have been replaced by digital trading platforms which look like, but are not, markets, and are better understood as fiefdoms. And profit, the engine of capitalism, has been replaced with its feudal predecessor: rent" — "I call it cloud rent" (PDF p. 9). The class map follows: traditional capitalists "have become vassals in relation to a new class of feudal overlord, the owners of cloud capital. As for the rest of us, we have returned to our former status as serfs" (PDF p. 10). Chapters 1–2 are memoir and post-war economic history (Galbraith's technostructure, Bretton Woods, the "Global Minotaur"); Chapter 3 defines cloud capital as "a produced means of behaviour modification" (PDF pp. 153, 199); Chapter 4 argues central-bank money funded the cloudalists; Chapter 5 defends the word; Chapter 6 reads US–China as a war between "two super cloud fiefs"; Chapter 7 offers the *Another Now* blueprint.

The book is the best-known popular statement of the reading Part I exists to refute: platforms as feudal lords. Be exact about its structure, because it is not an analogy book in the sense the project bans. Varoufakis insists on sequence — the enclosure of a commons *precedes* the technology: "each required a comprehensive plunder of a commons, a complicit political class, and only then a marvellous technological breakthrough" (PDF p. 66) — and supplies a formal political economy (Appendix 1) in which the fief replaces the market and rent replaces profit as the dominant income flow (PDF pp. 196, 204). His pendulum swings backward: the ending is a *new mode of production*, not a transitional dispersal. That is CLAUDE.md §1's second error at its strongest, argued from political economy rather than international relations, with a state not undermighty by accident but structurally captured: "a global superhighway to serfdom has been constructed not because Western states were too powerful but because they were too weak" (PDF p. 151).

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

The confirmations are real, and several are more than the manuscript expected from a hostile witness. The book is T3 (serious trade synthesis), so each carries colour and framing weight, never structural support.

*Platforms as institutions, not markets.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §I: "The entities that actually wield the non-territorial claim are not markets. They are *institutions*." Varoufakis reaches the same negation from the other side: "Enter amazon.com and you have exited capitalism… you have entered a realm which can't be thought of as a market, not even a digital one" (PDF p. 80); the cloud fief's "perfect centralisation… renders them a form of cloud fief belonging to whoever owns the algorithm" (PDF pp. 200–01). The manuscript says church; he says fief; both deny the market taxonomy attributed to Friedrichs.

*Expulsion as the sanction.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §III: "deplatforming as excommunication is a structural identity, not a flourish." Varoufakis: "with the removal of a link from their cloud vassal's site they could lose access to the bulk of their customers… A sanitised tech-terror is the bedrock of technofeudalism" (PDF p. 114); Tesla's "power to switch off one of its cars remotely" (PDF p. 82).

*China as the fused church.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §III: "the universal claim is not rival to the state but *absorbed into it*." Varoufakis: China's Big Tech "is directly bound into government agencies" (PDF p. 133); "It was President Xi who placed strict limits on Chinese cloudalists, like Jack Ma, in an explicit bid to keep China's cloud finance within what the party considers to be acceptable bounds – its own" (PDF p. 150; note 26, PDF p. 224). Spine §5's "China is probably closest to current Tudor norms" is confirmed in his own terms.

*The bloc geometry.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §IV: settlements "imposed within and between blocs, not above them." Varoufakis: "the world is dividing into two, mutually antagonistic, super cloud fiefs – one American, the other Chinese" (PDF p. 143). His count is two, not three-or-four — he declares Europe "geostrategically irrelevant" (PDF p. 144), the Depreter conditional stated as a verdict — but the shape converges.

*The 7 October 2022 chip rule.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §V: "investiture at the level of physics." Varoufakis dates the instrument identically and reads its extraterritorial reach the same way (PDF p. 141); the disagreement is over whose interest it serves (below).

*The Apple tribute.* Manuscript Ch. 2 §V frames Epic–Apple as a fight over "tribute terms." Varoufakis names it: "a 30 per cent ground rent, paid to Apple on all their revenues… the first cloud fief: the Apple Store" (PDF p. 111).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

Nothing in the manuscript or the spine is corrected by this book. The traffic runs the other way, and two of Varoufakis's errors touch the manuscript's proof case, so they should be on file before he is engaged by name:

- Varoufakis PDF p. 157 attributes the Standard Oil dissolution to Theodore Roosevelt ("as President Theodore Roosevelt did to Rockefeller when he broke up Standard Oil"). The dissolution was the Supreme Court's decree of 1911 under Taft, as manuscript Ch. 12 §IV correctly states. The manuscript's dating and its account of the decree's sections stand; his does not.
- Varoufakis PDF p. 139 calls the 14 August 2020 digital-yuan roll-out the moment when "For the first time, anywhere, a state had issued a fully digital currency." The e-CNY pilots pre-date that (spring 2020) and other central-bank pilots were running; the manuscript makes no first-mover claim and needs none. Do not import his.
- His Standard Oil description ("petrol stations, refineries and fuel transport systems… Breaking it up into regional oil companies," PDF p. 157) is the compressed telling Ch. 12 §IV warns against; the manuscript's account of the lag (Hepburn 1906 before, proration and interdiction after) is the better one.

## 4. What it ADDS

Prioritised by what the manuscript lacks.

*(i) The identity enclosure — the Kobrin void answered from the other side (Ch. 2 §II; Ch. 11 move five; Ch. 12 §III).* The manuscript says Kobrin "could not see… *which institutions would fill the void*." Varoufakis states what was enclosed: "until fairly recently, our relationship with our identity was mediated and controlled by the state, which held a monopoly on the powerful tokens that legitimise us as rights-holding citizens… And yet, astoundingly, our digital identity belongs neither to us nor to the state" (PDF p. 69) — and frames the enclosure as a *state decision*, alongside the Pentagon's release of GPS: "another political decision by the US government" (PDF p. 71). This is the ground the identity-payments-legibility stack occupies (the Indian stack, e-CNY, the GENIUS Act rails), and it lets move five be stated as *reclamation of a monopoly the state once held and let go*. Strong for the Henrician framing.

*(ii) Internet One as a state artefact (Ch. 12 §III–IV).* "a US government-built and -owned, non-commercial computer network that lay outside capitalist markets… but whose purpose was the defence of the capitalist realm" (PDF p. 67). Pair with Starshield: the reconnaissance and communications layers were state-built, privatised, and are being re-absorbed; the absorption prediction reads as the closing of a loop, and Varoufakis gives its opening.

*(iii) The off-switch from the platform side, and the conceded instrument (Ch. 12 §IV, beside Bratton).* The remote-disabled Tesla (PDF p. 82) and link-removal "tech-terror" (PDF p. 114) are the private discretion the twelve-case pattern exists to sever. Better still, note 8 (PDF pp. 225–26) recalls that telecoms regulators "succeeded in creating a lot more competition… by enforcing interoperability" and adds, "The one thing cloudalists fear is cloud interoperability" — one of the manuscript's twelve instruments, conceded by the book that says nothing can be done. A sceptic's concession of the type the Coda calls the book's signature.

*(iv) The Russian reserves freeze (Ch. 11 move one; Ch. 12 §III).* Absent from the manuscript. "four days after Putin's troops invaded Ukraine, Washington seized more than $300 billion belonging to Russia's central bank and evicted anyone transacting through Russia's central bank from all international payment systems" (PDF p. 138; the Venezuela/Afghanistan qualification at note 10, PDF p. 220). He argues this pushed money on to the yuan rails (PDF p. 140) and motivated the chip war as "a shock-and-awe assault aimed at Chinese cloud finance" (PDF p. 141). For the manuscript: a state exercising the money off-switch at the top of the stack, the rival bloc answering with its own rails — Ch. 11's Side-by-Side reading in the payments layer. Needs a T1 anchor (OFAC directive) before use.

*(v) China's "cloud finance" (Ch. 2 §III; Ch. 12 §IV).* WeChat as messaging, commerce and payments in one app, "the portal to cloud rent" (PDF p. 134), and the party then bounding Ant: a legibility stack privately built and publicly subordinated — the fused church with its history. The Big Five list (PDF p. 133) is his, T3.

*(vi) The Standard Oil objection, verbatim (Ch. 12 §IV).* "how does one break up Amazon, Facebook, PayPal or, indeed, Tesla today?" (PDF p. 157) — the objection the twelve-case series was assembled to answer, to be quoted as such and answered with "regulation of the switch rather than seizure of the property."

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

At full strength, in his words.

*(a) The direction of power.* "we have returned to our former status as serfs" (PDF p. 10); the cloudalists are "a revolutionary class actively displacing the capitalists from the top of society's pecking order" (PDF p. 88). The state is not the coming consolidator but the unwitting paymaster: "It was nothing short of a coup. Imagine getting the world's richest capitalist states to print the money that allows you to build a new type of capital stock" (PDF p. 89). Its instruments are declared spent: price regulation "is irrelevant," breakup "was only possible in the old days of terrestrial capital," and "there is little that government can do to stop them" (PDF p. 157). This reaches spine §6 (hold on balance: platform centralisation) and §8(b)–(c) (the United States will absorb the stack); it does not reach the DEFEND-tier mechanism directly, because Varoufakis never argues about decisive force at all.

*(b) The tense.* A terminus, not a mid-point: "It is through capitalist activity that technofeudalism was born and is now sweeping to power" (PDF p. 114); cloud capital "is here to stay – and to dominate" (PDF p. 125). This is CLAUDE.md §1's second error (dispersal treated as stable), stated as a new mode of production with its own crisis theory (Appendix 1 §11.6, PDF p. 205).

*(c) The licensing fee read as venality.* The manuscript predicts "Licence, and the licensing fee — the right to run frontier AI at scale conferred by the state, priced by the state, collected as the settlement's popular revenue" (Ch. 12 §IV). Varoufakis's whole political economy says a fixed cut of a platform's revenue is *rent*, and whoever collects rent is a co-rentier, not a sovereign: "This is not profit. It is cloud rent, the digital equivalent of ground rent" (PDF p. 112); rent "stubbornly refuses to enter circulation" (PDF p. 120). On his account the American licensing fee is the Crown joining the syndicate — spine §8(c)'s venality route, the H200 cut generalised — and a state financed by cloud rent has every incentive to protect the fiefs that pay it. The sharpest point of contact, because the spine already concedes that "a cut is income, not control."

*(d) Geopolitics without states.* "Compared to the original Cold War, the New Cold War has little politics behind it. Just naked technofeudal class interests" (PDF p. 137). The chip rule protects "Wall Street and Silicon Valley," the security language "very thinly disguised" (PDF pp. 136–37). Read so, every instrument Ch. 2 §V cites as the state's is the platforms' by proxy — and the manuscript's admission that the American moves run "half of them under doctrines that would deny the pattern" (Ch. 12 §IV) is grist for him.

*(e) Europe.* "Europe's lack of cloud capital means that the New Cold War… has already rendered Europe geostrategically irrelevant" (PDF p. 144). The manuscript holds the EU as a live conditional; he closes it.

**The book's best answer.** In reverse order. On (e), Varoufakis *is* the Depreter refinement stated without the conditional — apparatus, canon, no fisc, no stack; quote him as the verdict the manuscript declines to give yet. On (d), the answer is method: whose interest the chip rule serves is not decidable from motive, and the book does not decide it from motive; it asks who holds the switch afterwards, and a rule under which a territorial power "invests, denies and excommunicates at the level of the transistor" leaves the switch with the state whoever lobbied for it. Good. On (c) the answer is the spine's own — control, not income — but Varoufakis exposes that the manuscript's sentence, as drafted, leads with the *fee*, which is exactly what he has spent a book defining as rent. The renovated Ch. 12 must lead with revocation, state the fee as the fisc's by-product, and confront directly the objection that a rent-financed state is a captured state; the French settlement of 1439–46 — a crown that collected the levy *and* held the guns — is the precedent, and the two halves must be shown together. That answer is available and not yet made. On (a) and (b) the answer is the DEFEND-tier mechanism, and here Varoufakis has no instrument: no theory of decisive force, no fiscal-military state, no reason a "cloudalist" should hold anything a state with a siege train cannot take. His own definition of feudalism as "land and sophisticated weaponry" (PDF p. 58) concedes the second term and then forgets it. The honest report is that the mechanism answers him in full *only if* Part II holds — and that his concession that the CCP bounded its cloudalists (PDF p. 150), offered as a paradox, is the manuscript's leading case handed over by the rival. A good answer, stronger for being made against the best-known statement of the rival reading rather than against Bratton alone.

## 6. Placement

- **Ch. 1 §III — footnote.** The "market" misdescription has a popular rival that also denies the market and reaches for a *feudal* rather than ecclesiastical taxonomy; engagement deferred to Ch. 2. T3, rival.
- **Ch. 2 §I or §III — engaged BY NAME, one paragraph.** The "church, not market" amendment must say why *church* and not *fief*: a fief is a grant from a superior held on condition (Ch. 3's licence to retain), whereas the platforms answer to no grantor — which is what makes them universalisms and their absorption a Henrician rather than a tenurial question. His own vocabulary ("Jeff… grants them cloud-based digital fiefs, for a fee," PDF pp. 81–82) puts the platform in the *king's* seat of his metaphor. Rival, T3; the identity-enclosure passage (PDF pp. 69–71) as evidence in §II beside Kobrin.
- **Ch. 8 §VI — footnote.** "Sanitised tech-terror" (PDF p. 114) as the platform-side description of the sanction regime the chapter quantifies; the disanalogies paragraph already answers him.
- **Ch. 11 §VI moves one and five — evidence, footnoted.** The reserves freeze (PDF p. 138) once anchored at T1; Internet One and GPS as state releases (PDF pp. 67, 71).
- **Ch. 11 §VII and Ch. 12 §IV — engaged BY NAME, beside Bratton.** Spine §8(f) places Bratton's Cloud-feudalism-versus-Empire binary in ch11/coda; Varoufakis *chose* the first horn and should stand as its named holder. Quote the Standard Oil objection (PDF p. 157) and answer with the twelve-case pattern; quote the interoperability concession (note 8) as the sceptic supplying the instrument; state the venality reading of the licensing fee in his terms and answer it with control-not-income. Rival, T3.
- **Coda §VI — one sentence.** Bull entertained the dispersal as a possible successor system; Varoufakis declared it one and named it; the disagreement with both is the same disagreement about stability.
- **Appendix A §V — new entry**, T3, rival; engaged Ch. 2, Ch. 12.
- **Appendix C — no.** Nothing in the book is a measurable indicator with a sourced baseline.

## 7. Verdict

The book changes neither the direction nor the register of the manuscript, only its weight — but it changes the weight at one specific joint: it obliges Chapter 12 to answer the objection that a state financed by licensing fees has become a co-rentier rather than a consolidator, and the current draft's licensing-fee sentence, leading with the fee, invites exactly that reading. The single most important thing for the author to know is that Varoufakis is not an analogy-monger to be dismissed under the mechanism-not-analogy rule; he has a sequenced mechanism of his own (enclosure, then technology, then central-bank money) and a formal political economy, and the manuscript's advantage over him is precisely the thing his book never contains — a theory of decisive force and who can pay for it — so the engagement must be made on that ground and by name, in Chapter 2 (why church, not fief) and Chapter 12 (why control, not rent). His concession that the Chinese party-state bounded its cloudalists, offered as a paradox, is the manuscript's leading case handed over by the rival.
