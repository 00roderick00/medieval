# Assessment — Jan Zielonka, *Europe as Empire: The Nature of the Enlarged European Union* (Oxford UP, 2006; paperback with new preface, 2007)

*Read in full: /home/claude/books/Zielonka.txt (~123k words; 2007 paperback; main text printed pp. 1–191 = PDF pp. 14–204, constant +13 offset; prefaces PDF 6–9; notes pp. 192–271). Pins give "printed p. / PDF p." OCR clean; the figure at p. 86 / PDF 99 is scrambled. Manuscript sections read against it: Intro §§II–III; ch. 1 §§II–III, §VI; ch. 2 §§III–IV, §VI; ch. 10 §VI; ch. 11 §§VI–VII; ch. 12 §IV; Coda §IV and §VI; Appendix A §IV; Appendix C.*

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Zielonka writes a "polemical response to the mainstream literature on European integration" (p. 2 / PDF 15) against the statist paradigm — the EU as a Westphalian federation in the making. His thesis, from the paperback preface: "the ever larger EU increasingly resembles a neo-medieval empire rather than a neo-Westphalian super-state. And I also try to argue that such an empire is better suited for coping with the current cultural, economic and political pressures than a European super-state" (pp. v–vi / PDF 6–7). The engine is the 2004 eastern enlargement, read as "an impressive exercise in empire-building" (p. 20 / PDF 33) by conditionality, which imported so much diversity that "enlargement renders the rise of a European state impossible" (p. 9 / PDF 22). Two ideal types run through six functional chapters (Table 0.2, p. 14 / PDF 27): neo-Westphalian empire (conquest, centralised, coercion, military means) against neo-medieval (invitation, polycentric, incentives, "economic and bureaucratic" means, sovereignty "constrained through sharing").

The descriptive core: the Union "is anything but a state. It has no effective monopoly over the legitimate means of coercion. It has no clearly defined centre of authority. Its territory is not fixed" (p. 2 / PDF 15); and "Empire without an emperor is an odd concept, one might say. Obviously, the EU does not have any emperor. In fact, it does not even have a single centre of government" (p. 59 / PDF 72). Its normative conclusion is the rival claim: "I have no hesitation in concluding that Europe should embrace a neo-medieval EU" (p. 166 / PDF 179); "It is time to recognize the neo-medieval reality and make it work" (p. 191 / PDF 204). He uses no mechanism — "simple historical analogies never work, and I do not attempt to apply them" (p. 17 / PDF 30) — and his one gesture at how the first medieval order ended is Hirschman's, not Tilly's: "Westphalian states prevailed over other units existing in the Middle Ages because they were able to harden their borders and constrain 'exit' options for political and economic actors" (p. 177 / PDF 190). T2 for the EU's form as of 2005–07, T3 for medieval history (Kennedy, Davies, Ruggie at second hand); the neomedieval literature's only full-length application to a polity the manuscript casts as a candidate consolidator.

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**Ch. 12 §IV — "the single fisc and the single executive are not [present], its dynasty a treaty."** Zielonka states the fiscal absence in the manuscript's terms, from the Tilly school's premise. "How can any political and economic unit function properly without a fair degree of extraction and expenditure allocation? Even the most 'minimalist' nation states have a budget of at least 15 per cent of the GDP. The EU's current budget is merely 1.27 per cent of its combined GDP and even this low ceiling has never been reached in practice. If the Union wants to resemble a modern Westphalian state it needs to raise its revenues quite substantially and distribute them in a centralized, purposeful manner" (p. 96 / PDF 109). And: "Major contributors to the EU budget… fiercely resist any increase… They want the budget to be capped at 1 per cent of GDP" (p. 96 / PDF 109). His note 11 (p. 229 / PDF 242) concedes the manuscript's whole criterion: "Many scholars find the ability to extract and reallocate to be one of the major features of a state-building process and, in fact, the crucial pillar of any stateness" (Ardant, in Tilly ed. 1975). Ch. 5: "such crucial aspects of economic policies as taxation or social welfare are still firmly in the hands of the member states" (p. 117 / PDF 130).

**Ch. 2 §III — the EU as "a canonical authority… a church with no divisions"; ch. 11 §VI — "the European style is canon."** "the EU's most powerful instruments of pressure are in the field of economics rather than foreign and security policy proper" (p. 110 / PDF 123); its legitimising strategy is "Our norms are right" against the Westphalian "Might makes right" (Table 6.1, p. 144 / PDF 157); the neighbourhood strategy "is to bring the neighbours as close to the Union as they can be without being a member" (p. 112 / PDF 125). The acquis: "some 20,000 laws, decisions, and regulations, spanning nearly 80,000 pages and still growing" (p. 56 / PDF 69). The Brussels effect before Bradford named it, running on accession rather than market access.

**Ch. 12 §IV — "structurally the weakest in a precise sense."** "it is not the prime actor in charge of defence of its territory. In fact, it has no military capability to do the job" (p. 143 / PDF 156). Heisbourg, quoted: "The EU cannot have a proper security strategy as long as decisions on the use of force rest in the hands of its member governments" (p. 147 / PDF 160).

**Ch. 12 §IV — "the observable counter-moves are real."** The Union "is trying hard on behalf of its member states to regain a degree of control over the forces of globalization and to assert its sovereignty within its borders… even in the areas of defence and justice and home affairs" (p. 15 / PDF 28); "From time to time the Union manifests that kind of Westphalian reflex" (p. 103 / PDF 116).

**Ch. 1 §III and Coda §VI — the debt to Bull, precisely cast.** "When in 1977 Hedley Bull speculated on a possible return to the medieval pattern of interstate politics he still considered such a prospect relatively unlikely… Nevertheless, since the 1970s the situation in Europe has gradually developed according to the medieval scenario. Today we have in Europe the essence of the medieval politics identified by Bull, namely a complex 'system of overlapping authority and multiple loyalty'" (p. 162 / PDF 175).

**Ch. 1 §II — Friedrichs's duality, applied** (p. 157 / PDF 170, n. 69 to Friedrichs 2001); the Anderson attribution for the "patchwork" sentence confirmed at p. 121 / PDF 134 (n. 18 to *Lineages*, pp. 37–8).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

Nothing in the manuscript's facts. Four points of precision:

- **Appendix A §IV** files Zielonka as "needed for Ch2's EU-church section" and Wæver as "Engaged: Ch11 (the EU church)". The EU-church section is ch. 2 §III; ch. 11 §VI carries the canon *move*. Make the two entries consistent.
- **Appendix A §IV** cites Wæver's *JCMS* 1996 article for the "new middle ages of concentric circles." Zielonka's source for the concentric-circle empire — "from a 'central Cosmos to a peripheral Chaos'" — is Wæver, "Imperial Metaphors," in Tunander, Baev and Einagel (eds.), *Geopolitics in Post-Wall Europe* (Sage, 1997), p. 65 (Zielonka p. 13 / PDF 26; n. 44). If the manuscript wants the empire image rather than the securitisation one, the 1997 chapter is the pin.
- **Ch. 1 n. 1-3** says the 2012 Bull's "pagination matches the earlier printings." Zielonka cites the 1977 Macmillan edition at p. 254 for "overlapping authority and multiple loyalty" and 264–76 for the trends (nn. 103–104, p. 261 / PDF 274), against the manuscript's 245 and 248–66. The note should say "matches the 2002 printing; the 1977 first edition runs about nine pages later."
- **A caution.** Zielonka at p. 10 / PDF 23 attributes the Anderson "patchwork" sentence to Ruggie 1983 (n. 32). The manuscript's attribution is the right one; do not cite Zielonka p. 10 for it.

## 4. What it ADDS

**(a) The fiscal ceiling, stated by the rival — ch. 12 §IV; Appendix C, C10-1 and C11-1.** The "no fisc" limb is currently asserted; Zielonka supplies the 2005 baseline (p. 96 / PDF 109) and the criterion (n. 11). Carry the baseline and score against it: the 2021–27 MFF at roughly 1 per cent of GNI, NGEU's joint borrowing, SAFE and the own-resource proposals are the indicator's movement; Zielonka's number is the zero. [RE-CHECK AT PRESS.]

**(b) "Empire without an emperor" — ch. 10 §VI, ch. 12 §IV.** The Depreter refinement has its EU statement ready-made at p. 59 / PDF 72, extended: the rotating presidency means "the main centre of governance in the EU moves from one European capital to another on a regular basis, thus preventing the rise of a single European centre in Brussels" (p. 123 / PDF 136). And Charles Maier's aside: "the Crusader Kingdoms of Jerusalem and Acre with their 'condominized periphery' come closest to my imperial notion of the EU" (n. 50, p. 198 / PDF 211) — a polity that lost every fortress it held once the field army died at Hattin.

**(c) The Coal and Steel origin — ch. 12 §IV, ch. 10 §VIII.** "The Coal and Steel Community was in fact a major step away from the Westphalian prototype because the members of the Community were no longer free to make sovereign decisions in the field crucial for their readiness to fight wars" (p. 152 / PDF 165). The Union began by pooling the war-material substrate — the one thing the mechanism says a consolidator must own — and became the polity that owns least of it. The inversion belongs in the EU paragraph.

**(d) The canonical church builds fiscs for others — ch. 12 §IV, ch. 11 §VI.** In Bosnia the Union undertook "to support reform of the taxation system, in particular the introduction of value-added tax (VAT)… The High Commissioner subsequently established a 'Bulldozer Committee' to push through simplification of tax codes and boost public revenues" (p. 113 / PDF 126); Knaus and Martin on the High Representative: "When he speaks of enemies of the Bosnian state he means not armed paramilitaries committing premeditated arson, but business evading sales taxes" (pp. 113–14 / PDF 126–27). A polity with no fisc of its own administering its protectorates' fiscal settlements is the sharpest image of "canon without a fisc" the book has.

**(e) Exit as the medieval property — ch. 1 §IV, ch. 12 §VII.** "So far exit options are being chiefly utilized by chief executive officers (CEOs), Eurocrats, professional soccer players, and academics… They are the party of territoriality, to use Charles Maier's expression" (p. 184 / PDF 197). The over-mighty citizens are Zielonka's exit-class; the legibility stack is the exit-constraint his model calls the Westphalian move (p. 177 / PDF 190).

**(f) Two universalisms over Europe — ch. 2 §IV, ch. 12 §IV.** "like in the Middle Ages, European actors are subject to two competing universalistic claims" — the EU and the United States (p. 141 / PDF 154); "the EU increasingly resembles a neo-medieval empire, while the United States is still a typical Westphalian state" (p. 160 / PDF 173). The "canonical province" branch, visible in 2006 from inside the EU literature.

**(g) The Economist's amendment — ch. 2 §III.** Reviewers "suggested that my model would work more smoothly if there were in Europe an outside guarantor of free trade and competition: a medieval empire with a free-market papacy" (p. vi / PDF 7). The manuscript's answer is that the papacy arrived, and it sits in Brussels.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**(i) The EU is not a candidate consolidator, and cannot be one.** "The major objective of this book is to show that enlargement renders the rise of a European state impossible" (p. 9 / PDF 22). And the prescription runs directly against every Tudor move the manuscript scores: "I hope that this book will inspire us to search for more effective ways of governing the EU than through adoption of a European Constitution, strengthening the powers of the European parliament, creating a European army, or introducing a central redistribution and taxation system via Brussels" (p. 19 / PDF 32). This reaches the WAGER-tier four-polity casting (spine §6) and the "live conditional" of spine §5 and ch. 12 §IV.

**(ii) A bloc-level dispersal is stable, and better.** The manuscript stakes itself on dispersal being transitional (CLAUDE.md §1; Coda §VI). Zielonka is the counter-claim at the scale of one bloc: "A flexible neo-medieval empire in concentric circles would be in a better position than a European state to cope with the pressures of modernization and globalization. It would also be in a better position to compete with other great powers by pulling together vast European resources, but without eliminating Europe's greatest strength: its pluralism and diversity" (p. 22 / PDF 35; restated p. 191 / PDF 204). "Power in this system is deconcentrated, dispersed, divided, fragmented, or even fused" (p. 184 / PDF 197). The 2007 preface adds that events since "reinforce the neo-medieval scenario envisaged in this book" (p. vi / PDF 7).

**(iii) Bull's caution still holds globally.** Against ch. 1 §III's claim that the evidential threshold has been crossed on all five measures: "His scepticism is still justified in the global context despite the recent rise of private international violence in the form of terrorism and the ever greater spread of modern technologies" (p. 162 / PDF 175). Europe alone has gone medieval, and only because "two universalistic powers, the EU and the United States" hold it together.

**(iv) The Depreter limb is already tripped — or misframed.** Intro §III condition 4: the book fails if "in the one candidate asserting canon without a fisc, the canon alone proves sufficient to consolidate a bloc." Zielonka: canon alone already consolidated a bloc of twenty-five: "This was power politics at its best, even though the term 'power' was never mentioned" (p. 55 / PDF 68); "the design was truly imperialist, to use Robert Cooper's expression" (p. 55 / PDF 68); and, quoting Kovács and Kabachnik, "Eastern enlargement involves the construction of an empire that grows by willing dependencies, not by force" (p. 57 / PDF 70).

**(v) Military capacity is not what territorial control now turns on.** "modern weapons technology has made the traditional concept of territorial defence largely obsolete" (p. 145 / PDF 158); and in n. 64, "Global capitalism, new technologies (nuclear weapons among them), and democracy make it difficult for the EU to return to medieval behaviour" (p. 257 / PDF 270). Against the manuscript's premise that decisive force is still the thing polities are priced on.

**(vi) A rival account of the first ending.** The state prevailed by hardening borders and constraining exit (p. 177 / PDF 190, from Hirschman 1978); territoriality became possible with "electricity, steel, and steam" in the mid-nineteenth century (Maier, p. 10 / PDF 23). Artillery, the taille and the fifteenth century do not appear; "Armed forces were used rather reluctantly in medieval Europe" (p. 149 / PDF 162).

**The book's best answer.**

To (i) and (ii): Zielonka's evidence is 2004–07 and his text names the condition under which it would change — "Any significant change in the European governance system is more likely to result from external pressures and shocks" (p. 105 / PDF 118) — while his stability rests on "the pacifying presence of these two powers" (p. 162 / PDF 175), the second of which is the United States. The Cerny hinge (ch. 1 §IV) is exactly the exogenous pressure his model lacked, and it has since arrived. The manuscript does not claim the EU *will* consolidate; it scores whether it does and names the other branch — "the American bloc's canonical province — Burgundy's apparatus, inherited by the neighbour with the centre" (ch. 12 §IV). Zielonka's stable neomedieval Europe *is* that branch described from inside, with the American guarantee as the missing centre. The answer is good and should be made in his words: his empire is stable because a Westphalian state underwrites it. What the manuscript must then concede is a third outcome the wager does not score cleanly — a bloc that neither consolidates nor is absorbed, because the guarantor stays. Appendix C should say what distinguishes "province" from "stable plural bloc under guarantee", or admit that for the EU they may be the same thing.

To (iv): the answer is a definition, and the Introduction needs it. Zielonka's canon consolidated a *jurisdiction* — a legal order of 80,000 pages — not a fisc and not decisive force; his own text has compliance "often more apparent than real, but cheating is the essence of imperial relations" (p. 13 / PDF 26). The Depreter limb should read "consolidate a bloc's decisive capability" — otherwise Zielonka has already collected on it. An honest narrowing, not an escape: the mechanism was always about who pays for the guns.

To (iii): dated 2005 and resting on terrorism as the form of private violence; ch. 3's industry and ch. 1's arbitral record post-date him. Adequate.

To (v) and (vi): (v) is asserted without argument and the Ukraine record answers it; the spine (§5) owes ch. 12 the nuclear paragraph. (vi) is not a defended mechanism — Hirschman and Maier by citation — and Part II meets it on the record. But exit-constraint should be engaged by name: it is the neomedieval literature's only candidate for how the first order ended, and the legibility stack is exit-constraint by other means. Good answer; engagement owed.

## 7. Verdict

This book does not change the manuscript's direction and cannot touch the mechanism, since it offers none; it changes the weight and the precision of the EU case, which now has its rival stated by a serious scholar with the evidence of 2004–07 and a normative preference for the plural outcome. The single most important thing for the author is that Zielonka has already collected on the Depreter limb as drafted — canon alone did consolidate a bloc, a jurisdiction of twenty-five states built by conditionality and no fisc — so the Introduction's fourth condition must say "decisive capability", not "bloc", or the wager is lost on its own terms before the guns are counted. His concession at p. 96 and n. 11 that extraction is "the crucial pillar of any stateness", and his admission that the whole stable arrangement rests on the American guarantee, are the two sentences with which the manuscript answers him, and it should answer him by name.
