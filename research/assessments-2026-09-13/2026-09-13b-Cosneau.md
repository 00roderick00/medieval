# Assessment — Cosneau, *Le connétable de Richemont* (1886) — pièces justificatives only: app. LXXXIV, the Louppy/Luppé ordonnance of May 1445

Scope read: the pièces justificatives, PDF pp. 586–746 (printed pp. 562–722; PDF = printed + 24), with the table of pièces and chapter table (PDF pp. 727–29). The file also carries PDF pp. 1–154, not read per the task. Cosneau's narrative chapter on the reform (ch. V, printed pp. 358–77) is NOT in the file; the "[p. 360]" cross-references in pièce headings cannot be checked here. OCR: legible but with digit and superscript corruption ("4445" for 1445; "xxv*" for "xxv^e"; "1x**x" for "ix^xx x"). Inferential readings are marked.

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Cosneau's biography of Richemont (T2, 1886) prints as appendices archival pièces from the Bibliothèque nationale and the Archives nationales (K 68 for the 1445 material). The pièces are T1 primary documents in a scholarly edition; the headings and footnotes are T2 apparatus. There is no thesis in this range — documents, not argument. The heading that matters reads, verbatim: "LXXXIV / ORDONNANCE DE LUPPE-LE-CHASTEL (1445, 26 mai) [p. 360]." (PDF p. 634 / printed p. 610). The table of pièces repeats it: "LXXXIV. — Ordonnance de Luppé-le-Chastel (1445, 26 mai)......... 610" (PDF p. 728).

The document is a royal commission, not the founding ordonnance. Its opening sentence establishes that the founding acts already exist and are being applied: "avons, par grande et meure délibéracion, fait certaines ordonnances justes et raisonnables sur la maniére et ordre de vivre desdits gens d'armes et les logeis d'iceulx" (PDF p. 634 / printed p. 610). Its archival pin, as Cosneau gives it: "(K 68, n° 14.)" (PDF p. 636 / printed p. 612).

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**(a) The lance of six men and six horses.** Manuscript ch10 §II: the lance fournie "composée de six hommes et de six chevaux" — man-at-arms, coutillier, page, two archers, valet [^10-2], carried from Contamine. Louppy carries it at T1: "chacun homme d'armes ung coustiller, ung paige et trois chevaulx et deux archers, ung paige, ou ung varlet de guerre et trois chevaulx" (PDF p. 634 / printed p. 610); and again, arithmetically, "a trois personnes et trois chevaulx pour lance, et, pour deux archers, trois personnes et trois chevaulx" (PDF p. 635 / printed p. 611). Note the sixth man is "ung paige, ou ung varlet de guerre" — page *or* valet — which the manuscript's list should reflect.

**(b) The dating window.** Manuscript ch10 §I: the lost ordonnance lies "between an act given at Nancy on 9 January 1445 and the surviving text of Louppy-le-Château of 26 May 1445, and before 20 April." The 20 April terminus is now in corpus: app. LXXXIII, Richemont's safe-conduct "Donné a la Marche en Lorreyne, le xx^e jour d'avril l'an mil ccccxlv" (PDF p. 634 / printed p. 610), which licenses the bastard of Limeuil "en suivant l'ordonnance de monseigneur le Roy, pour faire cesser les pilleries et roberies ... de soy despartir des routes et compaignies, et mener en sa compaignie jusques au nombre de viii^xx chevaulx" (PDF p. 633 / printed p. 609) — i.e. up to 160 horse licensed home under the king's ordonnance, which therefore already existed on 20 April. The 9 January act is in Cosneau's chapter table ("Ordonnance du 9 janvier 1445. — Autre ordonnance.", PDF p. 729) but not among the pièces.

**(c) Garrison in the bonnes villes and accountable captains.** Manuscript ch10 §I: "quartered in garrison towns ... salaried by the crown." Louppy: "lesdiz gens d'armes seront logiez és bonnes villes de tous les pais de nostre roiaume, chacun selon ce que raisonnablement il pourra porter"; and the captains are "certains notables chiefz, noz subgetz bien recéans et qui ont que perdre en nostre roiaume ... lesquelx seront tenuz de respondre et rendre compte des gens qu'ilz auront en leur charge" (PDF pp. 634–35 / printed pp. 610–11). The reason given for garrisoning is disciplinary, not strategic: kept in the field "estoit fort a doubter que aucunement ilz s'escartassent, ne voulsissent pas bien obéir a leurs chiefz" (printed p. 611).

**(d) The export of the écorcheurs, 1444.** Manuscript ch03: "the Dauphin led the bands to the Upper Rhine in 1444–45." App. LXXXII (Orléans, 21 July 1444; PDF pp. 631–33 / printed pp. 607–09): after the truce "nous ayons ordonné que tous les capitaines et gens de guerre qui estoient et vivoient sur les champs en nostre royaume ... s'en yroient, en la compaignie de nostre trés chier et trés amé filz, le Daulphin de Viennois, lequel, par nostre ordonnance, les meine et conduit hors de nostre dit royaume" (printed p. 607). The same letter's phrase "en maniére que la force et auctorité nous demeure" (printed p. 608) is a usable line on the monopoly of force, T1.

## 3. What it CORRECTS

**Spelling.** Manuscript (intro; ch10 §I) writes "Louppy-le-Château", with a flag. Cosneau prints "LUPPE-LE-CHASTEL" in the pièce heading and table of pièces, and "Luppé le Chastel" in the dating clause of the document itself: "Donné a Luppé le Chastel, le xxv^e jour de may, l'an de grace mil cccc quarante et cinq et de nostre régne le xxiii^e" (PDF p. 636 / printed p. 612). Cosneau's own modernised form, in his editorial voice, is "Louppy": the chapter table has "Ordonnance de Louppy (du 26 mai 1445)" (PDF p. 729) and his footnote to LXXXVI reads "Cf. ordonn. de Louppy, ci-dessus, p. 614" (PDF p. 639; the page number is probably an OCR corruption of 611). Ruling for the chapter: cite the act as Cosneau does in the heading — *Luppé-le-Chastel* — when quoting the document, and "Louppy" when naming the place in the text; "Louppy-le-Château/-Châtel" is Contamine's (and the modern) form, not Cosneau's. Neither "-Château" nor "-Châtel" occurs anywhere in this range.

**Day of the month.** Heading and table both say 26 May. The OCR of the dating clause reads "le xxv* jour de may" — which is either "xxv^e" (25th) or "xxvi^e" with the i lost. Cosneau, who read the original, printed 26 in two places; carry 26 May but note the transcription discrepancy rather than resolving it from the OCR.

**What Louppy "presupposes".** Manuscript ch10 §I says "the Louppy text presupposes ... the retention, in permanent royal pay, of some fifteen hundred lances ... salaried by the crown." Too strong for this document. Louppy carries no total, no word "permanent", no crown salary, and no companies. It presupposes ordonnances (plural) on the soldiers' "maniére et ordre de vivre" and "logeis", a fixed lance, accountable captains, and lodging in towns. The 1,500 figure and crown pay rest on Basin, d'Escouchy and Contamine's other documents, and the sentence should be re-pointed to them.

## 4. What it ADDS

**(a) What Louppy actually enacts — the chapter can now say precisely.** It is addressed to five named commissioners for Poitou — "l'évesque de Poictiers, le seneschal de Limosin, Jehan Lebourcier, chevalier, messires Morice Claveurier, Jehan Chevrier, et aux esleuz sur le fait des aides en Poictou" (printed p. 610) — and it does five things. (i) Fixes Poitou's quota and its captains: "ou pais de Poictou seront logés ix^xx x lances et les archers, c'est assavoir soubz nostre amé et féal le seneschal dudit pais cent lances, soubz le maréchal de Loheac, au Bas Poictou lx lances et trente lances du nombre de Floquet" (printed p. 611) — 100 + 60 + 30 = 190 lances, and the arithmetic follows: "xi^c xl personnes et autant de chevaulx" = 1,140 persons and horses (the OCR "xifxz" read inferentially; 190 × 6 = 1,140 confirms it). (ii) Specifies maintenance in kind per person per year — "trois charges et demi de blé et deux pippes de vin"; per lance per month "deux moutons, et demi beuf ou vache"; per year "quatre lars"; for salt, oil, candles, eggs and cheese "vingt solz tournois" a month; per horse per year "douze chevaulx chargez d'avoine, et quatre charretées tant foing que paille" (printed p. 611). Cosneau's footnote records Vallet de Viriville's copy reading "une charge et demie" against his "trois" (printed p. 611 n. 1). (iii) Empowers the commissioners to assess and levy those supplies and money "le fort portant le foible" up to a sum left blank in the original — "jusques a la somme de [ ] livres tournois"; Cosneau's note: "En blanc. Au n° 14 bis, qui indique 40 lances, soit 240 personnes et 240 chevaux, logés dans les pays de Mende et du Gevaudan, la somme énoncée est 400 l. t." (printed p. 612 n. 1; the OCR's "260" is an error for 240). (iv) Makes the levy quarterly and universal: payable "par quartier d'an", by "exemps et non exemps, privilégiez et non privilégiez, et sans préjudice de priviléges pour ceste foiz", collected "par toutes voies acoustumées a faire pour noz propres debtes, non obstans opposicions ou appellacions quelxconques" (printed p. 612). (v) Backs it with summons before "nous et les gens de nostre grant Conseil quelque part que serons, sur peine de bannissement et de confiscacion de corps et de biens", with seizure of lay goods and the clergy's "temporel en nostre main réaument et de fait" (printed p. 612). Signed "Par le Roy, en son conseil. DELALOERE" (printed p. 612).

What it does NOT contain: no total establishment; no list of the fifteen captains; no company structure; no money wage (the 20 s.t. is a sundries allowance); no reference to the taille; no statement of duration. It is the billeting-and-provisioning instrument, executed province by province under the lost general ordonnance (K 68 n° 14 bis is the Gévaudan parallel). For ch10 §I: the founding act is lost, but its executing commissions survive, and they show a crown assigning numbered lances to named commanders in a named province and taxing that province, privileged and unprivileged alike, to feed them.

**(b) The three "voyes" (app. LXXXVI, PDF pp. 638–40 / printed pp. 614–16).** "Les trois voyes du vivre des gens d'armes premièrement tenues (1445)": the crown offered each pays a choice, effective "le premier jour de janvier mil ccccxlv" (i.e. 1446 n.s., inferentially), between (1) maintenance in kind plus "huit livres tournois pour lance" and 20 s.t. for the captain's estate monthly; (2) "vingt livres tournois pour lance fournie" plus grain, oats, hay and wood monthly; or (3) cash only, "xxxi l. t. a chacune lance fournie de six personnes et six chevaulx ... xxx l. t. et, pour l'estat du capitaine, xx s. t." (printed p. 615), with the soldiers paying "hostellage" of 30 s.t. a month and rotating billets "de trois en trois mois" (printed p. 616). Cosneau adds that by letters of 26 November 1445 the Bas-Limousin and the Marche could choose "31 fr. par lance fournie, par mois, ou 21 fr. et les vivres" (printed p. 616, citing K 68 n° 22, 23). This is the document that shows the payroll's *cash-ification* — the migration from provisioning in kind to a money rate per lance — and it belongs in ch10 §II beside Contamine's 15 l.t./month tariff, which it does not contradict (31 l.t. per lance of six ≈ the man-at-arms's 15 plus two archers at 7 l. 10 s. = 30 l.t.; the match is exact and worth stating).

**(c) Other pièces bearing on 1439–45.** The Nancy sojourn: LXXVIII bis n° 1 is "Donné a Nancy, en Lorraine, le xxiv^e jour de novembre ... mil quatre cens quarante quatre" (PDF p. 624 / printed p. 600) and LXXXVII "Donné a Nancy, en Lorraine, le xi^e jour d'avril ... quarante cinq ... aprés Pasques" (PDF p. 641 / printed p. 617) — with LXXXIII (La Marche, 20 April) they fix the court in Lorraine from November 1444 to April 1445, the setting of the 9 January act. The destruction phase of ch03's export–absorption–destruction sequence: LXXXI (Tours, penultimate day of February 1443 o.s., PDF pp. 629–31 / printed pp. 605–07) and LXXXII commission the prévôt of Paris and the baillis of Senlis and Meaux to hunt soldiers living off the country "par voye de fait et main armée", with advance pardon for killings. Earlier: LXXII (PDF p. 597 / printed p. 573), Parlement proceedings of November 1439 against Jacques de Chabannes for keeping at Corbeil and Vincennes "beaucoup de gens de guerre, qui ont fait plusieurs maulx et pilleries et peu d'obéissance a justice". Nothing in this range on the Praguerie (index only, PDF p. 736, pointing to narrative pp. 285–308) or on Montferrand 1440.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

Nothing at full strength, but one pressure point. Louppy is a *provincial provisioning levy in kind*, coercive, quarterly, universal — "exemps et non exemps, privilégiez et non privilégiez" (printed p. 612) — and it says nothing of the taille. A critic could argue that the 1445 army was initially fed by ad hoc billeting charges rather than by the permanent tax the manuscript's mechanism requires, i.e. that army and tax were less tightly coupled in 1445 than ch10 §II–III imply.

*The book's best answer*: the spine's 2 September ruling already states the hinge as "the settlement of 1439 to 1446", made in practice; Louppy and the three voyes are that practice — the voyes convert the levy into a cash rate per lance within seven months, and the levy is collected "par toutes voies acoustumées a faire pour noz propres debtes", i.e. by the machinery of royal debt. That answer is good and, if anything, the documents strengthen the co-evolution ruling (§8(a)) — the money follows the men by months, not years. No DEFEND-tier claim is touched.

## 6. Placement

ch10 §I: LXXXIV by name, T1 (Cosneau app. LXXXIV, pp. 610–12; AN K 68 n° 14), as the executing commission for Poitou; LXXXIII (pp. 609–10) as the 20 April terminus; rewrite the "presupposes" sentence per §3. ch10 §II: LXXXVI (pp. 614–16) for the money rate per lance beside Contamine's tariff, footnoting the 26 November 1445 letters. ch03: LXXXI–LXXXII as T1 footnotes for the export and destruction of the écorcheurs. Cosneau's narrative ch. V (pp. 358–77) goes on the RETRIEVAL LIST only if the chapter wants his "Du nombre des compagnies" and "De la taille perpétuelle"; superseded by Contamine.

## 7. Verdict

Only weight, and one precision: the Louppy text is now in corpus at T1 and it is a provincial billeting commission that assigns 190 lances to three named commanders in Poitou and taxes the province to feed them, not a statement of the standing army's size or pay. The single most important thing for the author: the chapter's sentence that Louppy "presupposes" fifteen hundred salaried lances must be re-pointed to Basin, d'Escouchy and Contamine, and the spelling settled as Cosneau prints it — "Luppé-le-Chastel" in the heading and dating clause, "Louppy" in his own editorial voice, never "-Château" or "-Châtel". The three voyes (app. LXXXVI) are the better document for the payroll argument and should be added.
