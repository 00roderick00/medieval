# Assessment — J. C. Sharman, *Empires of the Weak: The Real Story of European Expansion and the Creation of the New World Order* (Princeton, 2019)

*Read in full: /home/claude/books/Sharman.txt (~73k words; main text printed pp. 1–151 = PDF pp. 16–166, constant +15 offset; notes, bibliography, index follow). Pins give "printed p. / PDF p." OCR is clean except the running heads and one garbled note — ch. 3 n. 68 reads "Agoston 2012: 1300" (printed p. 164 / PDF 179). "Pizaro", "Cortes", "Columbo" are the author's spellings. Manuscript sections read against it: Intro §IV; ch. 4 §VI–VII; ch. 6 §V–VII; ch. 10 §I–III, §VII–VIII; ch. 11 entire; ch. 12 §V–VII; Coda entire.*

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Sharman is an IR scholar attacking the *global* extension of the military-revolution thesis — the Roberts–Parker claim that competition-driven European advances in guns, drill, fortification, army size and fiscal-administrative centralisation explain both the sovereign state and Europe's conquest of the world from 1500. His negative case: "Europeans did not enjoy any significant military superiority vis-à-vis non-Western opponents in the early modern era, even in Europe" (p. 1 / PDF 16); expansion overseas was carried by tiny private bands and chartered "company sovereigns" using none of the military revolution's tactics; the Ottomans, Mughals and Ming/Qing were the real conquerors of the age; and the whole apparatus "brings into question the idea of tight cause-and-effect connections between new weapons, tactics, large standing armies, and the rise of the sovereign state" (p. 2 / PDF 17). The non-European record "disconfirms the idea of a single path to military effectiveness, of sequences of necessary and sufficient causes, either technological or tactical, by which war makes states" (p. 6 / PDF 21).

His positive thesis has two layers. Historically, European expansion to 1750 rested on maritime preference ("lords of land" tolerating "masters of water"), local allies, deference to Asian empires and, in the Americas, disease. Theoretically — the half that matters most here — he rejects the functionalist engine beneath Roberts, Parker, Tilly and Hoffman: that competition produces well-adapted organisations by rational learning and Darwinian elimination. Against it he sets Elster on learning, Meyer's institutionalism (legitimacy over efficiency), Black and Lee on culture, and a set-piece on magical bulletproofing in African wars, where "the drivers of selection are present to an unusually clear degree, and yet the result equally conspicuously is the opposite of what the explanation says should happen" (p. 30 / PDF 45). The book is short, secondary-source, T3 in weight but transmitting T2 historiography (Agoston, Murphey, Aksan, Lorge, Andrade, Roy, Gommans, Stern, Parrott, Hui), and openly hostile to the causal form this manuscript uses.

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**The fiscal half of the mechanism, from a critic.** Ch. 10 §VII rests on sceptics' concessions; Sharman adds one. On the EIC: "The weight of evidence favors the idea that it was the EIC's superior institutional and financial capacity that allowed it to eventually triumph over a succession of South Asian and European rivals" (p. 94 / PDF 109); its "crucial advantage" was "its superior ability to buy military success… ensuring the mercenaries were paid consistently and that its armies were well supplied" (p. 93 / PDF 108). Ch. 10 §II's "an army *accounted for*", restated for Bengal.

**Rogers's cycle (ch. 6 §VII), observed in Bengal.** After Plassey, "With a massive increase in revenues, the Company could pay for larger and more capable armies, which increased the territory and population under its control, which generated more revenue, and so on" (p. 88 / PDF 103).

**The undermighty-king diagnosis (ch. 5), Mughal form.** Post-Mughal states: "enlarging territories and armies by ceding revenue rights left central rulers with less and less money" (pp. 92–93 / PDF 107–108); the nobles' cavalry "owed allegiance to the particular noble, not the emperor or the empire. They absorbed around 80 percent of all imperial land revenue" (pp. 91–92 / PDF 106–107).

**Artillery as the fortress threshold (ch. 6 §V, "fell because they were priced").** In Ceylon, "due to their lack of artillery the Kandyans could not capture Portuguese forts… and hence the Kandyan resort to allying with the Dutch" (p. 62 / PDF 77) — composition with the polity that has the train, in his own evidence.

**The Ottomans ran the settlement first (ch. 10 §II, ch. 11 §IV).** "The Ottomans had a permanent, standing infantry army equipped with guns, directly commanded by the sultan, well-supplied by a complex bureaucratic logistical system, and paid for with central tax revenues well before the European great powers started to do the same" (p. 100 / PDF 115); "Until the 1700s, the Sublime Porte was more capable in logistics, finance, and recruitment than its main European adversaries" and "less prone to the ruinous bankruptcies" (p. 108 / PDF 123).

**Contemporaries misdiagnose defeat (ch. 6 §V; spine §8(e).3, .6).** Spanish failure in North Africa "attributed not to the strength of the enemy but to bad leadership or the positions of the planets" (p. 119 / PDF 134); the VOC after Zeelandia scapegoated "the fort's commander" rather than "engage in serious introspection and reform" (p. 78 / PDF 93); and via Tuck: "it may be clear that one has lost; it is often far from clear why one has lost" (p. 113 / PDF 128). Sits beside Ibn Iyas and Bloch.

**Interstitial disorder (CLAUDE.md §1, claim 3).** Western militaries "must avoid overseas expeditionary campaigns that involve holding territory and controlling populations, because Western forces will generally lose such contests" (p. 145 / PDF 160).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

Nothing hard. Two imprecisions:

- **Ch. 11 §IV** gives the Janissary corps under Mehmed II as "roughly five to ten thousand." **Sharman p. 107 / PDF 122**: "around 20,000–30,000 in the period c.1400–1650" (n. 31: Murphey 1999: 45; Aksan 2011: 149–150). His is a period average and the manuscript's fifteenth-century figure is the more careful, but the text should say the corps grew to that order by the sixteenth century.
- **Ch. 6 §VII and ch. 10 §I**: "the only entities able to pay it were the ones we would shortly begin calling states." **Sharman pp. 69–70, 95 / PDF 84–85, 110**: the VOC and EIC — "a privately owned joint-stock company, a vassal of the Mughal emperor, the suzerain of various South Asian tributaries, and a direct ruler" — paid it for two centuries. True of Latin Christendom in 1450; false as a law. Bound it (see §5).

## 4. What it ADDS

**(a) The company sovereign as the historical form of "operative but never invested" — ch. 11 §VII, ch. 12 §VII, ch. 4 §VI.** Ch. 12 §VII asserts that "Chartered-company forms were invented where the crown wanted capacity it could licence but not run" without an instance. Sharman's chapter 2 is the instance, with the lag that ruling §8(c)(i) requires. Founding logic: "the easiest and cheapest way of achieving their geopolitical goals outside Europe was by creating monopolies, and then assigning these monopolies to a corporate actor" (p. 69 / PDF 84). The hybrid: the VOC "not only a Company of commerce, but also a Company of State" (p. 70 / PDF 85, a Dutch official). The absorption: "Toward the end of the eighteenth century, the British government began a gradual process of winding back the Company's sovereign and corporate privileges in tying it more closely to the state" (p. 82 / PDF 97), and it "followed rather than preceded the establishment of its hegemony on the subcontinent" (p. 95 / PDF 110). Kamen on the encomienda: "the crown was able to mount a military operation in the New World without the necessity, which it would in any case not have been able to fulfill, of sending an army there" (p. 40 / PDF 55). A chartered actor built decisive capability on its own credit, ruled a fifth of humanity, and was leashed by statute (1773, 1784, 1858) with its property left intact — the platform prediction already run once. Stern, Parrott and Ward go on the RETRIEVAL LIST.

**(b) Ottoman decentralisation 1650–1800 as a fifth failure mode — ch. 11 §IV, Coda §I.** "From a system in which the imperial treasury had taken almost 60 percent of tax revenues in the 1500s, by the end of the 1600s these local figures came to control 75 percent of tax revenues, and they later increased their share to more than 80 percent" (p. 112 / PDF 127); the timar cavalry "fell away"; more Janissaries "had to be paid for directly by the imperial treasury, creating considerable fiscal stress"; "the Janissaries seem to have given up drill around 1700" (pp. 111–113 / PDF 126–128). Aksan's comparison belongs in the text: "in the period 1768–1770 the English East India Company spent three to four times as much on its military than the sultan did" (p. 112 / PDF 127).

**(c) The coincidence objection, cleanly stated — ch. 10 §VII.** "The fact that several Western European polities developed the features that later came to define the modern sovereign state after their armies had increased in size may simply have been a coincidence" (p. 126 / PDF 141, n. 126 Gat 2006); "or is that just the way things happened to turn out in Europe for contingent, idiosyncratic reasons?" (p. 124 / PDF 139). Ch. 10 §VII answers Curry; it should name this and answer it with the ratchet ruling and the 1484 test.

**(d) A non-European learning-failure exhibit for ch. 6 §V.** Vijayanagara vs Bijapur, 1519: "Bijapur had hundreds of cannons, while the Vijayanagara army had few or none… the 'modern' Bijapuris were decisively defeated by 'backward' Vijayanagara. Both sides interpreted the result as an endorsement of their existing force structure" (p. 97 / PDF 112).

**(e) The Mapuche as the pre-modern longbow people — ch. 12 §V.** "a decentralized society, banding together in times of war, but with no capital or political center vulnerable to attack" (p. 45 / PDF 60); "fifty million pesos and more than 25,000 recruits" had not sufficed by the 1740s (p. 46 / PDF 61). Distributed denial for three centuries; nothing consolidated.

**(f) Albuquerque, for ch. 12.** "if Portugal should suffer a reverse at sea, your Indian possessions have not power to hold out a day longer than Kings of the land choose to suffer it" (p. 60 / PDF 75). Capability held on sufferance.

**(g) A citation gap.** Sharman reads Rogers as dating the break "with the rise of infantry and artillery in the 1400s, which he further sees as critical to Western dominance overseas" (p. 105 / PDF 120; n. 25: Rogers 1995b: 56, 60–61). Ch. 6 cites Rogers 1993 only; the 1995 version with the overseas extension is the one Sharman attacks and the manuscript must be seen not to endorse.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**(i) The technology-to-institution arrow is a Eurocentric artefact.** "there is a tendency to see events that happened in a certain order in Europe as causing each other, and as necessarily having to happen in that specific sequence" (p. 14 / PDF 29). In China, "Neither new artillery nor new styles of fortification had much impact in changing existing political institutions" (p. 126 / PDF 141); in South Asia, "Artillery made relatively little difference to the conduct of sieges, and in some ways favored defenders more than attackers. Certainly gunpowder weapons did not remake polities. There was thus nothing natural or inevitable about the sequences that happened to occur in Europe" (p. 127 / PDF 142); "The Mughals incorporated guns into their existing mode of cavalry-based warfare, further undermining the idea at the center of the military revolution that a given military technology necessarily required a given tactical or institutional approach" (p. 92 / PDF 107). Reaches the DEFEND-tier mechanism (spine §6) and ruling §8(a).

**(ii) Competition does not select the fiscal-military form.** "polities tend to be very durable and are rarely eliminated by conquest" (p. 22 / PDF 37); "the survival of inefficient institutions remains a central puzzle" (p. 150 / PDF 165); Hui reads European state-building as "a series of short-term improvised expedients that she terms 'self-weakening'… extensive reliance on debt… the use of mercenaries rather than conscript armies" (p. 128 / PDF 143). Cuts at spine §7, "the consolidation is compelled."

**(iii) The state was not the necessary payer.** "Contra the teleology of the military revolution, the 'modern' state monopolizing armed force was bested by the private wielders of violence" (p. 61 / PDF 76); "In fiscal-military terms war did not make a state, because the EIC triumphed as a company sovereign" (p. 69 / PDF 84); "it was a private company, or at least a private-public hybrid, that eclipsed the states, both European (the Portuguese and French) and Asian" (p. 95 / PDF 110).

**(iv) Decisive force is not decisive.** "Better weapons, complete air superiority, superior communications… far more lavish funding… somehow consistently failed to deliver victory against non-Western opponents" (p. 145 / PDF 160); "Winning battles but losing wars became a recurring theme" (pp. 144–145 / PDF 159–160). Against ch. 6 §V's "Decisive force is decisive" and the Iran exhibit in ch. 12 §V.

**(v) The Ottoman ratchet slipped.** The polity that ran the settlement first decentralised its fisc from the 1650s and lost from 1768, and "technology was not the problem" (p. 113 / PDF 128). If artillery makes the fiscal settlement irreversible, this is the irreversibility failing in the one non-European case the manuscript admits.

**The book's best answer.**

To (i): the manuscript claims a *price* mechanism under stated conditions (Coda §I), not that guns remake polities wherever they land. Sharman's own South Asian evidence — artillery "favored defenders" — says the first condition failed there: no wall economy collapsed, so no surrender clock ran. And his Ottoman chapter confirms the mechanism where sieges were decisive: "Ottoman mastery of sieges is highly significant for the broader military revolution thesis" (p. 111 / PDF 126). Good answer, but it needs a sentence the manuscript lacks: the mechanism is siege-specific, and its first condition is a fortification regime the new capability can price. The Coda must state that scope condition by name.

To (ii): the spine already separates requirement from trend (§7), and the English control case is a polity demoted, not eliminated — Fazal concerns extinction; the manuscript concerns rank. Adequate. But Hui's "self-weakening" (debt, venality, mercenaries inside the French settlement) is sharper than Sharman lets on and should be met in ch. 10 §III, not folded into the sceptics-concede arc that ruling §8(f).4 closes.

To (iii): Sharman is right about the words and the manuscript right about the mechanism. The EIC paid the price on Indian credit — "90 percent of this credit originating from local lenders" (p. 94 / PDF 109) — and was absorbed by the state once its capability became decisive for the metropole, by licence and statute, property intact: ruling §8(b)/(c) run in 1773–1858. Rewrite "shortly begin calling states" as "fiscally capable centres — states, or hybrids the state would later absorb," and take the EIC as the settlement's proof with the lag. Good answer; two sentences and a paragraph.

To (iv): the book's claim is bounded to force that takes and holds inside blocs; Sharman's post-1945 record is expeditionary war in the interstices, conceded to disorder by design. The Iran exhibit is a destruction exhibit, not a holding exhibit, and its framing sentence should say so. Adequate with a wording fix.

To (v): not currently answered, and it should be. The ratchet held where only external assemblies could reverse the settlement (France, 1484); it failed where the state's own service classes — Janissaries turned hereditary, ayan turned tax-farmers — reversed it from inside. That is a fifth failure mode, and the one the platform chapters should fear: absorption undone by the absorbed capability becoming an estate.

## 6. Placement

- **Intro §IV** — footnote. One clause stating the manuscript claims no European superiority over non-European polities disarms most of the book before it opens.
- **Ch. 4 §VI** — by name, one paragraph: the company sovereigns as the dispersal's most developed actor. T3 pointing to Stern and Parrott (T2) for retrieval.
- **Ch. 6 §V, §VII** — footnotes: Vijayanagara/Bijapur and Hess into the misdiagnosis paragraph; Rogers 1995b via Sharman's pin.
- **Ch. 10 §VII** — by name, as rival: the coincidence objection answered with the ratchet and 1484.
- **Ch. 11 §IV** — by name, as evidence and rival: Ottoman priority (ally) and Ottoman decentralisation (fifth failure mode); Aksan's EIC/sultan comparison in text.
- **Ch. 11 §VII, ch. 12 §VII** — by name, as ally with a lag: the EIC precedent; the two "states" sentences rewritten.
- **Ch. 12 §V** — footnote: Mapuche beside the drone paragraph; "winning battles, losing wars" acknowledged in the Iran framing.
- **Coda §I** — by name: the anti-functionalist objection (Elster/Meyer/bulletproofing) as the strongest philosophical objection to a mechanism under conditions, and the siege scope condition added to the conditions.

Tier: T3 throughout; its Ottoman and EIC chapters transmit T2 and should serve as a retrieval map, not a source of record.

## 7. Verdict

Sharman does not change the book's direction — the manuscript never made the claim he attacks, and his own Ottoman and Bengal chapters carry the fiscal half of the mechanism — but he changes its register at two joints: the "only states could pay" sentences must admit the company sovereigns and take the EIC as the absorption precedent with the lag, and the Coda must state the mechanism's siege-specific scope condition by name, because that condition, not Eurocentrism, is why guns "did not remake polities" in Mughal India. The single most important thing the author should know: the Ottoman Empire, admitted to the pattern as its limit case, reversed the settlement from inside between 1650 and 1800 — treasury share of revenue from about 60 per cent to under 20 — without losing the technology, and that fifth failure mode, absorption undone by the absorbed capability becoming an estate, is the one the platform chapters have not yet imagined.
