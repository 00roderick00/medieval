# Bobbitt, *The Shield of Achilles: War, Peace, and the Course of History* (Knopf 2002; Anchor 2003) — assessed against *The Artillery State*

## 1. The book in two paragraphs

Bobbitt's claim is that the modern state has had six constitutional orders — princely, kingly, territorial, state-nation, nation-state, market-state — each born of an "epochal war" in which a strategic and a constitutional innovation selected one another. The relation is explicitly non-linear: "I propose… to treat the relationship between state formation and strategic change as that of a field, as contrasted with those causal relations that are usually characterized along a line" (PDF p. 101 / printed p. 74); "More than one revolution has occurred because more than one constitutional order of the state has arisen" (PDF p. 100 / printed p. 73). What selects is legitimacy: "it is the constitutional order of the State that tends to confer military advantage by achieving cohesion, continuity, and, above all, legitimacy… And it is these strategic operations, through continuous innovation, that winnow out unsuccessful constitutional orders" (PDF p. 236 / printed p. 209). The first order he dates to Italy after 1494: "princes and oligarchs made a pact with an idea: the idea was that of the State, and its promise was to make the ruler secure" (PDF p. 107 / printed p. 80).

The second half is that the Long War of 1914–90 has ended the nation-state and is bringing forth the market-state, whose covenant is opportunity, not welfare: "the market-state exists to maximize the opportunities enjoyed by all members of society" (PDF p. 256 / printed p. 229). It "seeks a role as enabler and umpire, and shuns the role of provider and judge" (PDF p. 262 / printed p. 235), and lives with the paradox that "it will require more centralized authority for government, but all governments will be weaker" (PDF p. 261 / printed p. 234). It comes in three forms — entrepreneurial, mercantile, managerial, the last "using regional blocs as counterweights to national competition" (PDF p. 364 / printed p. 337). The security premise of the whole of Part III is in the Prologue: "For five centuries it has taken the resources of a state to destroy another state… This is no longer true, owing to advances in international telecommunications, rapid computation, and weapons of mass destruction" (PDF p. 19 / printed p. xxi).

## 2. What it CONFIRMS

**The mechanism's shape, at T3.** Manuscript, Introduction §I: the capital-intensification of decisive force "compelled the permanent taxation, the standing institutions and the administrative reach that we retrospectively call the state." Bobbitt: 1494 "set in train a series of events by which princes and oligarchs found it necessary to set up bureaucracies, first in order to raise money for mercenaries and fortress renovation" (PDF p. 120 / printed p. 93); and the fiscal threshold as selector — the princely state "withered and was everywhere superseded because it could not field forces to match the commitments of states that were larger, richer" (PDF p. 118 / printed p. 91). That is ch. 10 §VIII's "composition with a polity that can" pay, from a rival starting date.

**Wall-obsolescence as trigger, with primary witnesses.** Manuscript ch. 6 and ch. 10 §VII. Bobbitt supplies contemporaries the draft has not quoted: the Venetian senate, 1498, "the wars of the present time are influenced more by the force of bombards and artillery than by men at arms"; Guicciardini on 1494, "whenever the open country was lost, the State was lost with it"; Machiavelli, 1519, "[n]o wall exists, however thick, that artillery cannot destroy in a few days" (all PDF p. 107 / printed p. 80; Guicciardini via Mallett, *PBA* 67 (1981), at PDF p. 860 / printed p. 836 n. 6).

**The New Monarchies as one class.** Manuscript ch. 11 §I. Bobbitt, via Watson: the Italians "had power without legitimacy. The western kings had legitimacy without much effective power"; the rulers who learned the lesson turned "the legitimate but shadowy medieval overlordship of their realms into a [princely state] on the grand scale" — Henry Tudor, Louis XI, Ferdinand named (PDF p. 116 / printed p. 89). Ch. 7's checklist — "greatly expanded the permanent bureaucracies… introducing and maintaining standing armies, and they had centralized taxation specifically directed toward the ability to finance war… commenced to codify their civil and criminal laws" (PDF p. 123 / printed p. 96) — is three of the five moves.

**The Sforza thread.** Manuscript ch. 4 §III: functional and formal sovereignty are separable. Bobbitt's note: "Such was the rise of Francesco Sforza, a condottiere who became Duke of Milan by exploiting the state apparatus that the Visconti had developed" (PDF p. 860 / printed p. 836 n. 11); the body text gives the general form — the state, "once severed from the prince who brought it into being," could "provide others with the means of exercising the power they had seized, and legitimate their doing so" (PDF p. 109 / printed p. 82).

**The state does not wither.** Manuscript ch. 1 §VI, Coda §VI. Bobbitt: those who write that the nation-state is finished "are committed to maintaining that the State is withering away, a highly implausible view in my judgment" (PDF p. 28 / printed p. xxx); "History discloses no examples of states that have given up the constitutional authority to deploy domestic or international violence without ceasing to be states" (PDF p. 233 / printed p. 206).

**Per-bloc variation** (spine §8(b)): "Different cultures will adapt the market-state in distinct ways… a state-inflected market, and long-term social stability" (PDF p. 269 / printed p. 242).

## 3. What it CORRECTS

No manuscript date, figure or name is wrong by Bobbitt's account; on the medieval side he is a T3 synthesis (Keegan, Watson, Kennedy, Rogers) and the manuscript is better sourced at every joint. Three divergences of sequence, each correcting the other way:

- Manuscript ch. 10 §§I–III dates the settlement to France 1439–46, ratchet 1449–53. Bobbitt, PDF pp. 106–7 / printed pp. 79–80, dates the origin to Constantinople 1453 and 1494: "The French king, Charles VIII, however, financed the development of a cannon so light that it could be easily transported." His own note concedes the draft's chronology: "His predecessor, Charles VII, had used bombards to great effect earlier in the century. Harfleur… fell to Charles in only seventeen days after an attack by sixteen bombards" (PDF p. 860 / printed p. 836 n. 5). The Bureau brothers do not appear. Bobbitt's catalyst is the export of an instrument built two generations earlier, and he knows it.
- Manuscript ch. 10 §V: Louis XI operates a machine assembled in 1445. Bobbitt: Louis "established a princely state on the Italian model" (PDF p. 116 / printed p. 89), cited only to Watson p. 146 (n. 24). Direction of imitation reversed, on an IR synthesis.
- Manuscript ch. 4 §III: "Once — exactly once" did a contractor take a duchy. Bobbitt: "The condottieri themselves soon saw the advantage in turning their force on the authorities by whom they had been hired and supplanting them" (PDF p. 109 / printed p. 82) — generalised from a note citing Sforza alone.

Flag, do not resolve: the draft's Giovio "more than thirty-six pieces" (ch. 10 §IV) against Bobbitt's "siege train of at least forty artillery pieces" (PDF p. 107 / printed p. 80, from Keegan).

## 4. What it ADDS

**Ch. 4 §II (the buyers' fiscal capacity).** "Florence had an annual income greater than that of the king of England and the revenue of Venice and its Terra Ferma at the middle of the fifteenth century was 60 percent higher than that of France, more than double that of England and Spain" (PDF p. 110 / printed p. 83, citing Braudel, *Perspective of the World*, p. 720). The number behind "fiscally capable powers"; retrieve Braudel and cite direct.

**Ch. 4 §V and ch. 11 §VII (operative but never invested).** Bobbitt anticipated the distinction in a 2002 footnote: privatisation is "misleadingly" so called "because the government retains the power of determining the use of such forces, as opposed to a true privatization, in which shareholders replace the government in the directing role" (PDF p. 331 / printed p. 304 n.). Body text: "Privately owned satellites are leased out for military and intelligence functions"; "Those market-states whose economies are technologically sophisticated will be able to quickly convert that sophistication into military power" (same page). And for the chapter's close: "A market-state is not a market. There is an irreduceability of governing that cannot be assimilated into market operations" (PDF p. 364 / printed p. 337).

**Ch. 12 §V (the patron's layer).** His seventh proposal forecasts the March 2025 week from the patron's side: "In the future, the use of local proxy armies can offer a similar efficiency. Backed by the information and intelligence collection, the air power and the strategic direction of United States-led coalitions… the weapons and information they are provided must be carefully calibrated" (PDF p. 358 / printed p. 331).

**Ch. 12 §IV (the switch's prehistory).** The critical-infrastructure section is the fullest 2002 statement of the condition the draft says the state must reverse: "most of the critical infrastructure for the most developed state is in the hands of the private sector, which thus controls the information on which any attempts to ensure security depend" (PDF pp. 811–12 / printed pp. 787–88); "If it had been proposed in 1945 that the U.S. Bell System should be dismantled, objections on grounds of national security and law enforcement would almost certainly have trumped efficiency concerns" (PDF p. 812 / printed p. 788). A dated contemporary witness for the AT&T case in ch12/american-pattern.md and for the lag argument of spine §8(c)(i).

**Ch. 1 §III and Coda §VI (the tense).** The three paradoxes (PDF p. 261 / printed p. 234) — stronger centre, weaker governments; more participation counting for less; a retrenched welfare state promoting "infrastructure security… as never before" — describe the dispersal phase by a writer who took it for a destination. Bobbitt is the second founder who named the condition and mistook its tense.

**Ch. 10 §VIII bridge.** Rogers, as quoted: "The military expenditures of the Spanish monarchy… increased roughly twentyfold between 1500 and 1650, a 300% increase even after adjusting for inflation" (PDF p. 119 / printed p. 92); and Siena, fortified 1553, bankrupted by the works, "no funds left to raise a relief army," surrendered 1555 (same page).

**Introduction §IV.** Ch. 5's staging of Roberts, Parker, Black and Parrott (PDF pp. 97–101 / printed pp. 70–74), with Parrott's Wallenstein datum — a self-financing army of 50,000 feasible where 20,000 was not (PDF p. 98 / printed p. 71). Parrott's contractor-state objection is not in the corpus; enter it here, answer from Contamine.

## 5. What it CONTRADICTS

**(i) The price of decisive force has fallen.** "For five centuries it has taken the resources of a state to destroy another state… This is no longer true" (PDF p. 19 / printed p. xxi); weapons of mass destruction "so deadly relative to their size and cost that they can bypass even the most sophisticated attempts at defense by attrition… can be deployed clandestinely, so that the possibility of retaliation can be defied" (PDF p. 42 / printed p. 13); and the Epilogue: national security's three certainties — "national (not international)… public (not private)… victory (and not stalemate) — these three lessons of the past are all about to be turned upside down" (PDF p. 840 / printed p. 816). This reaches the DEFEND-tier claim (spine §6).

**(ii) The successor state sheds the switch.** "A state that privatizes most of its functions by law will inevitably defend itself by employing its own people as mercenaries" (PDF p. 25 / printed p. xxvii); on infrastructure: "internationalizing national security is only a little more distasteful than privatizing it. But there are really very few practical alternatives"; "the U.S. Centers for Disease Control (CDC) and Prevention, not the Pentagon, is the model"; "regulations will always come too late" (PDF p. 817 / printed p. 793). The exact negation of spine §8(c).

**(iii) Blocs are a category error.** "It is a failure of imagination… to assume that the only thing that will replace the nation-state is another structure with nation-state-like characteristics, only larger. It is in some ways rather pathetic that the visionaries in Brussels can imagine nothing more forward-looking than equipping the E.U. with the trappings of the nation-state" (PDF p. 261 / printed p. 234). His bloc is a trading counterweight, not a consolidator. Cuts at ch. 12 §IV's EU casting and the "inside blocs" frame.

**(iv) Nuclear use is likelier.** "the new era that we are entering makes their use by a great power more likely than in the last half century" (PDF p. 22 / printed p. xxiv) — against ch. 12 §V's overhang paragraph.

**(v) Direction is undecidable, and he says so.** "I have not argued, and do not wish to argue, that the State has changed in the precise ways it has because of strategic challenges to itself… I claim only that a coherent and helpful account can be given" (PDF p. 261 / printed p. 234). Spine §8(a) claims a ratchet; Bobbitt's field is a standing rebuke to any.

**The book's best answer.** On (i): his sentence is about resources to *destroy*; the manuscript's currency is force that takes and holds (ch. 12 §V). No non-state actor has fielded a weapon of mass destruction in twenty-four years; the disguised-attacker problem is orthogonal to consolidation; and his corollary that "the very massiveness of our conventional forces makes it unlikely we will be challenged openly" (PDF p. 22 / printed p. xxiv) did not survive February 2022. Good — but ch. 12 must say that "decisive" means decisive against a peer for territory, and place Bobbitt's vulnerability layer outside the mechanism beside the overhang. On (ii): the record has moved against him — CHIPS, the Intel stake, the DMA, Starshield — and "regulations will always come too late" is now scoreable in Appendix C; his paradox of a stronger centre and weaker governments is the dispersal described by a witness who took it for the terminus. Good. On (iii): weaker. His objection is that a bloc reproduces the nation-state at scale; the draft's reply is that the stack's price now requires the scale, and that the EU is conditional because it lacks the single fisc — which is Bobbitt's point from the other side. The Depreter test already concedes half of (iii); admit it. On (iv): the Russia paragraph answers the great-power claim but not the disguised-attacker one; the Coda's confessed limit should name him. On (v): fight it in the open. Bobbitt needs the field to hold six transitions; the manuscript claims one mechanism at one transition, with account books, and can afford a direction he cannot.

## 7. Verdict

The book changes neither direction nor register, only weight: it is the most serious grand-theory rival the project has not engaged, and it supplies from its own pages the Italian witnesses, the Sforza note, the privatisation footnote and the critical-infrastructure section the argument needs. The single most important thing to know is that Bobbitt's market-state is the neomedieval dispersal wearing a constitution — he described the condition the manuscript calls transitional and declared it a terminus, and his 2002 prescription for the switch ("the CDC, not the Pentagon"; "regulations will always come too late") is a scoreable prediction the record since 2022 has been moving against. The methodological quarrel — his field against the book's ratchet — must be fought by name in chs 1 and 10, because Bobbitt accepts the whole war-makes-state premise and still refuses the direction spine §8(a) asserts.
