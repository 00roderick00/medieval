## The five findings that matter

**1. A spine sentence does not survive.** Spine §8(a) says "No Estates
General met again until 1484." Major's appendix records an Estates
General at Tours in April 1468, entered without the query symbol he
gives every doubtful case: sixty-four towns, deputies "named in general
assemblies of the inhabitants", close to two hundred town deputies,
twenty-four archbishops, bishops and peers, and he expressly denies it
was managed (p. 55). Ch10 §III's "nearly forty years" and "no Estates
General met again until 1560" fall with it (1506 and 1558 are restricted
Estates General in the same appendix). **The substance survives
intact**: 1468 granted no tax, 1484's grant was breached within eight
months, and no province forced another convocation. Recorded, named as
Roderick's, not resolved. Major also destroyed the 1439 permanent-taille
tradition in 1960 and offers 1451 as the better hinge.

**2. The ratchet is substantially in Finer, and Tilly endorsed it.**
Finer's chapter 2 of the 1975 volume periodises by weapon, gives cannon
the decisive victories and the constitutional consequence, names the
bastion as the counter, states the extraction cycle and states the
French fiscal outcome; Tilly's chapter 1 praises him for showing "an
alternation of long pauses with giant steps closely following changes in
military technology". What is left to the book was graded honestly: the
**explanandum** differs and carries the answer (Finer's variable is the
military format, Bean's the size and number of states, the book's the
irreversibility of the fiscal settlement and the closure of the
sub-state path); Finer's **evidence** is weaker than the book's (he
transposes Formigny and Castillon and carries the taille tradition Major
had destroyed); the **ratchet's object** is the book's own. Ardant's
ceiling objection is **unanswered** and flagged. Bean's closing
paragraph already draws the line spine §8(g) draws, so ch10's Objection
6 is to be rebuilt as "How is this not Bean?".

**3. The coinage survives the ledger check.** Wolin has zero
occurrences of ornament, ornamental, façade, veneer, husk or decorative
across 375 pages — reported in full for honesty, he has "charade" and
"shell game" of one 2006 legislative episode, "hollowed out" of the
English monarchy, "ceremonial figurehead" of kingship, and one genuine
near-hit on democratic legitimation as "ceremonial and symbolic action".
His own phrases are "managed democracy", "dissociated democracy", "a
rhetorical democracy", "a bogus political democracy". And the object
differs: his is a regime type covering the whole polity, corporate and
cultural in mechanism, offered as "tentative, hypothetical"; the book's
is a measurable relation restricted to strategic commitments and the
decisive stack, with four published falsifiers, from a fiscal-military
mechanism. Five conditions are recorded for §VII, chief among them that
the term be introduced as a special case of post-democracy and that
Wolin's **dating objection** be conceded in a clause: he places the
separation in the Cold War defence establishment, sixty years before the
stack. That answer is graded partly good, partly a concession owed —
the honest claim is irreversibility, not arrival. His "predomination"
(p. 185) is a better name than "fusion" for §8(g)(3)'s indicator, and it
is his.

**4. Ruling (k) is qualified by its own new source.** Youings gives the
receipts — the £200 threshold decided by the *Valor*, £5,948 6s 8d in
exemption fines, Richardson's £899,120 over eight years, £37,754 11s
7¼d in warrant payments, sales at twenty years' purchase from December
1539, grants running 15, 35, 75, 112 across 1536–39, the confiscation
"just about doubled the Crown's resources" and made Augmentations "the
bank from which the king's government could be paid for". She also says,
flatly, that it "did not lead to the permanent augmentation of the annual
income of the Crown": over half alienated by Henry VIII's death, over
three-quarters by 1558. The crown took a **stock, not a flow**, and sold
it at a fixed price. Three readings of (k) are put to Roderick and none
adopted. This converges with Ertman from a second direction: his boards
reabsorbed by 1554, her revenue by 1558.

**5. Leseur adjudicates Cadillac and overturns two attributions.** The
town was carried by storm after battery night and day under Jean Bureau;
the castle composed the next day, the garrison prisoners except the
captain Gaillardet, who surrendered to Gaston de Foix's will. Escouchy is
right on mode and date and **wrong on command** — Courteault says so
flatly: attributing the siege to Dunois "c'est une erreur". Hall's 27
September is Bénauges. And **Vale's sentence is the reverse of what
stands at his own pin**: "Leseur, ir, 19" is tome II p. 19, where the
Castillon aftermath stands; on Gaston's arrival the place did not
surrender, and the captain was beheaded by Gaston as his perjured
liegeman, not by the king. No date appears anywhere in Leseur's Cadillac
narrative. The flag is narrowed, not closed, and one new caution is
raised honestly: Courteault's own notes disagree on which Cadillac.

## The provenance audit

The Perroy intake found that ch10/sources.md had carried since July a
Cazelles sentence — "une contribution de premier ordre à l'histoire de
l'État moderne" — that is **not in the review**. It came from the
short-quotation bank in research/acquisition-dossier.md, compiled at
that dossier's own grade without the review in hand. All eight of the
bank's quotations were therefore opened at the page or the page image.
**Six verified** (Watts p. 123; DeVries p. 127, whose printed text
hyphenates "techno-logical"; Rogers p. 242, where the fuller clause is
better than the fragment; Wilkinson p. 177; Curry p. 171; Stevenson
II.ii p. 619). **Two withdrawn** — Cazelles, and Keen's "a substantial
and important study", which is not in his review of Vale either; his
actual opening verdict is "an outstanding book, lucid, perspicacious,
and based on a sure footing of scholarly research". **Both failures are
reviewers' verdicts**, which is the pattern to remember: the compiler
appears to have paraphrased what a reviewer thought and set it in
quotation marks. Neither carried an argument, so nothing in the book
moves; but CLAUDE.md §4 is absolute, and the bank is not a source. The
consequence is proposed as rulings-sheet row P43-h.
