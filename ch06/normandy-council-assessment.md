## 1. Split campaign speed from siege speed

**What the draft carries.** The split exists evidentially but not as
a named device. §IV runs the same-wall compressions (Rouen three
weeks vs five months three weeks in 1418–19; Harfleur seventeen days
under sixteen bombards vs 1415's five weeks); §V runs the
composition census ("the great majority fell without a siege");
draft ll. 599–600 states "the same-wall compressions where fighting
happened, the composition census where it did not." But the causal
attribution differs from the council's: the draft reads the cascade
as the train's *reputation* propagating through politics ("the
politics was the medium through which the ballistics propagated,"
ll. 541–43), where the council reads a politically-fiscally caused
collapse to which artillery set the clock.

**What would change.** (i) The two quantities stated as two
quantities — campaign speed and siege speed — rather than left
implicit in the architecture. (ii) The Opus-5 phase split (central
Normandy falling July–November 1449 with the guns nearly invisible;
bombardment doing demonstrable work only in the winter sieges and
the Cotentin endgame) is ABSENT from the draft and is the sharpest
challenge on the table: it bears directly on the probe's sequencing
claim that "the hard demonstrations (Rouen, Harfleur, Formigny)
precede the mass compositions of spring–summer 1450"
(artillery-reconquest.md, analytical head). The two claims are
compatible only if Phase One (July–November 1449) is conceded as
largely non-ballistic — which the draft partly concedes already
("much of what fell in 1449–50 fell as towns had always fallen,"
ll. 252–56) without drawing the consequence.

**Source status.** All the required evidence is in repo at page
level: Rogers 1993, Curry 2010, the Somerset list (T1, Stevenson
*Letters and Papers* II.ii), the probe's own fortress table.
**The phase split is testable from the repo without any retrieval:
re-cut artillery-reconquest.md's table by date and mode** (falls
before ~December 1449 by mode vs falls after), which converts the
council's sharpest claim from assertion into a checkable count.

**Recommendation. ADOPT, in two steps:** run the date-cut on the
probe table first; if it confirms the phase structure, the split
enters the memo as a Revisions entry with the table as evidence and
the reputational-surrender mechanism restated per phase (Phase One:
political-fiscal collapse, guns as background threat; Phase Two:
ballistic compression where fighting happened). If the date-cut does
not confirm it, the finding is recorded against the council and the
draft stands. Either way the chapter gains; no gated retrieval is
required.

## 2. The *délai de secours* as the named central device

**What the draft carries.** One full instance, correctly textured:
Honfleur — "delivered on 18 February 1450 unless the English
relieved it by battle in the open field before that date; hostages
were given for performance… the garrison was no longer selling
months, it was buying weeks" (ll. 360–65); enforcement via Talbot's
hostageship ("These surrenders were not battlefield improvisations.
They were contracts, enforced," ll. 371–72); Falaise conditioned on
Talbot's release. The term "délai de secours" appears nowhere; the
device is not generalised; and the draft records Formigny destroying
"the last English field army" (ll. 646–47) without connecting that
fact to relief clauses expiring en masse.

**What would change.** The composition-with-relief-clause becomes
the chapter's named tempo mechanism: the guns priced the threat, the
clause encoded the price, Formigny deleted the relief, and the
remaining clocks ran out together. This is the council's single most
structural upgrade and it fits the draft's existing legal-order
material (ll. 433–46, 839–49) rather than displacing it.

**Source status.** The Honfleur instrument and Talbot's hostageship
are already sourced in repo. The GENERALISATION — "nearly every
place fell by notarised *appointement* with a relief date" — is the
council's characterisation and is NOT yet verified at scale: it
requires a sweep of the Somerset list and the Stevenson volumes
(both in repo; no retrieval needed, but the sweep has not been run)
before the draft may say "sixty legal instruments." Curry p. 187
(in repo) supports the domino effect and
preparations-not-bombardment, not the notarial count.

**Recommendation. ADOPT the naming and the Formigny link;
QUARANTINE the count.** Memo Revisions entry naming the device on
the Honfleur exhibit, with the Formigny→expiry connection tagged
[BOOK'S ARGUMENT] unless and until the in-repo sweep documents
clause-dated capitulations at scale; the sweep itself is a
follow-on job against the Somerset list and Stevenson, no
retrieval required.

## 3. Somerset's dispatch, Worcester's *Boke of Noblesse*, Blondel's line

**What the draft carries.** Somerset's 1449 dispatch is quoted (ll.
584–89, via Curry 2010: 185–87) — deployed as English exhaustion
inside the third counter-argument, though the adjoining sentence
("the fiscal state that could afford the train faced a fiscal state
that could no longer afford walls, powder or relief," ll. 592–94)
already makes the council's inversion point in substance. Worcester
is wholly absent from draft, memo, sources.md and retrieval-master.
Blondel is present via a different sentence (the walls-and-minds
line, ll. 342–44, Stevenson 1863: 152); the "not walls but
soldiers" epigram is absent.

**What would change.** The English state's own post-mortem enters in
its two authentic documents: Somerset's dispatch re-read as a
procurement-deficit report, and Worcester as the losing side's staff
analysis independently reinventing the finance-first diagnosis.

**Source status — the strongest of the six.** Worcester is NOW IN
REPO (`corpus/retrieved/Worcester-Boke-of-Noblesse-Gutenberg.txt`,
Gutenberg #33953, Nichols 1860 pagination preserved as `{n}`
markers), with the load-bearing passages verified this session:
- Nichols p. 74 (file ll. 5000–04): the second great hurt —
  rebellion "thoroughe theire wanhope, havyng no trust of hastie
  socoure and relief of an armee to come in tyme covenable"; and
  the third — "famyn of vitaile and penurie of money, and lak of
  provision of artillerie and stuffe of ordenaunce" — Worcester
  naming money-penury AND an English artillery-provision deficit,
  converging with Somerset's dispatch from the English side.
- Nichols pp. 29–30 (file ll. 3297–3305): "in defaute of exercising
  of armes… thoroughe the said coloure of trewes, and for lak of
  good provisions bothe of artillery and ordenaunce."
- **CAUTION on the council's Latin rubric.** "Defectus pecuniae ad
  solvendum soldarios fuit causa una prodicionis ducatus
  Normanniae" (file ll. 3442–43, Nichols pp. 33–34) glosses
  Worcester's KING JOHN / 1203 exemplum, not 1449–50 directly;
  Worcester's own bridge ("a like mysfortune… fille unto us") does
  the contemporary work. The finance-first attribution should rest
  on the p. 74 passage, not the rubric.
- Blondel's epigram is verified findable in the in-repo
  `narrativesofexpu00stev.pdf` text layer ("Non enim moenia…, sed
  militum virtus bello instructa urbes et castra tutatur," *De
  Reductione* ~pp. 17–18) — **but its context is Brittany's
  truce-era demilitarisation, not Normandy's walls**; usable only
  with that context stated.

**Recommendation. ADOPT.** The cheapest, best-evidenced upgrade of
the six: both texts are in repo at page level today. Memo Revisions
entry pairing Somerset (procurement deficit) with Worcester p. 74
(money-penury plus artillery-provision deficit) as the English
finance-first post-mortem; the epigram admitted only with its
Breton context; the 1203-rubric caution carried. **Housekeeping
flag for Roderick:** the draft cites "Stevenson 1863" (~15 times)
but ch06/sources.md has NO entry for Stevenson's *Narratives of the
Expulsion* — only *Letters and Papers* and a stale "not yet
consulted" Berry pointer. The sources.md entry should be regularised
when the memo entry is made.

## 4. Castillon with Howard's dissent and Basin's temerity reading

**What the draft carries.** Castillon stands as the unqualified
clean case ("the major key, and it will bear the weight," l. 662;
memo §5: "Castillon is the clean case Formigny is not"). Basin's
impetuosity reading appears only as pre-refuted via Pollard ("the
blanket charge of impetuosity, Basin's and the older tradition's,
founders on a career of battles prudently refused," ll. 764–66);
the divine-vengeance half is absent. Michael Howard's dissent
("Castillon was in no sense a victory for field artillery") appears
nowhere in the repo — not in any ch06 file, not on any retrieval
list.

**What would change.** The chapter's cleanest exhibit is presented
as what it is — contested in both the contemporary record (Basin's
temerity-and-vengeance) and the modern literature (Howard) — and
argued through rather than assumed. Given the book's
sceptics-concede signature, staging the dissent and answering it is
in-register; ignoring it is the one posture the apparatus cannot
afford.

**Source status.** NOT retrievable from memory: Howard's sentence
must be pinned to the edition (*War in European History*, OUP 1976;
page unknown until the book is in hand) — added to retrieval-master
this session. Basin at first hand remains a wanted row
(retrieval-master 2026-08-04: Samaran ed. gated; open Quicherat
scans noted); in-repo Basin is fragments via Contamine's apparatus
and via Pollard, whose ~p. 5 verbatim ("thinking that his name
alone would cause the enemy to take flight") is already in memo
Revisions 14.

**Recommendation. ADOPT IN PRINCIPLE; EXECUTE AFTER RETRIEVAL.** The
dissent may not be staged from the council's summary (that would
cite Howard from memory — CLAUDE.md §4 forbids it). Until Howard's
page is pinned and Basin's passage is read in an edition, the memo
may carry only what is already sourced: Basin-via-Pollard (in repo)
plus a [UNVERIFIED — RETRIEVAL: Howard 1976] placeholder naming the
dependent claim.

## 5. The Gascony 1451–53 reversal as controlled experiment

**What the draft carries.** Fully — the strongest match of the six.
The 1451 loyalty test (Guyenne falling rapidly despite pro-English
sympathy; Fronsac by composition; Blaye in five days; the
pension-buying texture via Vale); the 1452 counter-experiment
("Talbot appeared before Bordeaux with no siege train at all, the
city rose… What politics alone could not do — as 1453 would
demonstrate — was hold walls against the train. The two Bordeaux
reversals, twenty months apart, are as close to a controlled
experiment as the fifteenth century offers," ll. 570–80); the
paired constitutional settlements at ll. 839–49.

**What would change.** Nothing structural. The council's framing
("guns produce tempo, not allegiance") is already the draft's.

**A finding AGAINST the council's embellishment.** The council
attributes the October 1452 rising to "a broken tax exemption."
Vale 1969 — T2, IN REPO, read this session — explicitly contests
the tax-breach story at its source: "There is no evidence, apart
from Thomas Basin's account, that Guyenne was subjected to taxation
which might constitute a breach of the treaty of 12 June 1451 by
the king" (TRHS 1969, print pp. 131–32); and on the preliminaries,
"Thomas Basin is hardly the most reliable of authorities" (p. 126),
with the Bordeaux-embassy tradition called "much embroidery on
Basin's theme." Vale documents MORE evidence of forcible taxation
under Talbot's own 1452–53 administration than under the French.
The draft's existing wording ("the city rose for its old
allegiance") is therefore SAFER than the council's. Record the
disagreement (Basin and the chronicle tradition vs Vale) per
CLAUDE.md §5; do not import the tax trigger.

**Recommendation. NO CHANGE to the draft's structure; do not adopt
the tax-exemption trigger.** If Roderick wants the fiscal-grievance
texture, it must be staged as the contested point it is, Vale's
dissent stated — a memo Revisions entry can carry the
historiographical note; nothing more.

## 6. Source-independence of the *Recouvrement* tradition

**What the draft carries.** Nothing. Berry Herald, Blondel, Chartier
and Escouchy are treated as separate corroborating witnesses (e.g.
the Rouen chronicle column lists all four side by side). Existing
cautions are individual (Somerset-list partisanship; Leseur's
exaggeration; Berry's white-cross prodigy flagged) — not the
interdependence point: that the *Recouvrement*, Chartier, Berry
Herald and parts of Escouchy constitute one victory tradition, so
the French artillery testimony counts for less than its volume.

**What would change.** A methodological discipline, not a draft
passage: wherever the draft stacks the French chroniclers as
independent corroboration of artillery's role, the stack must be
discounted to one tradition plus genuinely independent witnesses
(the Somerset list; the English chronicles; Blondel where he
diverges). This strengthens the book's apparatus (it is exactly the
kind of caution critiques.md exists to hold) and slightly weakens
the artillery-forward testimony — which the ratchet reading (spine
§8(a)) can absorb, since the book's mechanism no longer rides on
chronicler enthusiasm.

**Source status.** Michaud-Fréjaville is listed nowhere in the repo
and the attribution of the interdependence finding to her is the
council's — NOT verifiable from anything in repo. The claim needs
its actual scholarly carrier identified and retrieved (route to
verify: her *Recouvrement* scholarship, likely via Persée/
OpenEdition; entered on retrieval-master this session).
Contamine's "Un regard sur le Recouvrement de Normendie" is the
adjacent piece the council also names; route likewise to verify.

**Recommendation. ADOPT the caution now, AT THE APPARATUS LEVEL
ONLY** (a critiques.md/memo methodological note that the French
testimony is one tradition — this much is verifiable from the
texts' own filiation once stated by a retrieved authority); the
named attribution waits for the article. No draft change until
then.
