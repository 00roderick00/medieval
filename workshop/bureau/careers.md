# The Bureau Brothers — The Work Itself
## Campaign by campaign; the institution as their biography

*WORKSHOP FILE — outside the book (CLAUDE.md §9 firewall). Corpus
citations as in lives.md. The organising claim, which is the book's
own thesis applied to two men: their achievement is not a list of
sieges but the offices, arsenals, salaried gunners and budget lines
they built — the sieges were the demonstrations.*

---

## 1. The proving grounds: Meaux 1439, Pontoise 1441

Gaspard stands among the named captains at the siege of Meaux (C72
T1 fn 163) — the siege where the crown's army was 63% écorcheur-
staffed (T1 ~pp. 262–63) and where a fresh pay tariff had to be
improvised (Annexe VI): the brothers' laboratory was an army being
rebuilt around them. The account series says the real work had
begun earlier: Jean's artillery accounts from August 1439,
Gaspard's from January 1441, with Bessonneau "pratiquement
supplanté… dès 1437" (Annexe VIII; T1 ~p. 303 region). Pontoise
(1441) is the tradition's other proving siege [DIGEST/tertiary
level in the corpus — the campaign detail awaits the French
narrative sources; not developed here].

## 2. The blueprint: the 1442 programme

"Le document d'archive le plus complet qu'on ait sur l'artillerie
royale française au milieu du 15e siècle" (C72: 666): Jean,
"commis par Charles VII 'ou fait et gouvernement' de son
artillerie," plans the season — 6 bombards, 16 veuglaires, 20
serpentines, 40 couleuvrines plus ribaudequins (4,198 l.t.);
20,000 lb of powder (2,200 l.t.); against 3,100 l.t. for the whole
traditional arsenal. Read as biography: a financial officer
writing a procurement forecast — the administrative genre itself
is the innovation, and the 2.06× ratio is the thesis of the book
they will never read.

## 3. Normandy 1449–50, siege by siege

Rouen: the train present, the fall political (ch06's record).
**Harfleur:** the signature works — "seize grosses bombardes"
before the wall; the king in the trenches; **Jean governor of the
artillery AND the mines**, Chartier's character sketch ("fort
subtil et ingénieulx"); Gaspard beside him as master; the
composition sealed with Jean's own seal among the six (Chartier
ii. 178–79). Honfleur (18 Feb 1450): the conditional-surrender
instrument (Blondel at 155). **Caen:** "the artillery of Gaspard
and Jean Bureau" with the sapping bringing down wall-lengths and
the corner tower (probe row — both brothers named in the
tradition). Cherbourg (Aug 1450): the tide-battery anecdote
remains tertiary-carried and flagged. The census pattern around
them: the two documented breaches end in composition; the mode is
surrender-on-terms — the train's reputation doing the work its
barrels began (ch06, reviewed). The brothers' particular art, on
the record, is **integration**: guns, mines, approaches and
treaty-craft as one system — Chartier's praise of the
"approaches, fosses, entrenchments and mines" is of THEIR
trenches (ch06 memo §4).

## 4. Formigny's two culverins — whose were they?

The honest answer: not provably theirs. Blondel names the hand
that placed them as "Giraud, regiarum machinarum unus magistrorum"
(Stevenson 1863: 172) — a master, not THE masters; Contamine is
silent on any Bureau attribution (ch06 memo Revisions 13,
negative). The popular tradition's "Bureau guns at Formigny"
remains unproven; the institutional reading is better anyway: by
1450 the train had *masters* in the plural — a staff, not two
heroes.

## 5. Gascony 1451

Jean enters Bordeaux with Dunois and Coëtivy at the head of the
column (probe row, 30 June 1451; Escouchy i. 356–58 for the day
and the keys) — and takes the mayoralty [1451–52, then perpetual
from 1453; T4, lives.md]. Blaye's five-day battery and storm,
Fronsac's surrender without a shot (ch06's record): the campaign
that priced Gascon resistance.

## 6. Castillon 1453 — the masterpiece, with its caution

The strategy: "par l'advis… de maistre Jehan Burreau, chevalier,
seigneur de Monglat" — besiege Castillon to master the river; "A
ce faire se conclud tout le conseil" (Escouchy ii. 33).
**Single-source caution, stated:** the counsel-attribution is
Escouchy's alone among the corpus chronicles; Chartier's 1453
chapters and Berry do not carry it [and the manuscript carries it
with Escouchy named — ch06 §VI]. The park: ditch and palisade,
the river at the back, "a line of 250 guns of all calibres massed
wheel to wheel" (Pollard ch. 1, p. 1); guns trained on the
approach ("afusterrent leur artillerie au lez devers dont véoient
venir ledit Talbot," Escouchy ii. 39). The town surrendered two
days later under continuing fire; Cadillac's salvo-prepared storm
and Bordeaux's punitive composition closed the year (ch06's
record). Jean's mayoralty is the composition system personified:
**the gunner who reduced the province became its governor** — the
instrument of coercion converting, in one career, into the
instrument of administration.

## 7. After: Charles VII's last years, Louis XI's reign

Jean: prévôt des marchands of Paris 1450–52 [T4]; the 1462
Catalonia rearguard as grand treasurer (Leseur II. 121); d. 1463.
Gaspard: the office through the change of reign — his lieutenant
buying guns for Rouen in peacetime (Oct 1452, Vale); the
Montlhéry-era train he served carried named great guns
("L'ostesse"; "l'ung des douze pers" — C72 T1 fn via Perroy 188);
captaincies of royal castles 1463–65 [T4]; master until his death
in 1469 — and the permanent ordinaire wage-account series begins
c. 1469 (C64: 231, the 22nd account of 1491): **the payroll
series that outlived everyone starts the year the last Bureau
died.** The institution they built then ran without them: ~150
career gunners, twenty-year service records, two budget lines,
3.3% administrative overhead, quarterly pay "comme c'est la règle"
(C64: 226–32) — the biography's real last chapter is an account
book with neither brother's name in it.
