## 1. Origins and family

Sons of **Simon Bureau, bourgeois of Paris**, and his wife Hélène;
the family from **Semoine in Champagne**, translated into the Paris
merchant-official world — Jean the second son (b. c. 1390), Gaspard
the third [T4 — fr.wikipedia, both articles; the corpus attests the
Champagne origin at tertiary level only, ch06 memo §2]. Basin's
sneer gives the contemporary social read: "homo plebeyus et, ut
aiebant, aggerum et fossarum faciendorum artifex" — a plebeian, a
craftsman of embankments and ditches [TRANS. CLAUDE] (Basin, *Hist.
Ch. VII*, t. I, p. 212, via C72 T1 fn 94).

## 2. Jean's pre-artillery career — the delicate detail, flagged

The pointer record has Jean studying law in Paris, working as a tax
official, and serving as **commissaire au Châtelet under the
English occupation** (Bedford's Paris), before joining Charles VII
in **1434 as receveur ordinaire de Paris** [T4 — fr.wikipedia;
**FLAG: the English-service claim is the file's most delicious and
most checkable detail and rests, so far, on the pointer tier alone.
The corpus neither confirms nor contradicts it. Verify against
Gallia Regia / the DBF entry / Favier's Paris studies before ANY
narrative use — the man who expelled the English having begun as
their clerk is too good to carry unverified**]. The corpus's own
anchor for the transition: "Jean Bureau in royal service from 1434,
master of the artillery by 1439" (ch10 memo §3, tertiary), with the
documentary spine in the account series (below).

## 3. The offices — Jean

- **Master of the artillery:** accounts open **August 1439**
  (Annexe VIII, C72 T2); the pointer record gives royal letters of
  29 Sept 1439 [T4 — consistent with the series]. In 1442 "commis
  par Charles VII 'ou fait et gouvernement' de son artillerie" —
  the procurement programme in his name (C72: 666). At Harfleur
  1449, "gouverneur" of artillery **and mines**, "fort subtil et
  ingénieulx" (Chartier ii. 178); among the six sealers of the
  town's composition (Chartier ii. 179).
- **Trésorier de France from 1443** (ch06 memo, tertiary; the title
  in the chronicles: "trésorier de France" at Harfleur, Chartier
  ii. 178; "grant thresorier de France" still in 1462, Leseur t. II
  p. 121).
- **Prévôt des marchands de Paris, 17 Aug 1450 – 19 Aug 1452**
  [T4 — fr.wikipedia; NEW to the project's record: the head of
  Paris's merchant municipality between the Norman and Gascon
  campaigns — verify].
- **Mayor of Bordeaux:** 1451–52, then **maire perpétuel from
  1453** [T4 — fr.wikipedia; this two-stage reading RESOLVES, at
  pointer level, the probe's flagged conflict between the "Aug
  1451" and "post-1453" mayoralty traditions — both true, two
  grants; verify in the Bordeaux historiography, retrieval list].
- **Seigneuries:** Monglat/Montglas (the title Escouchy gives him
  at the Castillon council — "maistre Jehan Burreau, chevalier,
  seigneur de Monglat," ii. 33); La Houssaye-en-Brie (1450),
  Fontenay-en-France, Thieux, Noisy-le-Sec, Marle, la Malmaison
  [T4 list].
- **Ennoblement: October 1447, at Bourges** [T4 date] — the act
  itself in the corpus at T1 reference: "Jean et Gaspard Bureau
  furent reconnus nobles par Charles VII" (A.N. JJ 178, n° 256,
  via C72 T1 fn ~p. 405). Note the date: ennobled BEFORE the
  reconquest — the crown ennobled its financial-technical men on
  the eve of using them.

## 4. The offices — Gaspard

- **Master of the artillery:** accounts open **January 1441**
  (Annexe VIII, C72 T2) — the corpus's documentary correction of
  the circulating tradition. The pointer record repeats "27
  December 1444, succeeded Pierre Bessonneau" [T4 — fr.wikipedia;
  **CONTRADICTED by the account series: Bessonneau's last official
  function 20 Feb 1440, "dès 1437… pratiquement supplanté par Jean
  et Gaspard Bureau" (C72 T1 ~p. 303 region; Annexe VIII). The 27
  Dec 1444 date may be a formal regrant — hold both, prefer the
  accounts**]. "Maistre de l'artillerie du roy" at Harfleur
  (Chartier ii. 178); among the Meaux 1439 captains (C72 T1 fn
  163); **still master in 1469** — the year's register is "sous le
  maître de l'artillerie, Gaspard" (C72 T1 ~p. 297) — dying in
  office (d. 1469 [T4]).
- **His lieutenant:** Jean Aubry, buying artillery in Paris for
  Rouen, Oct 1452 (Vale 1969) — the office delegating procurement.
- **Seigneuries and honours:** purchased Villemomble from Jean de
  Bretagne, 21 March 1445, for 9,000 l.t.; Montfermeil,
  Nogent-sur-Marne; captain of Beauté, the Louvre and Poissy
  (1463–65); and — extraordinary if confirmable — **"marquis de
  Castillon," 1464** [T4 — fr.wikipedia; **FLAG HARD: a marquisate
  named for the battlefield would be the thesis performed as
  heraldry, and "marquis" is a rare style in 1460s France — verify
  in DBF/Gallia Regia before any use**].
- Ennobled with Jean (JJ 178 n° 256; Oct 1447 [T4 date]).

## 5. Money

The masters' pay was modest and the office compensated in trade:
the older pattern — "fabricant et garde-magasin," contracting with
artilleurs and founders "d'où sans doute d'assez gros profits"
(C72 T1 ~p. 29x) — and in land: Villemomble at 9,000 l.t. cash
(above) is the measure of what the office's operators could
accumulate. A company captain's supplement — one livre per lance
per month — is cited for Bureau's gages (Perroy p. 177 via C64 fn).
The budget lines they administered dwarf what they drew: 1442's
6,398 l.t. programme; the ordinaire at 24,236 l.t. (1491 series
they founded, 22nd account = series from c. 1469, i.e. under
Gaspard); the extraordinaire at 111,733 l.t. (1489) (C72: 666; C64:
231–32).

## 6. Marriages, children, afterlives

**Jean** m. **Germaine Hesselin, 24 May 1428**, lady-in-waiting to
Queen Marie d'Anjou, of a Paris official family; five children —
Jean the Eldest, Pierre, Simon, Philippa, and **Isabelle, married
in 1463 to Geoffroy, son of Jacques Cœur** [T4 — all fr.wikipedia;
the Bureau–Cœur alliance, if confirmed, joins the two greatest
new-men houses of the reign at the altar — verify]. **Gaspard** m.
(1) Guarix Burelle (c. 1440), (2) Richarde de Vérines (c. 1450);
three daughters — Jeanne, Marguerite, Gérarde/Gasparde [T4]. The
male Bureau line's fate after the 1460s: NOT ESTABLISHED in the
gathered record — a documentary gap.

## 7. Deaths and burials

**Jean: d. 5 July 1463, Paris; buried at Saint-Jacques-de-la-
Boucherie** [T4 — fr.wikipedia] — the great church of the Paris
merchant quarter: the gun-master buried among the bourgeoisie he
came from, not in a soldier's chantry [the reading is mine;
the burial site is the T4 record]. His last attested field service
in the corpus: the rearguard of Gaston IV's 1462 Catalonia army,
"messire Jehan Bureau, grant thresorier de France" (Leseur t. II,
p. 121). **Gaspard: d. 1469** [T4], in office (the 1469 register,
C72 T1 ~p. 297); burial unrecorded in the gathered material.
