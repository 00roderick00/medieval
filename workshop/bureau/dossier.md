## 1. The world they came from

Paris under the double monarchy: a bourgeois household out of
Champagne, a father in trade, a second son put to the law and the
receipts [T4/tertiary skeleton, lives.md §§1–2]. If the Châtelet
detail confirms, Jean's first offices were ENGLISH offices — a
commissaire in Bedford's Paris, learning exactly the
assessment-and-audit craft he would later turn against his first
employers [T4 — VERIFY; the irony is not usable until it is]. The
accounting world made the man: the 1442 programme (C72: 666) is a
clerk's document — line items, unit costs, a season's forecast —
and the hand that drafted it later sealed a town's surrender
beside marshals (Chartier ii. 179). Picture the younger Jean at a
Châtelet counting-board, tallies on strings, the parchment smell,
ink on the fingers of a man who would die a lord [COLOUR —
UNSOURCED as staged; the offices are the T4 record].

## 2. What a master of the artillery's day held

The office as the sources give it: contracts with founders and
artilleurs — the master a "fabricant et garde-magasin" with "assez
gros profits" in the trade (C72 T1 ~p. 29x); the saltpetre-men of
Paris answerable at his auditoire (C64: 232 n. 40 — the practice
under his successors, the jurisdiction the office accreted); the
powder keeper, the wagon captain, the keeper of the small engines
reporting in (C64); the founders at their bell-pits (T1 ~p. 297);
the account-book — HIS genre — with its quarterly rule (C64: 232).
A working day, staged: dawn with the clerks over the receipts; the
forenoon at the founders' yard hearing a casting dispute; noon
with the saltpetre farmers' prices; the afternoon in the saddle to
inspect a magazine; candle-light over the muster-roll of gunners
whose names he knows because there are only eight score of them
(the ~150, C64: 226–27) [COLOUR — UNSOURCED as a composed day;
every component office and relation is sourced above].

## 3. Jean at the rampart, Castillon, aged about sixty-three

Not fifty-something: born c. 1390, he stood at the park aged
about sixty-three [lives.md §1 — correcting the tasking's guess].
The scene the sources license: the park he had counselled into
existence (Escouchy ii. 33), the guns laid on the approach road
(ii. 39), and the financial official at the parapet as the
banners came on crying "Talbot! Talbot!" — the two careers of the
century facing each other across a ditch: the man of the lance,
sixty-six, unarmoured on a white palfrey [Pollard ch. 1, p. 1,
hedged]; the man of the ledger, sixty-three, behind 250 guns he
had budgeted. What Jean did with his hands that afternoon —
whether he touched a linstock, whether he stood or sat, what he
said when the horse went down — no source records [stated as the
gap it is]; give him stillness and arithmetic, a man watching a
column close range brackets he already knows [COLOUR — UNSOURCED].

## 4. The brothers and the old captains

The evidence of relations is thin and runs both ways. Deference
TO them: Chartier's unprompted praise ("fort subtil et
ingénieulx," ii. 178); the council concluding on Jean's advice
with marshals present (Escouchy ii. 33); the king in THEIR
trenches at Harfleur (Chartier ii. 178). Friction ABOUT them:
Basin's class sneer, written by a bishop who chose the detail —
ditches and embankments — that made the insult stick (via C72 T1
fn 94). Between the two poles, the 1445 world that made them
possible: a captain-list scrubbed of bastards and commoners
("aucun bâtard, un seul roturier," T1 ~p. 387) into which the
crown had ALREADY ennobled its two indispensable commoners (Oct
1447 [T4 date]; JJ 178 n° 256) — the nobility filter and the
Bureau exemption are the same policy seen from both sides. Any
staged scene of a marshal's condescension at a siege-table is
[COLOUR — UNSOURCED].

## 5. The ennoblement — the thesis as ceremony

The act exists (JJ 178 n° 256, C72 T1 fn ~p. 405); the date and
place rest at pointer tier (October 1447, Bourges [T4 — VERIFY]);
the ceremony itself is nowhere described. If it is ever staged:
two Paris accountants kneeling in a Bourges hall to rise as
gentlemen, three years before their guns opened Normandy — the
gun-clerk made a lord BEFORE the victories, credit extended by a
crown that knew what it had [the sequence is sourced; the hall,
the kneeling, the wording of the moment — COLOUR — UNSOURCED].
The afterlives complete the figure: Jean's daughter to Jacques
Cœur's son (1463 [T4]) — new money marrying new money at the top
of the new state; Gaspard buying Villemomble for 9,000 l.t.
cash [T4]; and — if it survives verification — a Bureau made
**marquis de Castillon** (1464 [T4 — VERIFY HARD]): the
battlefield itself minted into a title. Jean's grave closes the
arc where it opened: Saint-Jacques-de-la-Boucherie, the
merchants' church [T4] — buried a lord among the ledgers'
people.
